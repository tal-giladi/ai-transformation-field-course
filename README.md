# AI Transformation in the Field

Walk into ten different organizations and run the whole AI transformation yourself — discovery, portfolio, guardrails, a tested pilot, adoption and the steering-committee decision.

## Who this course is for

You know the ideas: AI strategy, value, governance, change. Maybe you finished IBM's "Lead AI Transformation" certificate, or you have read the same material. What you lack is the doing. You are a new head of AI, a consultant, or the manager who was told "you own AI now", and on Monday you have to walk into an organization and start.

This course is the doing. You will interview the people, read the data room, measure the baseline, write the policy, build and test the pilot, train the team, measure adoption honestly and stand in front of the steering committee with a recommendation you can defend. Then you will do it again, in a different organization where the same move fails for a different reason.

## How it works

**Stage 1 — The field kit (modules 1–7).** The method, step by step, practised on Harbor & Pine, a 120-person furniture importer in Rishon LeZion. Every lesson produces a real document you keep: an engagement charter, a shadow-AI census, a findings memo, a scored portfolio, an ROI model, a policy pack, a working pilot with an eval report, a training session, an adoption dashboard, a steering pack and a handover.

**Stage 2 — Ten engagements (modules 8–17).** Ten organizations, each with its own data room, its own people and a hidden truth: an online retailer that wants a chatbot, an insurer, a clinic network choosing an AI scribe, a municipality, a law firm, a factory whose data is not ready, a software company shipping an agent, a bank with 40 stalled pilots, a nonprofit with no budget, and a program you must rescue in 30 days. You run each one from the first call to the steering-committee decision, then see how it played out.

**Stage 3 — Capstone (module 18).** An organization you have not seen, with no hints, a full deliverable set and a recorded executive presentation. Optionally, a real engagement in your own workplace.

## What you do in every organization

- **Read the data room**: org charts, policies, contracts, KPI reports and real-shaped exports (tickets, claims, time sheets, logs). The data is synthetic and reproducible, and it has findings planted in it, the way real data does.
- **Interview the stakeholders** by roleplay: paste a persona into any LLM chat and talk to the sponsor, the CFO, the sceptic, the frontline employee or the vendor. Each one knows things they will only tell you if you ask the right way. A coach prompt gives you feedback; a pre-run transcript is there if you prefer to read.
- **Build and test**, with no code (spreadsheet + LLM chat) or with Python. Both tracks produce the same artifacts: a pilot, a test set, an eval report with numbers.
- **Decide and defend**: the readout, the steering committee, and the debrief with two or three futures and the expert's deliverables to compare with yours.

## What you need

- A computer, a spreadsheet tool and access to at least one LLM chat (a free tier works; file upload helps).
- Optional Python track: Python 3.12 or newer and an API key for one LLM provider (about US$10 for the whole course), or a free local model.
- About 37 hours of reading and 250 hours of hands-on work in the lessons, plus a project at the end of every module. Every lesson says how long its hands-on part takes.

## The context: Israel first

All organizations in the course are Israeli, and the law and regulators come first from Israel: the Privacy Protection Law and its Amendment 13, the data security regulations, the Privacy Protection Authority's guidance, sector regulators and labour law. EU rules (GDPR, the AI Act) and international standards (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001) come in where they reach the organization. The course is in English. It is not legal advice: the lessons show you what to check and when to bring in counsel or the DPO.

## Interactive simulations

Some lessons link to small simulations (pilot economics, human review under load, adoption over 26 weeks, the steering meeting, an incident hour by hour, agent permissions). They are educational models: simplified on purpose, so you can see how a decision plays out.
