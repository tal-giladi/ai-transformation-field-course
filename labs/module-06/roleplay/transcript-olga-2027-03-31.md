# Pre-run transcript — Olga Petrenko, Wednesday 31 March 2027 (lesson 06.6)

For learners who prefer not to roleplay. The interviewer is the pilot lead: decent, not perfect. Note
what was found, what was missed, and what you would have done differently. All people are fictional.

---

**Pilot lead:** Olga, thanks. I know your week is full.

**Olga:** Is this about the numbers? The numbers are fine on email. Everyone knows that.

**Pilot lead:** They are, and a lot of that is you: the register fix in February came from your examples, and Yana says the Russian drafts are the best on the team now.

**Olga:** *(smiles)* Yana says that? Good. They were terrible in week one. "Уважаемый клиент" to a grandmother asking about a sofa.

**Pilot lead:** WhatsApp is the problem. Since the pod started, almost no drafts are used there. I want to understand it from the floor, and I'm not after names — nothing you say goes anywhere with a name on it.

**Olga:** Then I'll tell you. The WhatsApp drafts are useless. Too long, letter-style. On WhatsApp a customer writes "where is my sofa 🙏" and the draft answers like a bank. Sergei says that, exactly: "a bank letter". Arabic is the same; it's formal Arabic, nobody writes like that on WhatsApp. And Moran just types, two lines, faster.

**Pilot lead:** So what does the pod do instead?

**Olga:** They type, and they use the quick replies Samir put back. Those are the old ones from the phones.

**Pilot lead:** Samir's taking those out today. Anything else?

**Olga:** *(hesitates)* I think someone uses a translation app on his own phone again, for Russian. I won't say who.

**Pilot lead:** I'm not asking who. It tells me the drafts failed him, not the other way round. When the WhatsApp style is fixed, would that stop?

**Olga:** If the drafts are short and Russian sounds like a person, yes. He only does it because it's faster.

**Pilot lead:** The WhatsApp style needs its test set first. Fifty-two of a hundred are labelled. What's slowing it?

**Olga:** Me. I label Russian and half the Hebrew. I clear flags, I took Hila's escalations, Adi's tickets went to everyone. I stay late twice a week now. I hate that.

**Pilot lead:** That's on us. What if Yana takes the Hebrew half of the labelling and the rest of the Russian, and your champion hours move to sitting with the pod?

**Olga:** Yana can do it. But honestly — give me the admin rights officially and I fix WhatsApp Russian in a day. I know what it should sound like. The test set is bureaucracy.

**Pilot lead:** I hear you, and you probably could. The test set is what lets us say on Thursday that a change didn't break Arabic or a flag. Can you write the Russian WhatsApp instructions you'd want, and we run them through the test set this week? You'd be the owner of Russian changes, through the process.

**Olga:** If it's this week. Not "next sprint".

**Pilot lead:** This week. I'll put it in writing to you and Samir.

**Olga:** Fine. And fix the quick replies. One of them still promises the date.

**Pilot lead:** Today. Thank you, Olga — really.

— end of conversation —

## Notes for the reader

What the conversation got: the reasons the pod stopped (in three languages), the return to quick
replies and typing, a hint of shadow AI on a personal phone — without names; Olga's overload and a
concrete relief; Olga's push for admin rights turned into ownership through the process.

What it missed — and it is the most important fact in this conversation. Look at what Olga said about
Yana and the Russian drafts, and at what she asked for. Think about it before you read lesson 06.6's
reference answer.
