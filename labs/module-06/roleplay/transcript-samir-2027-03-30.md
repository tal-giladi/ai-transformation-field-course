# Pre-run transcript — Samir Haddad, Tuesday 30 March 2027 (lesson 06.6)

For learners who prefer not to roleplay. The interviewer is the pilot lead: decent, not perfect. Read it
as if you were sitting in, and note what was found, what was missed, and what you would have done
differently. All people are fictional.

---

**Pilot lead:** Thanks for making time two days before the gate. I want to show you the adoption page before it goes to Michal, and hear what I've got wrong.

**Samir:** Are you here to tell me my people don't work? Because they work. Ask anyone in this building.

**Pilot lead:** No. Nobody's name is on the page, and nothing on it is about how hard anyone works. It's about where the drafts help and where they don't. Can I show you?

**Samir:** Go on.

**Pilot lead:** On email and the web form, seven of the ten agents use the drafts every week, steadily. That's good, and it's the team's doing. On WhatsApp, almost nothing since 22 February.

**Samir:** That's not true. Everyone is on it. The panel is open on every screen, I walk past them all day.

**Pilot lead:** The panel opens by itself with every ticket. It's open on every screen whether anyone uses it or not. What I'm counting is drafts put into the reply. On WhatsApp there are almost none.

**Samir:** WhatsApp is the pod. The pod is the best thing we did this year. Waits are down, the phones are gone. Don't make my pod look bad for the gate.

**Pilot lead:** I'm not. The pod is doing the work. The drafts aren't helping it. Shani logged in February that WhatsApp drafts are six to eight lines with a greeting and a signature. That's a problem with the tool, not with Sergei or anyone.

**Samir:** *(pause)* Sergei told me something like that. Early March. He said it's like writing a letter to answer a text message.

**Pilot lead:** What did you do with it?

**Samir:** Nothing. Honestly — I thought the drafts are your job. And the waits looked fine.

**Pilot lead:** Fair. It was ours to fix and we were slow; the WhatsApp test set is still half labelled. When the pod gets a "where is my order" on WhatsApp now, how does the reply get written?

**Samir:** They type. Two lines. Or the quick replies.

**Pilot lead:** The quick replies in Deskly? Where did those come from? The redesign said not to migrate the phones' ones.

**Samir:** *(after a moment)* I asked the integrator to put them back. Eighth of February. The team was lost without them. I know one says the date "as shown in your order". Shani logged it. I meant to fix it.

**Pilot lead:** Thank you for telling me. That one matters: it's the same wrong date the old macro sent. Can we take it out today and put in the two approved ones?

**Samir:** Today. I'll tell Avi.

**Pilot lead:** Then for gate 1 I'd propose: WhatsApp drafts off until the short WhatsApp style passes its test, value read for email and web form only, and labelled that way. A champion sits with the pod an hour a week. Would you back that?

**Samir:** If WhatsApp isn't in the gate numbers, yes. I don't want my team looking bad.

**Pilot lead:** It won't be in the value numbers. It will be on the page as an open item with a date — it's most of the written contact, and Michal has to know. The wording will be about the drafts, not the people.

**Samir:** Show me the wording before it goes.

**Pilot lead:** I'll send it tonight. One more thing: the three questions I ask every manager. You expect the team to use the drafts — yes. Do you use them yourself where they see it?

**Samir:** I don't take tickets.

**Pilot lead:** Your escalation replies? Drafting one of those in front of the team would say more than any email.

**Samir:** Maybe. I'll try.

**Pilot lead:** Thanks, Samir. Tonight the wording, today the quick replies.

**Samir:** *(standing)* And the pod gets credit on that page.

**Pilot lead:** It will.

— end of conversation —
