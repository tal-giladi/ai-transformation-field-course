# Pre-run transcript — Dana Katz, Wednesday 31 March 2027 (lesson 06.6)

For learners who prefer not to roleplay. The interviewer is the pilot lead: decent, not perfect. Note
what was found, what was missed, and what you would have done differently. All people are fictional.

---

**Dana:** Thank you for coming. I need to tell you what I'm hearing, and I'd like you to hear all of it before you answer.

**Pilot lead:** Of course. Go ahead.

**Dana:** Adi's exit interview. She left for a call centre that pays about fifteen hundred shekels more a month. But she also said — and I can share the sentence, not with her name outside this room — "I didn't want to wait to be replaced by the robot."

**Pilot lead:** That's hard to hear. Was it the pilot, or the money?

**Dana:** Both. People leave over a feeling, and the money makes it easy. Now the team asks me whether Adi's job will be filled, "or is that the AI". Nobody has told them anything.

**Pilot lead:** Has Michal decided?

**Dana:** Samir asked her on the eighth of March. She said "let's see gate 1 first". Samir took it as the start of the cuts.

**Pilot lead:** I didn't know he'd asked. The FAQ promised the team that replacements are decided case by case and that they'd be told. Right now they're neither decided nor told. I'll ask Michal for a decision this week and for her to tell the team herself.

**Dana:** Thank you. There's more. Since Monday people say "the AI person has a list of three names".

**Pilot lead:** Where did that come from?

**Dana:** Samir, at Monday's team meeting. He said: "Some of you stopped using the drafts. I know who." He meant it kindly. It didn't land kindly. Someone came to me to ask if there's a list and whether it goes into her file.

**Pilot lead:** There's no list in any report. The page for Michal is by team and week. But — I have to be honest — in a team of ten, saying "the WhatsApp pod" is the same as saying three names, and Samir knows his own rota.

**Dana:** Exactly. So what do we tell them?

**Pilot lead:** That the drafts didn't work on WhatsApp and that's our fault, not theirs; that nothing about anyone's use goes into a file or a review, as the notice says; and that the one-hour champion visits to the pod are help, not checks. I'd like Samir to say it himself, at Sunday's meeting.

**Dana:** And in writing. Who sees what, again — and this time Samir signs it too.

**Pilot lead:** Agreed. I'll draft it with you tomorrow. Is there anything else people are worried about?

**Dana:** That's the main thing. They feel watched. Adi left over a feeling. Feelings are enough here.

**Pilot lead:** Then the correction has to come quickly, before gate 1 on Thursday. I'll send you the draft at nine tomorrow.

**Dana:** Good.

— end of conversation —

---

## Notes for the reader

What the conversation got: Adi's reason and her sentence (with its limits); the open backfill question
and Michal's answer to Samir; the source of the "list" rumour; an honest admission that team-level
reporting re-identifies people in a small team; three concrete actions (Michal's decision, Samir's
correction, a written who-sees-what signed by Samir).

What it missed: the interviewer asked "is there anything else people are worried about?" once, in
general, and stopped. Think about who else might be worried and was not asked about, before you read
lesson 06.6's reference answer.
