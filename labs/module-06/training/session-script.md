# Session script — "Your drafts, your call" (60 minutes, role-based)

Harbor & Pine Customer Service · AI drafts pilot · lesson 06.2. Trainer: the pilot lead, with the
group's champion as co-trainer (Olga for Russian, Lina for Arabic, Shani for Hebrew). Groups of two to
four people, by language, so every exercise is in the trainees' own languages.

**Goal of the session (say it in one sentence at the start):** by the end you can open any written
contact, check the AI draft in under three minutes, decide used / edited / discarded, and raise the right
flag — on our real messages, in your languages.

**What the session is not:** a features tour, a talk about AI, or a test with consequences. Nobody's
results from the exit check go to their manager.

## Before the session

- [ ] A training copy of Deskly with the panel switched on, loaded with the eight exercise cards as
      tickets (the integrator set it up; no live customer data is used beyond last year's messages in the
      cards, which are already in Deskly).
- [ ] Printed exercise cards for the group's languages, the job aid in the group's language, the exit
      check sheet (`proficiency-check.csv`, one row per trainee).
- [ ] Cover on the queue confirmed with Samir (at most four people off the queue at once).
- [ ] The co-trainer has done the cards herself in the dry run (Sunday 7 February).

## Run sheet

| Minutes | Block | What the trainer does | What trainees do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Why and what changes | Three sentences: what the drafts are for (time on Russian, Arabic and long replies; no more wrong-date macro), what does not change (you send every reply; you may discard any draft), what is logged and how it is reported (by team and week, never in performance reviews; from the comms pack). Ask: "What worries you about this?" Write the answers on the board; answer the ones you can, park the rest for Samir with a date. | Say what worries them |
| 5–12 | Live demo on card 1 | Open card 1 in the training Deskly. Think aloud: read the customer, read the facts beside the draft, read the draft, the four checks, mark "used as is", send. Then card 2: show the blank ETA, mark "edited — wrong fact", show the logistics@ rule. | Watch; call out what they would check |
| 12–37 | Hands-on: the cards | Each trainee works the cards for their languages at their own screen (four cards in 25 minutes: two in their language, one Hebrew, card 8). The co-trainer walks round. The trainer does not answer "is this right?"; asks "what on the screen tells you?" | Decide the mark, the reason, the flag, and write what they would send |
| 37–47 | Debrief the cards | Go through each card the group did: what they marked and why. Use the answer key. Name the four checks every time one catches an error. Spend the most time on card 7 (flags) and card 3 or 6 (promises). | Explain their choices |
| 47–53 | Flags and the stop button | When to add a flag the panel missed; who clears it (Samir, Olga); what Samir can switch off (per channel and language) and how to tell him. "If you see something that worries you, you don't need permission to discard." | Ask questions |
| 53–57 | Job aid and help | Hand out the job aid. Champions, office hours (Tuesdays 13:00–13:45), the issues log. Session 2 (practice clinic) date and time. | Put the job aid by their screen |
| 57–60 | Exit check and two questions | Each trainee does one fresh card on their own (the trainer picks one they did not do); record on the exit sheet: four checks applied, correct mark, flag raised if needed. Two questions on a card: "How useful was this for your real work (1–5)?" and "What will you do differently tomorrow?" | Do the card; answer the two questions |

## Session 2 — practice clinic (60 minutes, same week)

Not a second lecture. Trainees work their **own live queue** with the drafts on, for 50 minutes, with the
champion beside them. Every discarded draft gets its reason; anything odd goes into the issues log on
the spot. Last 10 minutes: each person names one thing the drafts get wrong in their language and one
they get right. The trainer collects those for the champions' first weekly huddle (06.3).

## Role versions

- **Samir (manager), with Rina:** the same cards 1, 3, 7, plus: switching drafts off per channel and
  language; reading the weekly team-level numbers; clearing flags; his own use of the drafts in front of
  the team ("show, don't tell": the frontline-manager questions in 06.4).
- **Rina (claims and returns):** cards 3 and 4 plus inbound translation of Arabic and Russian claim
  messages; she decides claims, the draft never does; recording every claim outcome in the claims log.

## If something goes wrong

- The training Deskly is down: use the printed cards on paper; the checks are the same.
- A trainee says the drafts are bad in their language: agree to collect examples in session 2 and bring
  them to the champions' huddle. Do not argue it in the room.
- A trainee asks "will this replace me?": give the answer from the comms pack FAQ word for word, and say
  who approved it (Michal and Yoav). Do not improvise a better promise.
