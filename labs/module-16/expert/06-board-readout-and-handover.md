## The pre-read (one page, Ronit presents, I answer questions)

**The decision we ask for:** adopt the one-page rules (v1) for staff and volunteers, and approve no new
spending: cancel ₪4,497 a year of unused or risky subscriptions; any AI purchase comes back in March with
evidence.

**What we found (one sentence):** our people already use AI — often on personal accounts, sometimes with
family and donor details — and our shared drive was open far wider than our policy says; most of the
writing burden came from one funder's report format.

**What we did in four weeks:**
1. Closed the exposures: care sheets no longer open by link (18 Nov); `Families` from 46 accounts to 9;
   former staff suspended; donor sheet from 14 editors to 4, ID numbers separated; a second administrator.
2. Adler-Haddad agreed a shorter report from Q4 (our outcomes framework and up to six stories told with the
   family's approval). Coordinators spent about 6 hours each in the last report week; we expect about 2.
3. Two tools on the Google accounts we already have, at no cost: a grant drafter (Noa: 8 of 10 drafts
   usable; one invented number caught and fixed) and a case-note and letter helper (Arabic and Russian
   letters usable; Amharic stays human; the coordinator removes names first, always).
4. One page of rules (attached).

**What we recommend against:** paying for any AI tool now (the free tools have not yet hit their limits;
decide in March with Noa's use data); GrantGenie (it trains on what we upload and converts to US$49 a month
on 1 January); any AI with donor data (a mail merge does the job); a chatbot for families.

**What a "no" means:** the rules stay unwritten; people keep using personal accounts "carefully"; the
subscriptions renew.

**Still open (for the lawyer, answers expected in January):** our security level; whether the Kiryat Hadas
agreement means we need a privacy protection officer; what the social-work confidentiality duty means for
AI; what to do about the September newsletter.

## The hardest finding, rehearsed with Ronit (Monday 7 December)

The hardest finding is hers: the newsletter story was written by her, with her own ChatGPT, from a
coordinator's notes, about a family on the old consent form, and the mother found out from a neighbour.
First attempt (I opened with the consent statistics): she defended the story's purpose for five minutes.
Second attempt: I started with what the mother asked for — an apology, in Arabic, from someone senior — and
then the facts. Ronit: "I'll call her this week, with Samah. And I'll tell the board myself." She did, in
one sentence, at the start of item 2. The board's question turned from "why did this happen?" to "what
else do we need to fix?", which is the question the rules page answers.
