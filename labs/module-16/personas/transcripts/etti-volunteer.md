**Interviewer:** Etti, Ronit says you've led more weekends than anyone. How did you start?

**Etti:** 2012. My neighbour's grandson. I was a kindergarten teacher, I thought, what's one more weekend.
Eighty weekends later... *(laughs)*

**Interviewer:** What makes a weekend go well?

**Etti:** Knowing each child. Who needs his music to fall asleep, who can't have nuts, when the medicine is.

**Interviewer:** How do you know that before you arrive?

**Etti:** The care sheet. The link is in the WhatsApp group, I open it on the phone.

**Interviewer:** And during the weekend, if something changes — a medication time, say?

**Etti:** We write it in the group. With the child's name, so the counsellor sees immediately. That's the
rule.

**Interviewer:** What happens to the group after the weekend?

**Etti:** Amir closes it after a week. *(pause)* Mostly. I have some groups from last year still. People
don't leave.

**Interviewer:** And photos?

**Etti:** The parents love the photos. We send them in the group on Saturday afternoon.

**Interviewer:** You signed a confidentiality form at some point?

**Etti:** Years ago, on paper. With Liat. I think.

**Interviewer:** You use your own Gmail for the drive?

**Etti:** Amir added it in 2019. I see the care sheets and the weekend lists.

**Interviewer:** If things changed so that there were fewer details in the WhatsApp groups, what would make
that easy for volunteers?

**Etti:** Not another app, motek. Many of the volunteers are students, some are seventy. One printed sheet
per child, like the old days. And the counsellor has the details.

**Interviewer:** That's really helpful. Thank you.
