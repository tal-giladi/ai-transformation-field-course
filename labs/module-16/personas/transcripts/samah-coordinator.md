# Transcript — Samah Khatib (senior family coordinator)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 16.2. Tuesday 17 November 2026, 13:00, the Open Hands room in Shefa-'Amr,
35 minutes. The interviewer is the pro bono consultant. Read it as if you were sitting in: note what the
interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Samah. Before anything: I'm a volunteer, here four weeks. Nothing you say will
be written down with your name unless you agree. Is that OK?

**Samah:** Yes.

**Interviewer:** Could you tell me about your work with families — a normal week?

**Samah:** About eighty families. Calls, visits, committees, National Insurance. After each contact I write
a note. Letters to families in Arabic. And every quarter, Adler-Haddad.

**Interviewer:** When do you write the notes?

**Samah:** At night, mostly. In the day I'm with families.

**Interviewer:** Could you walk me through the last Adler-Haddad report — how you did it, step by step?

**Samah:** *(pause)* I take my notes from the quarter, for each family under the grant. I write 150 to 250
words in English: situation, what we did, outcome, next step. Hana sends some back. Shira collects them.

**Interviewer:** How do you get from Hebrew notes to English text?

**Samah:** I think in Arabic, write notes in Hebrew, the foundation wants English. *(pause)* I use a tool
to help with the English. On my phone.

**Interviewer:** Which tool, and which account?

**Samah:** Gemini. My own Google. There is no other way I finish.

**Interviewer:** Do you paste the notes as they are?

**Samah:** *(long pause)* Yes. With the names. I know. I take out the family name, not always the child's
name.

**Interviewer:** Thank you for telling me. This stays without your name. Is it the same for letters to
families?

**Samah:** Letters, yes, sometimes. Committee decisions come in Hebrew; I explain them in Arabic. Mostly by
voice message, many parents read little.

**Interviewer:** In the time log you had about seven and a half hours on the narratives that week. Is that
the whole thing?

**Samah:** That week. It's a lot of evenings.

**Interviewer:** If you could change one thing about the report, what would it be?

**Samah:** That they don't need 120 stories. Who reads 120 stories?

**Interviewer:** Would it help if the foundation accepted numbers plus a few stories?

**Samah:** It would give me my evenings back. But stories need the family's agreement. The old consent form
doesn't say anything about stories.

**Interviewer:** Has that been a problem?

**Samah:** There was a story in the newsletter in September. A family of mine. The mother was hurt. I told
Ronit.

**Interviewer:** Would the family talk to me, do you think?

**Samah:** I can ask. She may say yes.
