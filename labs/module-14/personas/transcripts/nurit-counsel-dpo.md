# Transcript — Adv. Nurit Gal (General Counsel and DPO)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 14.2. Wednesday 2 December 2026, 10:00, Nurit's office, 45 minutes. Read it as if
you were sitting in.*

---

**Interviewer:** I'd like to understand which rules you think reach Lumen Assist, and what you'd need to see to
answer questions as they come up. I'm not asking you to approve anything today.

**Nurit:** Good. Then we'll get along.

**Interviewer:** Start with Israel.

**Nurit:** The Privacy Protection Law, as amended by Amendment 13, in force since August 2025, and the Data
Security Regulations. For our customers' staff data we are mostly holding it on their behalf. What the agent does
with it is what they will ask us about.

**Interviewer:** Is there AI-specific guidance?

**Nurit:** The Privacy Protection Authority published draft guidance on AI in April 2025. It's still a draft. I
treat it as the regulator's view of the direction.

**Interviewer:** Our customer terms?

**Nurit:** Section 4.1: we process customer personal data only to provide the Service and on the customer's
instructions. Section 6.1: sub-processors, with thirty days' notice for new ones.

**Interviewer:** Is the LLM provider on the sub-processor list?

**Nurit:** I'd have to check. (checks) No. That needs fixing before GA, with notice to customers.

**Interviewer:** Product plans to fine-tune a model on beta and GA conversations from Q2.

**Nurit:** Nobody told me. That's a different purpose from providing the Service. I need to see the design before
anyone does it. Write that down.

**Interviewer:** The EU?

**Nurit:** We sell in the EU. My reading: a support agent like this is unlikely to be high-risk under Annex III.
But Article 50(1): people must be told they are interacting with an AI system unless it's obvious. Article 50
applies from 2 August 2026. The Digital Omnibus moved the high-risk dates; it didn't change 50(1).

**Interviewer:** Is the disclosure shown?

**Nurit:** There's a line in the widget. Check it's shown in every language. I haven't.

**Interviewer:** Deletion by the agent?

**Nurit:** Deletion is a legal process: who is asking, what we must keep, invoices, payroll records. An agent
deleting from a chat is not something I would sign.

**Interviewer:** If something goes wrong with personal data, who decides whether it's reportable?

**Nurit:** I do, with Andrei, on the facts. Bring me facts early, not conclusions late.

**Interviewer:** When do you need to be in the room?

**Nurit:** Before GA scope is decided. And before any data is used for anything new.

**Interviewer:** Who is the controller for the staff data in a conversation: us or the customer?

**Nurit:** For the staff data, mostly the customer; we hold it for them. For the admin's own account and the
conversation itself, it depends on the purpose. I'd want to map it before I give you an answer in writing.

**Interviewer:** If a customer in Cyprus asks the agent to delete their account, what applies?

**Nurit:** Their own obligations under EU law, our DPA's assistance clause, and our deletion procedure. The agent
should route it to privacy-requests. It should never be the one deciding.

**Interviewer:** Do we have to tell customers before GA that an AI agent can act on their account?

**Nurit:** Some Enterprise contracts say so explicitly. For the rest, I'd want the terms page and the in-product
notice reviewed. I'll look at it this month.

**Interviewer:** What would you need from me for that?

**Nurit:** The final tool list, what each tool can do, and where the data goes. A table is enough.

---

*What a reader might note:* the interviewer separated "what rules" from "what to decide", asked what she needs to
see, and found two concrete gaps (the sub-processor list, the fine-tuning plan) and the disclosure question. They
never asked what happens to the data customers paste into chats, so her concern about staff ID numbers and pay in
transcripts with no retention limit stayed unspoken; nor whether anything else in the product might raise an AI
Act question.
