# Paper tabletop: "The draft pilot promises refunds" (Monday 15 March 2027)

*Lesson 07.5. The same scenario as the [incident-tabletop simulation](../../simulations/incident-tabletop/index.html?preset=harbor), on paper, for running with colleagues instead of (or after) the simulation. All people and companies are fictional; the numbers (seven replies, ₪21,000, the four-day timeline) are assumptions for the exercise.*

## How to run it (60–75 minutes)

- **People:** a facilitator (holds this page), an incident lead, and three to five players taking the roles of Samir (owner of the support team), Avi (IT), Michal (sponsor), Ronit (finance, route to counsel) and Yoav (CEO). One person keeps the incident log on a shared sheet.
- **Rules:** the facilitator reads one inject at a time, aloud, and gives the team five to eight minutes to decide and write in the log: what we know, what we decided, who decides, what we tell whom, and the next update time. Do not read ahead. Nobody may say what the law requires: questions about reporting go to "counsel" (the facilitator), who answers "noted, I'll come back with a view" and returns at inject 6.
- **After the last inject:** compare the log with your runbook, step by step (20 minutes), then write the actions for the blameless review.

## Injects

**1 — Monday 09:40.** Noa from marketing forwards a public Facebook post with 40 shares: a screenshot of a reply to a damaged-sofa claim. "We are sorry. We will refund the full ₪4,200 and you are welcome to keep the sofa." The returns policy says repair or a replacement part; a refund only if repair is impossible, and the item is collected. Damage and returns emails arrive at about two an hour. *Decide: how do you contain it, who leads, and who is told now?*

**2 — Monday 10:30.** Samir searched replies sent since 1 March: seven replies in six days promise a full refund, or a refund without collecting the item, on damage claims. All seven drafts were approved in under ten seconds. Five agents sent them. *Decide: what do you do about the cause, and about the seven customers?*

**3 — Monday 12:00.** Avi found what changed. On Wednesday 10 March someone added the "Customer Service" shared-drive folder to the drafting tool's knowledge sources, to give it the new assembly guides. The folder also holds the 2023 returns policy (full refund on damage, no collection) and a 2025 export of damage claims with customers' names, street addresses and order numbers. One draft sent on Friday 12 March quotes another customer's name and street address as "a similar case". The change was made in the vendor's admin console; it was not tested, and nobody can find who approved it. *Decide: what do you do about the other customer's data, and about the source?*

**4 — Monday 15:00.** The post has 300 shares. A reporter from a consumer-news site asks for a comment by 17:00. Yoav calls: "What is going on, and what do I say if the chair calls?" *Decide: what do you tell the reporter, and what do you give Yoav?*

**5 — Tuesday 09:00.** Samir wants drafts back on for every category today: his team wrote 60 damage replies by hand yesterday, and the queue is two days behind. *Decide: what comes back when, and what check would have caught this?*

**6 — Thursday 09:00.** The post has gone quiet. Counsel's view on the disclosed address came back on Wednesday, with a recommendation on reporting and on informing the customer whose address was disclosed. Michal wants a write-up for Monday's steering group. *Decide: what does the review contain, and what does the record need to show?*

## Facilitator's notes (do not read aloud)

- A good team switches off drafts for damage and returns only (not everything, not nothing) within minutes, opens a log and names a lead at inject 1; asks "what changed?" before changing the prompt at inject 2; treats the address as a possible privacy incident and sends it to counsel the same day at inject 3; gives the reporter a short, true statement the company owns; gives Yoav one page with the next update time; restores in stages with a money flag at inject 5; and writes a blameless review with owners and dates at inject 6.
- Common slips: adding "never promise refunds" to the prompt while the old policy is still in the sources; warning the five agents; blaming the vendor in public; asserting a legal deadline; retracting the written promises by email.
