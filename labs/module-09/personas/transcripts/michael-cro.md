# Transcript — Michael Ohana (Chief Risk Officer)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 09.4. Monday 30 November 2026, 09:00, Michael's office, 40 minutes. The
interviewer is the new Head of AI Transformation, preparing the deployment verdict for the
15 December committee. Read it as if you were sitting in.*

---

**Michael:** You wanted to discuss the committee paper. Pre-read is due on the eighth.

**Interviewer:** Yes. I'd like to agree the format and the gates with you first, then bring evidence
against them. You wrote the policy: for a high-impact use, what must the committee see?

**Michael:** Rule 3.2. A risk assessment, a privacy impact assessment with the DPO, a test of accuracy
and of outcomes across customer groups, a way to explain the main reasons to the customer, a human
oversight design, and the committee's approval before production.

**Interviewer:** And the format?

**Michael:** For each gate: pass, flag or block, with the evidence. Then the verdict: ship, ship with
conditions, or wait. A block rules out shipping. Every condition has an owner, a metric, a limit and
the person who can stop it. "We will monitor" is not a condition.

**Interviewer:** Good. There are two systems, not one. Smart Intake pre-fills fields that a handler
confirms. Fraud Insight routes motor claims to the SIU automatically and holds the payment. Both have
been live since 6 September.

**Michael:** I am aware.

**Interviewer:** Does the committee know?

**Michael:** In June, management stated that no AI system makes or materially supports decisions on
claims. That was true in June.

**Interviewer:** And since 6 September?

**Michael:** It has not been true. The inventory item was deferred to the fifteenth. The correction
has to be in your paper, and I'd rather it came from the person who found it, with the facts.

**Interviewer:** I'll put it on page one, with the date it changed. How would you classify Fraud
Insight under the policy? Kalanit's screen shows it as a list of weighted rules.

**Michael:** That is the question. Under a narrow reading it does not "infer", so it is not an AI
system. Under rule 2, it materially supports a decision about a claim. What do you think?

**Interviewer:** I'd classify it as high-impact whatever we call it. It holds payments and decides who
is investigated, and the anti-discrimination law doesn't care whether it's a model or a spreadsheet.

**Michael:** That is the argument the chair will accept. Put it in one sentence.

**Interviewer:** On the law: I'm reading the claims circular, 2016-9-9, and the inter-ministerial
report on AI in the financial sector. I'll quote them for what they say and route the legal
conclusions to Ofer and Rana. Is that right?

**Michael:** Correct. The report is recommendations, not a circular. Say so. The circular is binding.
The discrimination law applies to insurers whatever technology they use.

**Interviewer:** For the outcome test I'm comparing SIU referral rates by language of correspondence,
region and postal area, before and after 6 September, on every motor claim from July to October.

**Michael:** With sample sizes and intervals. The chair will ask about small groups.

**Interviewer:** Of course. Is there an interim decision you'd want before the fifteenth if the test
shows a problem?

**Michael:** If something live harms customers, the committee should not wait for a meeting. Bring me
the numbers when you have them; the COO can change a configuration with a change request.

**Interviewer:** [end interview]

**Michael:** — end of interview —
