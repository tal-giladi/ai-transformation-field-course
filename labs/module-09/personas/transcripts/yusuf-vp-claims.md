# Transcript — Yusuf Darawshe (VP Claims)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 09.2. Monday 9 November 2026, 10:00, Yusuf's office in Ramat Gan, 40
minutes. The interviewer is the new Head of AI Transformation. Read it as if you were sitting in.*

---

**Yusuf:** Welcome. Ilana told me you will make claims faster. Many people came to make claims faster.

**Interviewer:** I'm not here to make anything faster yet. I want to understand how a claim actually
moves. Could you take me through a normal motor claim, from the call to the money?

**Yusuf:** The claimant calls, or more often the agent emails us. The intake clerk registers it in
Kalanit. We send the list of documents. The claimant sends them, slowly. The assessor sees the car.
When everything is in, the handler marks "file complete", decides, and finance pays on Monday or
Thursday.

**Interviewer:** When does the list of documents go out, in practice?

**Yusuf:** The manual says the same day. In practice, when the clerk has the file in front of her.
With agents, often when the agent has sent "everything", which is never everything.

**Interviewer:** And then?

**Yusuf:** Then a second request, sometimes a third. A photo is not clear, the estimate is missing a
page, a handler decides he wants a police confirmation.

**Interviewer:** How often does that happen — a second round?

**Yusuf:** I would guess half the files. Hadas in QA did a time study in October; she may have more.

**Interviewer:** I'll ask her. The dashboard says 10.5 days on average. Does that match what you see?

**Yusuf:** It is the number from "file complete" to decision. That part is not bad.

**Interviewer:** And before "file complete"?

**Yusuf:** Before that, the documents. That is where the claimant waits. You want my honest view? The
dashboard measures the part that was already fast. Internal audit said something similar last year.

**Interviewer:** That's very useful. How is "file complete" set — is there any judgement in it?

**Yusuf:** The handler sets it when the last document arrives. If he later finds something missing, he
asks again, and the next "file complete" is the one that counts. So the clock is always short.

**Interviewer:** So the 30-day regulatory clock is respected, and the claimant can still wait six
weeks.

**Yusuf:** Yes. Exactly that.

**Interviewer:** What about the winter? Ilana's pilot starts on 1 December.

**Yusuf:** December to February is twice the work. Storms, water in homes, accidents on wet roads. In
January intake falls a week behind. The six temporary clerks start training on the sixth of
December. If you ask my team leads to check a new system in January, they will say yes and do nothing,
because they cannot.

**Interviewer:** That's clear. Smart Intake has been running in Haifa and Ramat Gan since September.
What do the clerks say?

**Yusuf:** It fills things in, but you check everything anyway. And the Arabic invoices come out
empty. Some garages write the numbers in Arabic digits.

**Interviewer:** And Fraud Insight?

**Yusuf:** The SIU is busier. Moti asked for more external investigator days.

**Interviewer:** Busier with what kind of claims?

**Yusuf:** Motor. You should ask Moti for his numbers.

**Interviewer:** I will. Last thing: if you could fix one thing before the winter, without any
technology, what would it be?

**Yusuf:** One complete list of documents, the first day, in the claimant's language. Half of my
problems would go away.

**Interviewer:** [end interview]

**Yusuf:** — end of interview —

---

**What to notice.** Open, workflow questions ("take me through a normal claim") and the follow-ups
("when, in practice?") got the document chase, the "file complete" clock, the audit finding and the
winter capacity. The last question ("one thing, without technology") is a good discovery close.

**Questions nobody asked.** What changed in the SIU queue since September, by centre or by language of correspondence? (He deflected to Moti; nobody made it safe for him to answer.) Try them in your own interview.
