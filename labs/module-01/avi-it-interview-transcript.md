# Transcript — Avi Mizrahi (IT manager)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 01.6, for learners who prefer not to roleplay. Wednesday 4 November
2026, 09:00, the IT room, 40 minutes. The interviewer is the new Head of AI and Operations
Improvement, preparing the shadow-AI census. Read it as if you were sitting in: note what the
interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked. Fictional people and company.*

---

**Interviewer:** Thanks for the time, Avi. I'm trying to find out where AI is already used here, so we
can write sensible rules. I'm not looking for anyone to blame, and I'll report by department, not by
person. What sources do we have?

**Avi:** Sources… the SaaS list is mine, a spreadsheet, about twenty applications. Last proper review
was in 2025. Some AI columns say "Unknown" — I never went through the vendors' feature updates, sorry.

**Interviewer:** That's useful already. What else?

**Avi:** The web proxy. NetGuard, the MSP runs it. It logs every request: time, a pseudonymised user ID,
department, destination host, category, bytes in and out. We keep 90 days. I can export it.

**Interviewer:** What does it show about AI sites?

**Avi:** *(pause)* Honestly? A lot of chatgpt.com from Customer Service. Some Gemini, a bit of claude.ai.
DeepL. Marketing goes to a copywriting site, copygenie. And there's an "api.linguaflow.ai" from several
departments that the proxy can't even categorise. I saw it in the spring.

**Interviewer:** And nothing happened then?

**Avi:** Nothing forbids it. The 2021 policy doesn't mention AI. And I'm one person — I didn't want to
start a war with Samir and Noa.

**Interviewer:** Fair. Nobody gave you a rule to enforce. Could you export two weeks, by department,
with the user IDs kept pseudonymised?

**Avi:** The 11th to the 22nd of October is easy. Same format as always.

**Interviewer:** Can the IDs be mapped back to names?

**Avi:** Through the MSP, yes. But I won't do that without management and legal saying so. You don't
need names for a picture by department, do you?

**Interviewer:** No, I don't. Departments are enough. What about software on the laptops?

**Avi:** The MSP's device management exports the browser extensions on every machine, monthly. The last
one is from 28 October. There'll be translation and writing extensions on it — people install them from
the Chrome store. The policy says software needs my approval; nobody ever decided if extensions count.

**Interviewer:** And paid tools people buy themselves?

**Avi:** Expense claims are in Priority. Finance has them, not me. Ask Ronit or Natalia.

**Interviewer:** Where do customers' data live, in case someone pastes it somewhere?

**Avi:** Deskly is in the US region, StoreHub in the EU, Priority in Israel. I wrote a note for Michal
about retention — nobody ever decided most of it.

**Interviewer:** If you could have one thing from this programme, what would it be?

**Avi:** A written AI policy and one approved tool for everyone. So people stop using ten free ones and
I have something to enforce.

**Interviewer:** That's on the plan for the next two weeks. Last question: who should I talk to about
AI tools in their own teams?

**Avi:** Samir for Customer Service. Noa for marketing. Eyal in purchasing — his buyers translate
supplier emails all day.

**Interviewer:** Thanks, Avi. I'll send you the exact export request today: proxy 11–22 October,
pseudonymised, and the 28 October extension inventory.

**Avi:** Send it in writing and I'll have it by tomorrow.

**Interviewer:** [end interview]

**Avi:** — end of interview —
