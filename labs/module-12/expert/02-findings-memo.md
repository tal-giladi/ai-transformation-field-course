# 02 · Findings memo — Atlas & Rowe

To Yoav Dagan, 27 November 2026, for the 3 December committee. Labels: **M** measured from the data
room, **R** reported in interviews (unattributed), **I** inferred. Nobody is named; no finding is about
an individual.

## The answer first

The firm is not choosing whether to use AI: about four in five lawyers already reach consumer AI tools
from the office, client documents among what goes in, without a rule, consent or a check. A
fabricated citation has already nearly reached a client. The knowledge base any tool would search is
mostly unreviewed. And on half the firm's fees, saving time removes fees, while clients already take
their share when work gets faster. The question for the partners is the order: rule and knowledge
first, then a tested tool, with pricing that keeps part of the gain.

## Findings

1. **AI use is widespread and invisible to the firm's own survey.** 52 of 90 lawyers (43 of 64
   associates, 9 partners) reached AI sites from the office or VPN in September–October; 27 uploaded
   171 files (M, proxy). In the October survey 28% said weekly use and 89% said they never put client
   information in a tool (M, survey). The proxy cannot see phones (R), so the true figure is higher.
   *Consequence:* any rule must start from current practice, not from a blank page.
2. **Client documents have gone into consumer tools.** DLP: a client-confidential draft SPA on 3
   September (closed without telling the client; outside ethics counsel asked, no answer), three more
   client-marker alerts in October still open (M, DLP and ethics log). One group has used personal paid
   accounts on client documents since 2024, reimbursed as "software": 58 claims, ₪4,225 in 2026 (M,
   expenses; R).
3. **A fabricated citation nearly reached a client (20 October).** A non-compete memo cited a ruling
   that does not exist (FCR-2022-318), a ruling reversed on appeal (FCR-2020-460, reversed by
   FCR-2022-390) and a paragraph saying the opposite of the claim (FCR-2021-124 ¶5); it was approved for
   sending and stopped the next morning by a senior associate (M, memo export; R). Not reported (R).
   The firm's insurer asks about near misses by 15 January (M, questionnaire 6.5). *Route to Orit.*
4. **Research is about a quarter of associates' time, and it is hidden.** Narratives show research on
   25.2% of associate hours; the RES code on 6.2% (M, time entries, three groups, Q3). RES lines are
   written off 40.0% of the time, research recorded as drafting 12.0%, other work 4.3% (M). Associates
   record research as drafting because RES gets cut (R). *Consequence:* the firm's own data
   understates where AI would act, and the write-offs show who already pays for research: the firm.
5. **There is no backlog.** Associates are at 81% of target; 1 of 64 on target (M). Freed hours cannot
   be sold today (R, finance).
6. **Where research sits by billing type:** hourly 56.8%, fixed 24.0%, capped 14.5%, retainer 4.7% (M).
   On hourly work a saved hour is a lost fee; on fixed fees it is margin. Tech & IP's fixed fees took
   38.1% more hours than they pay for in 2025 (M, matters).
7. **Clients take their share.** After the 2025 NDA automation (3 days → under 1; 2.38 → 0.81 hours per
   NDA), Carmel moved to a fixed ₪1,150 per NDA: NDA fees about a third below 2024 though volume rose,
   while the firm's fees per hour on NDAs rose from ₪860 to ₪1,422 (M, NDA workstream). Two other
   clients cited automation or turnaround to win discounts (M, renewals log). A litigation panel was
   lost to a competitor with fixed fees about 30% lower and AI-assisted review (M). One renewal request
   for 2027 is open (M; detail with the managing partner).
8. **Clients have AI terms.** Four clients' guidelines have AI clauses; Carmel's require disclosure,
   consent, verification, no hourly for technology-avoidable time and 48-hour incident notice (M).
9. **The knowledge base is stale.** 1,180 documents; 71% never reviewed; 105 privacy documents still
   "current" and not reviewed since Amendment 13 came into force (M, KM index). In the 78 documents
   exported for the vendor's PoC, 13 are superseded and the index marks 2 (M, corpus).
10. **The vendor's claims were measured elsewhere.** 6.2 hours: self-reported survey at US/UK firms;
    demo: 12 English questions on US law; Hebrew beta; whole-DMS indexing that does not follow the
    firm's DMS permissions (ethical walls) (M, vendor documents). In the vendor's own PoC on the firm's
    documents, 28 of 80 citations fail a basic check (none invented; 10 superseded, 12 misstated) (M,
    pilot test set).

## What I did not find

file for AI use; no AI policy; no owner for knowledge.
