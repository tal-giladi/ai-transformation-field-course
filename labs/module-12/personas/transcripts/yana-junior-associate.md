# Transcript — Adv. Yana Petrenko (junior associate, International desk)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 12.2. Tuesday 24 November 2026, 18:30, a small meeting room, 30
minutes. Read it as if you were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and
which questions worked.*

---

**Interviewer:** Thank you for staying. Let me say how this works: I'm talking to about a dozen people.
Nothing goes into my notes with a name, and this isn't about checking up on anyone. The firm wants to
know how the work is really done so that any rule it makes fits real life.

**Yana:** OK. Leah said to be helpful.

**Interviewer:** What does a typical week look like for you?

**Yana:** SPAs, shareholders' agreements, side letters for foreign investors. In English. Some clients
send things in Russian.

**Interviewer:** How late do you usually work?

**Yana:** Nine, ten. Sometimes later.

**Interviewer:** When you get a research question late at night, what do you do?

**Yana:** I look in Mikbatz first. If it's not there I ask someone, but at night there's no one.

**Interviewer:** And then?

**Yana:** (pause) Sometimes ChatGPT. Everyone does.

**Interviewer:** That's useful to know, and normal. How do you check what it gives you?

**Yana:** I try to open the cases. When there's time.

**Interviewer:** And when there isn't?

**Yana:** (quietly) Then sometimes I don't. I thought the cases looked real.

**Interviewer:** Did something go wrong once?

**Yana:** Is this going to my partner?

**Interviewer:** No. I don't record names. I'm interested in what in the system lets it happen, not in
who.

**Yana:** In October I was lent to litigation for a week. A memo at eleven at night. I pasted the
clause and the question, not the client's name, into the free ChatGPT, and I used the cases it gave.
The partner approved it in the morning. Karim stopped it. One case didn't exist. I haven't slept well
since.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. That took courage. What would have helped you that night?

**Yana:** A firm tool that only answers from our own documents. That shows the paragraph. That says
"not found" instead of inventing.

**Interviewer:** And what worries you about AI, for yourself?

**Yana:** That juniors stop getting first drafts. That's how we learn. And my hours are already below
target.

**Interviewer:** If there were a firm rule and a firm tool, would people follow it?

**Yana:** If the tool is good and the rule is clear. Now nobody knows what's allowed.

**Interviewer:** Did you know what the firm's rules on AI are?

**Yana:** There's an IT policy from 2019. It doesn't say anything about AI. People say "don't put
client names in".

**Interviewer:** Has anyone trained you on these tools?

**Yana:** No. YouTube.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything you'd like the partners to hear, without your name?

**Yana:** That we're not lazy. We're alone at night.
