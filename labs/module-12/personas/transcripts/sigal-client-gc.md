# Transcript — Adv. Sigal Mizrahi (general counsel, Carmel Retail Group)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 12.4. Tuesday 8 December 2026, 16:00, video call, 30 minutes. Yoav
agreed to the meeting and knows its purpose. Read it as if you were sitting in: note what the
interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Sigal:** I assume you're here to explain why fifteen percent is too much.

**Interviewer:** No. I advise the firm on how it uses AI. I'm not negotiating for it. I'd like to
understand what Carmel needs, so the firm's decision takes it into account.

**Sigal:** All right. My board wants external legal spend down twenty percent next year.

**Interviewer:** Which parts of the spend matter most to them?

**Sigal:** The repeat work. Supplier agreements, lease renewals, employment documents, NDAs. Hundreds a
year. Disputes and deals are different.

**Interviewer:** You had an experience with NDAs.

**Sigal:** Yes. In 2025 they got our NDAs from three days to under one. Great. Then I asked for a fixed
price; we agreed ₪1,150 per NDA. I pay much less than before. That's what I want for everything
repeatable.

**Interviewer:** And the fifteen percent on hourly rates?

**Sigal:** (laughs) That's my opening. What I want is fixed prices for the repeat work, maybe a quarterly
subscription for routine advice, hourly only for real disputes and deals. If the package costs us about
ten percent less than this year on that work and it's predictable, I can defend it.

**Interviewer:** That's very clear. When does it need to be on your desk?

**Sigal:** Before the panel review closes on 31 January.

**Interviewer:** Your guidelines have an AI section. What's behind it?

**Sigal:** Two fears. A fake case in something with Carmel's name on it. And Carmel's data in a tool
that trains on it.

**Interviewer:** What would you need in order to consent to AI use on your matters?

**Sigal:** An enterprise tool that doesn't train on our data and deletes it. Tell me which tool. A
lawyer verifies every citation, and that's documented. And if something goes wrong, I hear it from
the firm, not from a court.

**Interviewer:** Section 7.4 says you won't pay hourly for time a reasonably efficient firm wouldn't
spend. How would you judge that?

**Sigal:** I wouldn't, on each bill. That's why I want fixed prices. Then their efficiency is their
business.

**Interviewer:** So if the firm becomes more efficient on fixed-price work, you don't need to see the
hours.

**Sigal:** Exactly. I need the price and the quality.

**Interviewer:** How do you measure quality on repeat work today?

**Sigal:** Turnaround, and how often my team has to fix what comes back. On NDAs, almost never now.

**Interviewer:** Would you want to know which tool a firm uses, or just that it meets your conditions?

**Sigal:** Which tool, once. Then that it meets the conditions. I don't want a technology report every
quarter.

**Interviewer:** If the firm proposed fixed prices for twelve contract types, how many would matter
most?

**Sigal:** Supplier agreements first. Then leases and employment documents. NDAs are done.

**Interviewer:** And the advice your team asks for informally by phone?

**Sigal:** That's where a subscription makes sense. I hate six-minute units for a five-minute question.

**Interviewer:** What would make you leave the panel?

**Sigal:** A surprise. On price or on quality.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. I'll pass this to Yoav and Eli as you said it.

---

*Notes for the learner.* Opening by refusing to negotiate, and asking what her board needs, turned the
15% into a package she would accept and gave her conditions for consent in her own words. The
interviewer never asked who else she is talking to, so her test with a rival firm (30 supplier
agreements at fixed prices with AI-assisted review) stayed hidden, and with it the urgency.
