# Transcript — Adv. Noa Friedman (partner, Tech & IP)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 12.2. Thursday 19 November 2026, 09:00, the café downstairs, 40
minutes. Read it as if you were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and
which questions worked.*

---

**Noa:** I'll be honest, I'm worried you're here to slow us down. The discount expires on the 31st.

**Interviewer:** I'm here to make sure whatever the firm decides survives the vote and the first six
months. I'd like to start with your practice, not the product. What does your group actually do?

**Noa:** Start-ups, mostly. Seed and Series A packages, SaaS terms, privacy documents. Fixed fees,
because founders can't stomach hourly bills. Some hourly work for bigger tech clients.

**Interviewer:** Walk me through a normal Tuesday for one of your associates on a seed package.

**Noa:** Term sheet comes in. They mark it up against our form, draft the SHA, the investment
agreement, the board resolutions, answer the founders' questions at ten at night.

**Interviewer:** And what tools do they use for that today? I'm not looking for anyone to blame. I want
to know what the firm's real starting point is.

**Noa:** (long pause) Fine. My team has used ChatGPT Plus since spring 2024. Six of us. Personal
accounts, I approve the expenses as software. They paste in term sheets, SHAs, privacy policies.
Mostly with names taken out. Somebody had to.

**Interviewer:** Thank you for telling me. Have clients been told?

**Noa:** No. It never came up.

**Interviewer:** Your slide says 6.2 hours a week per lawyer. Where does that number come from?

**Noa:** From Parshan. Their customers.

**Interviewer:** Measured how?

**Noa:** A survey, I think. Their deck has a footnote. US and UK firms.

**Interviewer:** So self-reported, by other firms' lawyers, on other firms' work.

**Noa:** Yes, but every firm reports the same thing. It's real.

**Interviewer:** It may be. I'll measure ours. Your slide multiplies the hours by ₪950. If an
associate saves two hours on an hourly matter, what happens to the bill?

**Noa:** It goes down, unless you bill value. My draft policy says partners can bill the time a task
would normally take.

**Interviewer:** That's a question I'll take to Orit, because it's about what the firm may charge, not
about tools. On fixed-fee work the saving stays with the firm, doesn't it?

**Noa:** Exactly. That's my whole point.

**Interviewer:** Would your group pilot something first, before ninety seats?

**Noa:** If it's a real tool and not a committee. But ninety seats or nobody adopts it.

**Interviewer:** What did the demo on 8 October actually test?

**Noa:** Twelve research questions. All answered with sources in seconds.

**Interviewer:** On whose material?

**Noa:** Their demo set. American cases, in English. Tamar has sent them our documents for a proof
of concept.

**Interviewer:** Hebrew?

**Noa:** Beta. They say it's coming.

**Interviewer:** What would make you say, in June, that the tool had failed?

**Noa:** If nobody uses it. Or if it gave us one wrong answer that reached a client. That would end
it.

**Interviewer:** Who checks the outputs in your team today?

**Noa:** The associate, then me. I read everything that goes out.

**Interviewer:** Who in your group should I talk to about how the work is done?

**Noa:** Michal. And any of the associates, they'll tell you.

---

*Notes for the learner.* The interviewer made it safe and got the two-year use of personal accounts,
the source of the 6.2 hours and the billing idea, and routed the billing question correctly. They did
not ask how her fixed fees perform against the hours, so the overruns (and why AI matters so much to
her practice) stayed hidden; the matters file would have given them the number to ask about.
