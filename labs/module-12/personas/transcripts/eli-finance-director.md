# Transcript — Eli Ben-Hamo (finance director)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 12.3. Monday 16 November 2026, 11:00, Eli's office, one hour. The
interviewer had opened the matters file and the finance summary. Read it as if you were sitting in:
note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Eli:** Before we start: what's our realisation?

**Interviewer:** Eighty-seven percent in 2025, on your summary. Lowest in Tech & IP at seventy-six,
because the fixed fees overrun.

**Eli:** Good. You read it. Go on.

**Interviewer:** Noa's slide says ₪23.3M of capacity. How would you read it?

**Eli:** Compared with what? Capacity is hours. Hours become money only if someone buys them.

**Interviewer:** Does the firm have more work than hours today?

**Eli:** No. Associates are at about eighty-one percent of target. One associate is on target. If they
save hours, there's nothing waiting to fill them.

**Interviewer:** So on an hourly matter, an hour saved is…

**Eli:** An hour not billed. Fees down. On a fixed fee, the opposite: same fee, fewer hours, better
margin.

**Interviewer:** How much of the firm's fees are hourly?

**Eli:** About half. A fifth fixed, the rest caps and retainers.

**Interviewer:** Has anything like this happened before, where work got faster?

**Eli:** Carmel's NDAs. In 2025 we automated the template. Three days became less than one. Hours per
NDA went from about two and a half to under one. On hourly billing the fees fell with the hours.

**Interviewer:** And then?

**Eli:** At the renewal Carmel asked for a fixed ₪900 per NDA. We settled at ₪1,150. We earn about a
third less on NDAs than in 2024, though they send more. But per hour we earn about ₪1,400 instead of
₪860. We gave them most of the gain and still did better per hour. Nobody noticed, because nobody looks
per hour.

**Interviewer:** That's the most useful thing I've heard today. Is anything open with Carmel now?

**Eli:** It's in my log as open. Ask Yoav. It's not my news.

**Interviewer:** Fair. How are partners paid?

**Eli:** Modified lockstep. Seventy percent seniority; thirty contribution, half of that their own
billed hours.

**Interviewer:** So a partner whose team bills fewer hours…

**Eli:** Loses points. You understand why the vote looks the way it does.

**Interviewer:** Where do write-offs come from, mostly?

**Eli:** Partners at billing review, research lines especially, and capped matters when the cap is
hit. Fixed fees don't show write-offs; they show in realisation.

**Interviewer:** The Tech & IP fixed fees took about thirty-eight percent more hours than they pay for
in 2025, by the matters file. Is that right?

**Eli:** Roughly. Noa sells packages at prices founders accept and her team works until it's right.

**Interviewer:** If that group did the same work in fewer hours, what changes?

**Eli:** Same fee, fewer hours. Margin. Or capacity to sell more packages, if there's demand. There
is, in her market.

**Interviewer:** Which clients pay us most?

**Eli:** A few large ones. Carmel is fourth.

**Interviewer:** What would you need to believe a business case?

**Eli:** By billing type. Hourly, capped, fixed, retainer, separately. Capacity shown as hours, not
shekels. And what clients will take back at renewal, because they will.

**Interviewer:** Can I have the data to build it that way?

**Eli:** You have most of it. Ask me for anything else.

---

*Notes for the learner.* The interviewer passed Eli's test, asked "compared with what?" on his behalf,
and got the NDA story, the absence of a backlog and how partners are paid. They never asked for his own
model of the Parshan purchase, so they did not learn that finance put it in the budget as a cost only,
with no revenue effect modelled; the finance summary says so in one line.
