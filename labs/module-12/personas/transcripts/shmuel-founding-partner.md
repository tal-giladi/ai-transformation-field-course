# Transcript — Adv. Shmuel Atlas (founding partner, litigation)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 12.3. Tuesday 1 December 2026, 17:00, Shmuel's office, 30 minutes, "no
slides". Read it as if you were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and
which questions worked.*

---

**Shmuel:** So. You're Noa's consultant.

**Interviewer:** I'm Yoav's, and I'm nobody's side. I've read the October minutes and three months of
your group's time entries. I'd like to understand the work, not sell you anything.

**Shmuel:** Three months of time entries. Nobody reads those. What did you find?

**Interviewer:** That research is about a quarter of your associates' hours if you read the
narratives, and about six percent if you read the codes. Why would that be?

**Shmuel:** (laughs) Because I cut RES lines. I don't charge clients for the kids learning the law. So
the kids write "drafting". I'm not stupid. I let it go.

**Interviewer:** On your matters research lines are written off more than anywhere else in the extract.

**Shmuel:** I believe you. The client pays for my judgement, not for a junior reading twenty cases.

**Interviewer:** You said in the meeting, "if my team does the research in half the time, I bill
half." Most of your work is hourly. Is that the heart of your objection?

**Shmuel:** Half of it. The other half is that these machines invent law. You've read about the New
York lawyers. And our own Supreme Court has written about lawyers citing judgments that don't exist.

**Interviewer:** Yes, I've read them. Has anything like that come close here?

**Shmuel:** Here? We check our work.

**Interviewer:** Have you lost work in the last year to firms that price differently?

**Shmuel:** (pause) Fine. We lost Ofek in September. The insurance panel. The other firm offered fixed
fees about thirty percent lower and "AI-assisted document review". They didn't buy better lawyers.
They bought a cheaper process.

**Interviewer:** If a tool did the document review in disclosure, under a lawyer's check, would you
use it?

**Shmuel:** For disclosure, yes. Thousands of emails, that's labour, not judgement. Research, no. Not
unless a person checks every case.

**Interviewer:** What would "checks every case" mean in practice?

**Shmuel:** Open the judgment. Read the paragraph. See if it was appealed. Like I taught Karim.

**Interviewer:** How long does that take per citation?

**Shmuel:** Ask Karim. Ten minutes if you do it right.

**Interviewer:** And the precedent bank? Would a tool that searches it help?

**Shmuel:** Mikbatz is a cemetery. Half of it is from before Karim was born. A machine that searches a
cemetery finds you a nice grave.

**Interviewer:** If the bank were reviewed first, and the tool only searched what was reviewed?

**Shmuel:** Then we'd be talking about a different thing. Who reviews it? Tamar alone, three days a
week?

**Interviewer:** That's a question I'll put to the committee with a cost. What does a client expect
from you that a tool can't give?

**Shmuel:** That I sign it. That if I'm wrong, it's me. A machine doesn't go to the Bar's ethics
committee.

**Interviewer:** How do your associates learn research today?

**Shmuel:** By doing it badly and me marking it in pencil. That's how I learned.

**Interviewer:** If a tool did the first search and the associate checked every source, would they
still learn?

**Shmuel:** Maybe. If they check. Nobody checks at midnight.

**Interviewer:** Last question: what would you want the partners to decide on 14 January?

**Shmuel:** Not to sign a two-year contract for ninety people because of a discount. And to keep the
kids learning research.

---

*Notes for the learner.* The interviewer earned trust quickly by citing the time entries, and got the
write-off habit, the lost Ofek tender and the one place Shmuel would accept help. They asked whether an
error had "come close" in a way that sounded like a test, and accepted "we check our work"; the memo he
approved on 20 October stayed hidden. A softer question about how memos are checked before they reach
clients, with no blame implied, would have opened it.
