# Transcript — Adv. Orit Levinson (general counsel and ethics partner)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 12.4. Wednesday 18 November 2026, 14:00, Orit's office, 45 minutes. The
interviewer brought the DLP log, the engagement-letter template, Noa's draft policy and Carmel's
guidelines. Read it as if you were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed,
and which questions worked.*

---

**Interviewer:** I'm not asking you for an opinion today. I'd like to understand what's already known,
where the decisions sit, and what you need from me.

**Orit:** Good. Then we'll get along.

**Interviewer:** What are the rules a lawyer here works under when client information goes into a
tool?

**Orit:** Two layers. Confidentiality: the Bar's ethics rules, rule 19, a lawyer keeps confidential
what the client brings to his knowledge unless the client expressly agrees otherwise. Privilege:
section 90 of the Bar Association Law protects lawyer-client communications in proceedings unless the
client waives it. Whether putting a document into an outside AI service risks either is exactly what
the ethics committee has started to address.

**Interviewer:** The committee's opinions: what do they say, in your reading?

**Orit:** The first, May 2024, was general. The expanded one, September this year: the lawyer keeps
full responsibility and must verify every output; restricted client information doesn't go into open
systems; where it is used, informed consent, and as I read it a general clause in a retainer isn't
enough. Read it yourself. My summary isn't advice.

**Interviewer:** Our engagement letter, clause 11, says "we may use technology tools". Is that consent?

**Orit:** It was written in 2023 for cloud storage. In my view it is unlikely to be enough. That's for
outside ethics counsel to confirm.

**Interviewer:** The DLP log has an alert on 3 September: a draft SPA uploaded to a consumer AI site.

**Orit:** Yes. A corporate associate. I spoke to him, he deleted the chat history, I closed it. The
client, not Carmel, wasn't told. I've asked outside ethics counsel whether it must be. No answer yet.

**Interviewer:** Three more alerts in October are still open.

**Orit:** I know. I have no rule to apply. That's why I said at the partners' meeting we need one
whatever happens with Parshan.

**Interviewer:** Noa's draft policy?

**Orit:** Section 3, "anonymised documents in any tool", and section 5, billing "the time a task would
normally take", both go to outside ethics counsel. I won't sign either.

**Interviewer:** And the courts?

**Orit:** Our Supreme Court criticised a lawyer in February 2025 for citing judgments that don't exist,
and in March this year ordered a municipality to pay thirty thousand shekels in costs for relying on
fictitious sources. Foreign cases like Mata bind nobody here. Our own courts are clear enough now.

**Interviewer:** Carmel's guidelines ask for disclosure, consent, verification and a 48-hour incident
notice. Can the firm comply today?

**Orit:** Not with confidence. We don't know which tools people use.

**Interviewer:** Which clients have AI terms?

**Orit:** Four that I know of. Carmel, a bank that wants consent, an insurer that prohibits it unless
approved in writing, and a foreign fund that wants disclosure.

**Interviewer:** If the firm bought a platform, would that solve consent?

**Orit:** No. The opinion is clear that a closed platform still needs a risk review, and someone must
stop people who aren't on the matter from seeing the data. A tool doesn't consent for the client.

**Interviewer:** Who in the firm should own an AI rule?

**Orit:** The management committee approves it. I'll own the ethics part. Someone else must own the
tools.

**Interviewer:** If a client asks the firm "do you use AI on my work?", what should a partner say today?

**Orit:** Today? The truth is "some lawyers do, and we're putting a rule in place". That's not a
comfortable sentence.

**Interviewer:** What would you need from me?

**Orit:** The facts on what tools are used, in writing, without names. A draft interim rule I can mark
up. And a date by which the partners decide.

**Interviewer:** I can give you the first two by 3 December.

**Orit:** Then I'll send the rule to outside ethics counsel the same week.

*Notes for the learner.* Bringing the documents and separating rule, opinion and business decision
worked; the interviewer got the incident, the engagement-letter view and her conditions, and agreed
deliverables. They never asked about insurance, so the insurer's AI questionnaire (due 15 January, with
a question on near misses) stayed hidden, and with it the reason the near miss must be known before the
vote. They also did not tell her about the near miss Karim described.
