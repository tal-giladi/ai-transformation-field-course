# Transcript — Adv. Yoav Dagan (managing partner)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 12.1. Sunday 15 November 2026, 08:30, Yoav's office, 45 minutes. Read it
as if you were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions
worked.*

---

**Interviewer:** Thank you for starting early. Before anything else I'd like to read back what I
understood the job to be, and you correct me.

**Yoav:** Please.

**Interviewer:** "Advise the partnership, on the firm's own data, how Atlas & Rowe should use AI in its
legal work from 2027, what to buy if anything, and on what terms; test it on your own material." Is
that it?

**Yoav:** That's it. Though between us, the partners will hear "what to buy". They want a yes or a no
on Parshan.

**Interviewer:** Then I'll write your sentence as the objective and "should we sign Parshan" as the
question the work answers, not as the objective. Is that acceptable?

**Yoav:** It's honest. Yes.

**Interviewer:** Who decides at the end?

**Yoav:** The partners' meeting on 14 January. Twenty-six votes. The management committee prepares it;
I chair. I can't be seen to campaign.

**Interviewer:** What does "done" look like for you on 14 January?

**Yoav:** A paper the partners vote on. Something both camps can live with. And no fight in the room.

**Interviewer:** I'd add two things: a decision minuted with an owner and a date for each part, and,
if we test anything, results against thresholds agreed before the test. And the exits: either of us
can stop on 3 December, and after the decision paper on 17 December.

**Yoav:** Fine. Can we at least know before Christmas whether to take Parshan's discount? It expires
on the 31st.

**Interviewer:** You'll have my view on the discount in the decision paper on the 17th. Has anyone said
anything to Parshan already about the firm's intentions?

**Yoav:** (pause) I may have been too encouraging. Their account executive called after the partners'
meeting and I said we were probably in, that January was a formality. He sends me a reminder every
week.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. I'll treat that as a pressure on the timeline, not a commitment. Does
anything in writing exist?

**Yoav:** No. Only the order form they sent Noa.

**Interviewer:** What are clients saying about AI, or about price?

**Yoav:** Clients ask in pitches whether we "use AI". And… Carmel's general counsel called me on 29
October. From 2027 she wants fifteen percent off all hourly rates. "Your efficiency should reach us."
They're our fourth-largest client.

**Interviewer:** Do the partners know?

**Yoav:** Eli knows. Not the partners. I didn't want to throw a grenade into the AI debate.

**Interviewer:** I understand. I'd like to meet her, with your agreement, before the decision paper.
And I'd like to meet associates, not only partners.

**Yoav:** The partners will want to know who you're talking to.

**Interviewer:** Then I'll tell each supervising partner which of their people I'm meeting, and I'll
keep what people say unattributed. The reason is simple: associates do most of the research, and
they're the ones using tools today.

**Yoav:** All right. Tell me before, not after.

**Interviewer:** And before the committee on 3 December, I'd like an hour with you alone to go
through the findings.

**Yoav:** Good. I prefer no surprises.

**Interviewer:** Who should be the firm's owner of this after I leave on 14 January?

**Yoav:** Good question. Not Noa, it would look like a victory lap. Maybe Orit with Tamar. Let me think.

**Interviewer:** One last thing: what would make this engagement a failure for you?

**Yoav:** A vote of 13 to 13. Or a firm that buys something nobody uses.

---

*Notes for the learner.* The read-back, "done", exits and the private preview were handled well; the
interviewer found the vendor pressure and Carmel's 15%. They never asked how partners are paid or why
the vote splits the way it does, so the compensation reason behind the split stayed hidden. They also
did not ask what Yoav himself thinks the firm should do.
