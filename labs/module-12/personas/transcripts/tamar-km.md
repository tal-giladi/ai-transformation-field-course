# Transcript — Adv. Tamar Golan (head of knowledge management)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 12.2. Tuesday 17 November 2026, 10:00, Tamar's office, one hour. The
interviewer had opened the KM index and three corpus documents. Read it as if you were sitting in:
note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Tamar:** I assume you want the index.

**Interviewer:** I've opened it. I'd rather understand how it's used. How does a document get into
Mikbatz?

**Tamar:** A lawyer uploads it with a title, a group, tags. Status defaults to "current". Nobody
changes it after that.

**Interviewer:** The index shows about seven in ten documents never reviewed after upload. Does that
match what you see?

**Tamar:** It matches. Before 2015 there was nobody; since then there's me, three days a week. The
librarian left in March.

**Interviewer:** Which documents are used most?

**Tamar:** A few hundred carry almost all the use. A quarter are never opened.

**Interviewer:** I looked at the research export you made for Parshan. ARM memos that cite a ruling,
and a later memo that says it replaces the earlier one. Both are marked current in the index.

**Tamar:** Yes. That's exactly the problem. The later one says "this replaces"; the index doesn't
know. Among the 78 I exported, several rely on rulings reversed on appeal, and there's a 2019 supplier
data-processing clause that lets the supplier use client data "to improve its services".

**Interviewer:** How many documents like that across the bank?

**Tamar:** About forty I'd stop people using tomorrow. And around a hundred privacy documents written
before Amendment 13 came into force that nobody has gone through.

**Interviewer:** Did you tell anyone when you made the export?

**Tamar:** I told Noa some were out of date. She said the tool would sort it out.

**Interviewer:** Will it?

**Tamar:** A search tool finds what's there. It doesn't know what's true.

**Interviewer:** What would the tool be allowed to search, if it's connected to Archive?

**Tamar:** That's the other thing. Parshan's answer says its index follows permissions only for two
DMS products. Ours isn't one. So an index of the whole DMS ignores our ethical walls. A lawyer on one
side of a wall could get an answer built from the other client's documents. I wrote to Yoav and Orit
on 21 October. No reply.

**Interviewer:** That's important. I'll take it to the committee with the vendor's own answer.
Who should own each part of the bank?

**Tamar:** A partner per group. Signing off. Not me alone.

**Interviewer:** If the firm pilots a research tool, what should it search?

**Tamar:** Only what's been reviewed. Nothing else.

**Interviewer:** Your 2024 proposal was deferred. What happened?

**Tamar:** "Deferred to the 2025 budget." Then again. Then again on 22 October. Partners see knowledge
as overhead.

**Interviewer:** Who uses Mikbatz most?

**Tamar:** Juniors. Seniors have their own folders. That's the irony: the people least able to tell
what's stale use it most.

**Interviewer:** Does anyone tell you when a document is wrong?

**Tamar:** Sometimes Karim sends me an email. There's no process.

**Interviewer:** What about the Hebrew documents? The vendor says Hebrew is beta.

**Tamar:** Most of our litigation knowledge is in Hebrew. If it doesn't read Hebrew properly, it
doesn't read litigation.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. Can I come back with the list of the 78 and go through them with you?

**Tamar:** Please. Bring coffee.

---

*Notes for the learner.* Opening with the index and a concrete pair of documents earned the specific
examples and the ethical-walls problem. The interviewer never asked what a review would take, so
Tamar's offer (the 78 research documents in two weeks; the top 300 in about eight weeks with two
associates at half time and partner sign-off) stayed hidden, and the shortlist in 12.3 lacks its
effort line.
