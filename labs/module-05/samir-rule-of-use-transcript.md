# Pre-run conversation: agreeing the rule of use with Samir (7 December 2026)

For lesson 05.1, for learners who prefer not to roleplay. You (Head of AI and Operations Improvement) meet Samir Haddad, Customer Service Manager, in the small meeting room for 25 minutes. He has read the draft charter. The interviewer does a decent job and misses at least one thing: note what, and the question that would have found it. All people are fictional.

---

**You:** Thanks for reading the draft. Before we talk about the AI, can we agree who the drafts belong to? My proposal is that they belong to your team: you own the pilot, and you decide when it is switched off.

**Samir:** That is new. Everyone else talks about the AI as if it was yours or the CEO's. If it is mine, then I want the switch. Not a ticket to Avi. A button.

**You:** A setting per channel and per language, held by you and by me. Avi tests it with us in January, before it goes live. Would that work?

**Samir:** Per language is good. If the Arabic is bad, I don't want to stop the Hebrew.

**You:** Then let's go through the three questions. First: what may the draft decide on its own?

**Samir:** Nothing. My people send. If a reply goes out, it is my agent's name on it.

**You:** Agreed, and it's written that way. It suggests a category, a reply and flags. The agent confirms or changes the category, edits or throws away the draft, and sends. It never sends, never closes a ticket, never touches an order. Is there any step where you'd want even less?

**Samir:** Dates. Last year a customer got three different dates from three agents. If the machine writes a date, the customer will believe it more than us.

**You:** The draft may only write a date that comes from the order data, the updated ETA from Tamar's job. If there's no date, it asks for the order number and writes nothing. And any amount of money, never.

**Samir:** Good. And the refunds. Rina decides refunds, not a computer.

**You:** That's the flags. If a message asks for money outside the normal path, a refund after months, compensation for a delay, the draft only acknowledges and the ticket goes to a senior person. The question for you is who may remove such a flag, if it was wrong.

**Samir:** Me or Olga. Not the agent who wants to close the ticket fast. Same for anything about injuries or lawyers.

**You:** Writing it: only Samir or Olga clears a safety, legal, privacy or refund flag. Now the third question: how do we record when an agent disagrees with the AI?

**Samir:** Why do you need to record it? I don't want my people to feel watched.

**You:** Because the disagreements are how we find out whether it works. If agents throw away a third of the Arabic drafts, that's the most important number in March. I'd like three fields, one click each: used as is, edited, or discarded; a reason when discarded; and the category change, which the system records by itself.

**Samir:** And who sees those fields? If Michal gets a list that says "Ahmad discarded 40 drafts", Ahmad looks bad, but maybe Ahmad is the only one reading them.

**You:** That's exactly the risk. What would make it safe?

**Samir:** Per language, per week. Never per person. And not in the yearly review.

**You:** I'll write that into the rule of use: override logs are reported per language and per week, never per agent, and not used in performance reviews. And the line I'd like the team to hear from you: "an override is information, not a mistake".

**Samir:** They'll believe it from me more than from you. No offence.

**You:** None taken; that's why it's yours. One more: the stop rules. If two weekly samples in a row show more wrong replies than today, that language goes off. If an agent sends a draft that tells a customer another customer's address, everything goes off the same day. Is that too strict?

**Samir:** No. If that happens I'd switch it off anyway. Better it's written, so nobody says I panicked.

**You:** And the holdout: one ticket in ten, chosen at random, gets no draft until April, so we can show Ronit what the drafts really changed. The team will know it exists.

**Samir:** One in ten is fine. Ronit will like it.

**You:** Last thing. What are you most worried about, that this document doesn't cover?

**Samir:** That in April someone takes the hours and says "so you need two people less". The temps, fine, we agreed that with Michal. My permanent people, no.

**You:** The charter says the pilot's hours go to the peak without temps, and the hypothesis is written that way. Headcount is not in the scope. Michal signs it.

**Samir:** Then I sign too.

---

## What to notice

- Ownership first, then the three rule-of-use questions in order, each answered with something that can be built (a setting, two named people, three fields).
- Samir's fear about the override logs was found by asking "what would make it safe?", not by reassuring him; his condition became a line in the charter.
- The interviewer never asked what Samir wants to happen to the holdout tickets if the peak queue explodes. Samir would want the right to end the holdout early. That is a reasonable condition, and the gate report must then say the effect is directional, not attributed.
