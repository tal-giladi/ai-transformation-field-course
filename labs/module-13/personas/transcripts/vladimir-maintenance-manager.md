# Transcript — Vladimir (Vladi) Kaplan (maintenance manager, Plant K)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 13.2. Sunday 17 January 2027, 10:00, the maintenance office, 60 minutes, with interruptions from the radio. The interviewer is the operations-excellence manager. Translated from Hebrew, with Vladi's Russian left in.*

---

**Vladi:** So. The computer will fix the machines now?

**Interviewer:** I hope not. I want to understand how maintenance really works here before anyone buys anything. Can we start with last week? What broke?

**Vladi:** K07 again, hydraulic leak. K15, proportional valve, E-217. Two hot runners on the caps moulds at night. Normal week.

**Interviewer:** How do you decide what to check in a PM?

**Vladi:** The checklist in MaintPro. And experience.

**Interviewer:** The work orders often say only "fixed". Why?

**Vladi:** Because they write at the end of the shift, on the computer in the office. Three in the morning, a man has been alone with 26 machines. You want literature?

**Interviewer:** And the failure codes?

**Vladi:** *(snorts)* A consultant wrote them in 2019. Code 99 is half of everything. "Pump." Pump what? Bearing, seal, coupling? Nobody checks the code. Nobody ever used it for anything.

**Interviewer:** If we wanted to know which machines fail most, and how, where would we look?

**Vladi:** Ask me.

**Interviewer:** Fair. Where do we lose the most time?

**Vladi:** Moulds at night. A hot runner tip goes at one o'clock, tooling comes at half past seven. The machine is fine, it stands six hours. No sensor on the machine sees a hot runner tip.

**Interviewer:** And the three Kessler machines with condition monitoring?

**Vladi:** Since October. The screen shows vibration; nobody looks. K22's pump bearing went on the second of December. Afterwards I looked at the history: it was climbing for weeks. Nobody had set an alarm. *Nu*, now there is one, I set it.

**Interviewer:** How often do the night technicians call you?

**Vladi:** Twice a week, sometimes three. The rota is on the board, but they call me.

**Interviewer:** What would help them most?

**Vladi:** The manuals in a language they read. The parts list that is true; the one on the drive is from before the warehouse moved. And not dashboards. Please, no dashboards.

**Interviewer:** If the company started recording failures properly, what would it need from your side?

**Vladi:** A short list that makes sense. Ten, fifteen codes, not forty. A screen at the machine, not in the office. In Arabic and Russian also. And someone who reads it, otherwise it's again for the auditors.

**Interviewer:** Would you help write that list?

**Vladi:** If you don't make it a committee of consultants. Yes.
