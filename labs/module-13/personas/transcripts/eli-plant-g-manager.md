# Transcript — Eli Peretz (Plant G manager)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 13.2. Wednesday 20 January 2027, 09:30, Plant G meeting room and the maintenance corner, 50 minutes. The interviewer is the operations-excellence manager. Translated from Hebrew.*

**Eli:** Welcome to Galil! We're ready. Let's start the pilot here.

**Interviewer:** Thanks. I want to understand your maintenance data first. Can you show me the log?

**Interviewer:** The deck says Galil repairs in 3.5 hours on average, Plant K in 5.7. What does "ready" mean in your log? What has happened on the machine at that moment?

**Interviewer:** How long does that take, usually?

**Interviewer:** At Plant K the stop in ProdTrack ends when production restarts with approved parts. So the two numbers in the deck measure different things.

**Eli:** *(pause)* Ah. So we're not faster.

**Interviewer:** I don't know yet. I'll compare them on the same definition and show you before anyone else sees it.

**Eli:** OK. That's fair. Don't make Galil the bad guy.

**Interviewer:** I won't. The log says "machine 5" or "M5". ProdTrack says G05. Are they the same machine?

**Eli:** Mostly. In 2025 we rearranged the hall when the new lines came. The numbers on the floor stayed; the integrator installed ProdTrack after. A few might be different. Shlomi knows.

**Interviewer:** I'll check against the serial numbers. And the SCADA alarms?

**Eli:** From the old owners. Alarms only. Nobody looks unless something breaks.

**Interviewer:** You filled Predictiva's questionnaire as "ready" for all items. What did you mean by ready?

**Eli:** That we have the data. We have a log, we have the SCADA. We can start tomorrow.

**Interviewer:** And failure modes coded?

**Eli:** It's written in the "fault" column. You can read what broke.

**Interviewer:** Last question: what would help your technicians most?
