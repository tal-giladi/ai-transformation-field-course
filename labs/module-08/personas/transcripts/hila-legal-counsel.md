# Transcript — Adv. Hila Rosen (legal counsel)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 08.4. Monday 30 November 2026, 10:00, Hila's office, 45 minutes. The
interviewer brought the returns page, section 9 of the terms of sale, the Trail Club terms, the
warehouse SOP, three bot transcripts and the Verbalo order form. Read it as if you were sitting in: note
what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Interviewer:** Thank you for the time. I'm not asking you for an opinion on the spot. I'd like to
understand what's already known, what you need from the business, and where the decisions sit. Orly
said I may ask about your March memo.

**Hila:** She told me. Yes. On 24 March I sent Orly and Boris a memo: the returns page, store credit
only, ten percent handling, thirty-nine shekels, unopened only, doesn't match section 9 of our own terms
or the law's rules on refunds and fees. I recommended aligning the page and the warehouse SOP with the
terms. It's been "awaiting business decision" since April.

**Interviewer:** What does section 9 reflect, in plain terms?

**Hila:** The Consumer Protection Law's rules for cancelling a distance sale, as Northwind applies them.
Fourteen days from receipt. Money back within fourteen days of the notice. A fee of five percent or a
hundred shekels, whichever is lower. No fee for a defect, a mismatch or late delivery, and then we
collect. Seniors, people with disabilities and new immigrants: four months from the purchase if it was
made in a conversation with us. Made-to-order goods are excluded.

**Interviewer:** The bot is loaded with the returns page, the club terms and the terms of sale. In these
transcripts it tells one customer "store credit only", another "30 days, full cash refund". How do you
see that?

**Hila:** As Northwind talking to a consumer about cancellation terms. Whether a particular answer is
misleading is something I'd assess case by case. I won't give you a general opinion.

**Interviewer:** Fair. Do you know Moffatt v. Air Canada?

**Hila:** Yes. British Columbia, February 2024. The airline's chatbot gave a wrong answer on bereavement
fares; the airline argued the chatbot was responsible for its own words and lost. It's a Canadian
small-claims tribunal, it binds nobody here. But the question it asks is the obvious one: who is the
business speaking through? Our bot is us.

**Interviewer:** Who reviewed the Verbalo contract?

**Hila:** Nobody in legal. I learned it existed from the Authority's letter in August about the
thirty-day promise. I have concerns: section 7, they can train on our conversation data unless we opt
out. Section 6, liability capped at three months' fees, and they exclude answers built from our content.
The renewal notice is due by 1 April.

**Interviewer:** The claims log shows nine Authority inquiries this year. Are they mostly the same issue?

**Hila:** Mostly. Store credit instead of money, fees above five percent, refunds later than fourteen
days. One about the bot's thirty days. Four small claims; one asks for ten thousand including punitive
damages.

**Interviewer:** If Northwind changed the bot tomorrow so it never answers returns questions and hands
them to an agent, would that remove the problem?

**Hila:** It would remove one voice. The agents use the same page and the same macros. The problem is
the policy, not the channel.

**Interviewer:** The bot's conversations go to Verbalo's servers in Ireland. Is that something you or
the DPO would need to look at?

**Hila:** Yes. Since Amendment 13 the Privacy Protection Authority can impose much larger sanctions, so
"we never looked" is not an answer. I'd want to see what data goes in, the processing terms and the
opt-out. That's a review I haven't been able to do.

**Interviewer:** Which should come first, the policy or the contract?

**Hila:** That's a business decision. I'll tell you the exposure on each; Orly and Gil decide the order.

**Interviewer:** One more about the policy: some customers say they opened the box only to look. Does
opening it end the right to cancel?

**Hila:** Not by itself, in my view, for ordinary goods. Used is a different question. I'd want to word
that carefully in any policy; flag it for me.

**Interviewer:** What would you need from the business to move on the returns policy?

**Hila:** A decision on the business choices: whether club members keep thirty days, whether we pay
return shipping, whether store credit stays as an option. Then a draft I can mark up. And a date.

**Interviewer:** If I draft a single policy from section 9, with the business choices flagged, would you
review it?

**Hila:** Yes. Two weeks. Flag every place you made a business choice; I'll mark what's law.

**Interviewer:** And the demand letter in the claims log?

**Hila:** Threatened class action: fees above the cap and store credit instead of refunds, 2019 to 2026.
External counsel's early view is that the exposure is meaningful. Fixing the policy now would help.
That's all I'll say on it.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. I'll send the draft by 7 December with every business choice marked.
