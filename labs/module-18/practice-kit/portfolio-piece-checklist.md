# Portfolio piece checklist (lesson 18.5)

Use this for each engagement you turn into a portfolio piece: the ten course engagements, the capstone, and any real engagement you are allowed to show.

## 1. What the piece contains (two to four pages)

- [ ] **The situation in five lines:** organization type and size, the stated ask, who brought you in, the time box.
- [ ] **What you found:** the two or three findings that changed the recommendation, each with the evidence type (measured, reported, directional).
- [ ] **What you recommended, and against:** one recommendation, the recommend-against, the money.
- [ ] **One artifact excerpt:** a page of the readiness assessment, the eval report's results table, or the decision-ready case's front page.
- [ ] **What happened, or what would decide it:** the decision record's gates and re-decision date.
- [ ] **What you would do differently:** one honest line, ideally from your self-score against the expert.

## 2. Labelling

- [ ] Course engagements are labelled: "Training engagement on a synthetic organization (AI Transformation in the Field). All organizations, people and data are fictional." Never present them as client work.
- [ ] Real engagements: "Real engagement, anonymised and approved for use by [role] on [date]."

## 3. Anonymising a real engagement

- [ ] No organization name, logo, product name, location detail or unusual fact that identifies it (a sector plus a size plus a city can be enough).
- [ ] No names or roles that point to one person ("the CFO of a 300-person company in Ashdod" is a person).
- [ ] Numbers changed by a stated method (rounded, indexed to 100, or replaced by synthetic numbers with the same shape); ratios may stay if approved.
- [ ] No screenshots of real systems; redraw tables.
- [ ] No text that was confidential to the organization: prices, contract terms, internal plans, unreleased products.
- [ ] Personal data: none. Not even "anonymised" examples of real tickets or messages; write synthetic ones.
- [ ] The organization's approver read the final piece and signed off in writing.

## 4. Before you show it

- [ ] Someone who does not know the engagement read it and could not guess the organization.
- [ ] The piece answers "what did you do?", not "what did the team do?"; your role is stated honestly.
- [ ] Every number in it is labelled the way you labelled it in the engagement.
