# Harbor & Pine — data room

Harbor & Pine Ltd. imports furniture and home goods from Vietnam, China, Turkey and Italy and sells
them online, in two showrooms (Tel Aviv, Haifa) and to hotels and offices. About 120 employees, ₪96M
revenue. You joined on Monday 2 November 2026 as Head of AI and Operations Improvement, reporting to
the COO. This folder is what the company gave you access to in your first weeks.

Everything here is fictional and synthetic: the company, the people, the customers, the vendors. Data
exports cover 1 May – 31 October 2026 unless a file says otherwise. Every Stage 1 lesson (modules
1–7) uses some of it; the table says which.

## How to open

**Spreadsheet (Track A).** The CSV files are UTF-8 and contain Hebrew, Arabic and Russian text. In
Excel use *Data → From Text/CSV* and pick *65001: Unicode (UTF-8)*; Google Sheets and LibreOffice
detect it on import. The bigger files (tickets, orders, phone log, proxy log) open fine in any
spreadsheet; use filters and pivot tables. Markdown (`.md`) files open in any text editor or in the
course viewer.

**Python (Track B).** From a lab script, use the field kit:

```python
from fieldkit.data import data_room, load
room = data_room("harbor-and-pine")
tickets = load(room / "support" / "tickets.csv")   # *_at and *_date columns parsed as Israel time
```

**Times.** Timestamps are Israel local time with the UTC offset (`+03:00` in summer, `+02:00` from 25
October 2026, when daylight saving time ended). The phone system exports UTC (the `Z` at the end).
Some hand-kept files use day/month dates in several formats, as people really type them.

**Rebuilding.** The data files were generated by `generate.py` (Python 3.12+, standard library only,
with the text fragments in `textbank.py`). You do not need to run it; if you do, `python generate.py`
rebuilds the same files byte for byte. Reading the generator shows how the company was simulated and
spoils the exercises: finish Stage 1 first.
