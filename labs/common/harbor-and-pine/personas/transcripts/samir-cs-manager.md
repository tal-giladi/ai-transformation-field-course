# Transcript — Samir Haddad (Customer Service Manager)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 02.2. Monday 10 November 2026, 14:00, a meeting room next to the support
floor, 45 minutes. The interviewer is the new Head of AI and Operations Improvement. Read it as if you
were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Interviewer:** Thanks for the time, Samir. I know the team is busy. Before anything else: I'm not
here to count heads. I'm trying to understand how the work really happens. Nothing you tell me will be
quoted with your name on it. If I write something up, it's "the support team said", and you'll see it
first.

**Samir:** OK. *(pause)* People say a lot of things about why you're here.

**Interviewer:** I heard. I'd rather you judge me by what I do with what you tell me. Can you describe
the team?

**Samir:** Fourteen with me. Olga is the senior, she trains everybody. Rina does claims and returns. Eleven
agents. Russian: Olga, Yana, Sergei. Arabic: Lina, Rami, Ahmad, and me if needed. Daniel for English.
Everybody does Hebrew.

**Interviewer:** And the channels?

**Samir:** Email and the web contact form in Deskly, the phone in CloudVoice. Sunday to Thursday eight to
six, Friday morning a small shift.

**Interviewer:** How has the volume changed this year?

**Samir:** It doubled since the spring. May was around 270 tickets, October about 570. Plus the phone.

**Interviewer:** Could you walk me through a normal day? Say, last Tuesday. From the moment you open the
queue.

**Samir:** *(leans back)* Eight o'clock, I open Deskly. The night queue is full. Most of it is the same
question: where is my order. "You promised the 14th, today is the 20th." The agents send the macro — the
saved reply — and the macro gives the date from the order. Which already passed. So the customer writes
again, angrier. The good agents don't send the macro, they email logistics and wait. Logistics answers
when they answer. Meanwhile the phone rings with the same question.

**Interviewer:** How much of the queue is "where is my order"?

**Samir:** My feeling? A third of everything. Since July, maybe half.

**Interviewer:** Is that from a report, or from what you see?

**Samir:** From what I see. The report can't tell you. We changed the categories in July, "Delivery"
became "Delivery status" and "Delivery damage", and agents in a hurry put half of it in "Other".

**Interviewer:** Why do you think it's grown since July?

**Samir:** Because the containers are late. Vietnam, China. The website gives the customer a date when
they buy, and nobody changes it when the ship is late. Tamar knows the real dates. We don't. I'm not
blaming logistics — it's not Tamar's fault the ships are late. But the customer hears nothing until he
writes to us.

**Interviewer:** That's very helpful. What happens to the customer after the agent replies?

**Samir:** The agent sends the answer and closes the ticket. If the customer writes again, it's a new
ticket.

**Interviewer:** The KPI report says the average first response is 2 hours. How is that measured?

**Samir:** *(smiles)* You read the report. Business hours, Sunday to Thursday, eight to five. Email only.
Tickets that arrive on business days.

**Interviewer:** So the web form isn't in it?

**Samir:** No. The web form is a separate view, the agents check it a few times a day. And weekends — a
customer writes Friday night, we answer Sunday morning. That's not our fault, so I took it out in 2024.

**Interviewer:** How long does a customer really wait, then?

**Samir:** Longer. From the web form, a lot longer. I know.

**Interviewer:** Thanks for being straight about that. How do damage claims work?

**Samir:** Agent sends the photo request. Rina opens a claim in her sheet. She decides with me, she
arranges the replacement or the credit.

**Interviewer:** And the phone — how many calls?

**Samir:** About 17 a day on average in opening hours, much more on bad days. About 15% hang up before
we answer.

**Interviewer:** The Russian and Arabic tickets — do they take longer?

**Samir:** Yes. Fewer people, and a careful reply in Russian or Arabic takes time. Olga is fast. Very fast.

**Interviewer:** How does she do it?

**Samir:** *(pause)* She's good. Ask her.

**Interviewer:** I will. What would you change first, if you could?

**Samir:** The date. If the customer knew the real date before he writes to us, half my queue goes away.
And then — I'm afraid of that too — somebody will say "so you need fewer people".

**Interviewer:** I understand why that worries you. I can't promise outcomes I don't control, but I can
promise you'll see the numbers before anyone else does, and that I'll put on the table what the team
does with the time, not just the cost.

**Samir:** That's more than I expected. Talk to Tamar before you talk about my team's cost.

**Interviewer:** She's on my list for this week. Can I sit with two agents for an hour each, to watch
the queue?

**Samir:** After the peak would be better. But one hour with Olga and one with Hila, this week, fine.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Samir.

**Interviewer:** [end interview]

**Samir:** — end of interview —
