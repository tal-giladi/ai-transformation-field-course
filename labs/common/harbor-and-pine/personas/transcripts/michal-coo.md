*Pre-run interview for lesson 01.2. Monday 2 November 2026, 09:05, Michal's office, 30 minutes. The
interviewer is the new Head of AI and Operations Improvement, on their first morning. Read it as if you

understand what you need from me, not to tell you what I plan to do. Is that all right?

**Interviewer:** In your own words: why did you create this role, and why now?

**Interviewer:** Let me read that back. Success on 28 January is: one thing already working, a

Did I miss anything?

**Michal:** No. That's it. Write it down exactly like that.

**Interviewer:** I will. You said "one thing that already works". Does it have to be the chatbot?

**Michal:** *(pauses)* What do you think the chatbot is for?

**Interviewer:** I'd guess fewer angry customers and a support cost that stops climbing. But I'd rather

**Interviewer:** And before January, this year's money?

**Interviewer:** That's useful. What would make this fail, in your view?

**Michal:** An expensive bot that makes customers angrier. Or a war between Yoav and the operations
people where I'm the referee.

**Interviewer:** What worries you most right now?

**Interviewer:** If the evidence shows a smaller saving than 40%, what then?

**Michal:** I'd rather go to the board with a true smaller number than a big wrong one. But — as I

**Interviewer:** Timing: we're entering peak season. How does that affect what I can ask of people?

**Michal:** November to January everyone is at full stretch. Interviews, fine, keep them short.
Workshops with the whole support team, not before February.

**Interviewer:** Last thing. What do you want from me, and by when?

**Interviewer:** Thursday, one page. And I'll send you today a written version of the objective, the
