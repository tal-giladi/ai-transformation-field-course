# Transcript — Ronit Avraham (CFO)

*Pre-run interview for lessons 03.5–03.7. Tuesday 17 November 2026, 09:00, Ronit's office, 30 minutes.
The interviewer is the new Head of AI and Operations Improvement, preparing the ROI model. Read it as if
you were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Ronit:** Good morning. Michal said you want to talk about the business case.

**Interviewer:** Yes. Before I build anything I want to understand how you judge a case, so I build it
in your terms, not mine.

**Ronit:** That's a good start. The template is on the finance drive. Costs by year, one-off and running.
Benefits by year. Three scenarios. NPV and payback.

**Interviewer:** What rate do you discount at?

**Ronit:** Twelve percent. Payback preferably within 24 months.

**Interviewer:** And approval levels?

**Ronit:** Up to fifty thousand, the department head and me. Fifty to a hundred and fifty, the CEO.
Above a hundred and fifty over three years, the board.

**Interviewer:** The support cost — the board heard 3.4 million. What's in it?

**Ronit:** FY2025. Salaries and employer costs about 3.02 million for 14 people. Helpdesk and phone
licences about 110 thousand. Space and IT allocation about 270 thousand. Fully loaded, 3.4.

**Interviewer:** If a project frees agent time, how do you count it?

**Ronit:** I don't, until there's a plan for the roles. Attrition, redeployment, or a decision — with
dates. Freed capacity is not cash.

**Interviewer:** That's clear. What about costs that move to other teams? For example, if logistics has
to do something new.

**Ronit:** Then it's a cost in their budget and it goes in the case. Most cases forget that.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything about timing I should know?

**Ronit:** Q1 cash is the weakest. After the winter sale. Big payments in February or March are hard.
And use 3.70 shekels to the dollar.

**Interviewer:** I noticed a line in the finance summary: a write-off of capitalised software in 2023, 860
thousand. What was that?

**Ronit:** *(puts down her pen)* The CRM project. 2022. The vendor promised a 360-degree view of the
customer and 20% more repeat sales. It cost about 1.1 million. The showrooms never used it properly. We
wrote off 860 thousand in 2023.

**Interviewer:** How was it judged at the time?

**Ronit:** On the vendor's numbers, in our template. Nobody owned the benefit. No stages. The board asked
me how I approved it. *(pause)* So now I want money released in stages, with conditions written before
we start, and benefits that land in somebody's budget.

**Interviewer:** That helps a lot. So a case that would get your support looks like…?

**Ronit:** A first stage that's cheap — under fifty thousand — with a condition I can check. If it
passes, the next stage. And no vendor benchmark as a benefit. Your own numbers, or nothing.

**Interviewer:** The CEO's 40% came from a vendor.

**Ronit:** I know. I was in the room.

**Interviewer:** If I come back with a smaller number that holds, will you stand behind it with the board?

**Ronit:** If I can audit it, yes. I'd rather defend a small true number than a big one.

**Interviewer:** Last question. What would make you say no immediately?

**Ronit:** Savings counted as cash on day one. A licence signed before a test on our own data. And
anything that needs a big payment in March.

**Interviewer:** Thank you. I'll bring a draft in your template next week.

**Ronit:** Bring the assumptions on a separate page. I read those first.

**Interviewer:** [end interview]

**Ronit:** — end of interview —
