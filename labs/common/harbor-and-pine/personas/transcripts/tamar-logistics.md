# Transcript — Tamar Shalev (Logistics Manager)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 02.2. Thursday 13 November 2026, 08:30, Tamar's office in the warehouse,
25 minutes. The interviewer is the new Head of AI and Operations Improvement. Read it as if you were
sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Tamar:** I have a container in customs and twenty minutes. What do you need?

**Interviewer:** Twenty minutes is fine. I'm trying to understand how an order gets from the factory to
the customer. Can you walk me through it?

**Tamar:** Purchasing orders from the factory. The factory loads a container. Gal-Ship, our forwarder,
books it. Vietnam and China every week, Turkey and Italy every two weeks. Haifa or Ashdod. It lands,
customs one to three days, warehouse one or two. My delivery coordinators call the customer and book a
slot. The delivery company brings it.

**Interviewer:** How do you know when a container will arrive?

**Tamar:** Gal-Ship. Their portal exports a file once a week. When an ETA changes they email me.

**Interviewer:** And you keep track of that where?

**Tamar:** My sheet. Shared drive, Logistics, Containers 2026. By container: original ETA, latest ETA,
when it was updated, arrived or not, notes. I update it twice a week.

**Interviewer:** Could I see it?

**Tamar:** *(turns the screen)* There.

**Interviewer:** It's in Hebrew, with dates in a few different formats.

**Tamar:** It's mine. I know what it says.

**Interviewer:** Fair enough. What does it say about this year?

**Tamar:** Since June, Vietnam and China are late. Almost every container. Forty of the last forty-one.
About two weeks on average — some five days, one twenty-three. Turkey and Italy are fine.

**Interviewer:** Why?

**Tamar:** Routes, connections, port congestion. Read the notes. It's not something I can fix.

**Interviewer:** I'm not suggesting you can. I've heard from Customer Service that your team gets a lot
of emails from them.

**Tamar:** *(sighs)* A hundred and eighty-seven between July and September. One order at a time. Itai
spends half his day answering. I told Samir to look at the sheet. He says his people can't search it by
order.

**Interviewer:** Can they?

**Tamar:** The container is on the order line in Priority, when Purchasing fills it in. Almost always. So
they look up the order, take the container, look in my sheet. It's two steps.

**Interviewer:** When an ETA changes, where does that update go after it reaches you?

**Tamar:** *(looks at the interviewer)* Into my sheet. That's it.

**Interviewer:** Nobody else gets it?

**Tamar:** Nobody asked. Purchasing sees the portal. Customer Service asks when the customer shouts. The
customer doesn't hear anything, the website still shows the old date. And then everybody says "logistics
is late". The ship is late. The date was a promise nobody checked.

**Interviewer:** So the customer's date never changes when the container's date changes.

**Tamar:** Never.

**Interviewer:** That's a big finding. How many orders are affected at any moment, roughly?

**Tamar:** Every order on a late container. A container is fifteen, twenty, thirty orders. You can count
it in Priority.

**Interviewer:** I will. What do you need from this program, if anything?

**Tamar:** Customer Service off my back. And more hands in the warehouse for the peak, but that's not
your department.

**Interviewer:** If the ETA updates went straight to the customers, would that take load off your team?

**Tamar:** Itai's emails would stop. Yes.

**Interviewer:** Last question. Is there anything about this you think I should know and haven't asked?

**Tamar:** You asked the right one already. The rest — come back after the peak.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, Tamar. I'll send you what I write before anyone else sees it.

**Tamar:** Good.

**Interviewer:** [end interview]

**Tamar:** — end of interview —
