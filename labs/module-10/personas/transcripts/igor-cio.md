# Transcript — Igor Shapiro (CIO)

*Pre-run interview for lesson 10.1. Sunday 15 November 2026, 09:00, Igor's office in Karmiel, one hour.
The interviewer had read the brief, the board minute, the demo-day scorecards and Igor's draft RFP. Read
it as if you were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they missed, and which questions
worked.*

---

**Interviewer:** Thanks for this. Before we talk about vendors, I'd like to read back what you asked me to
do and make sure I have it in your words. Then the practical things: who decides what, and when we can
stop.

**Igor:** Go.

**Interviewer:** "A recommendation, backed by evidence from Galil's own conditions, on which vendor Galil
should contract with, on what terms, and how it should be piloted with clinicians before any network-wide
rollout." Is that it?

**Igor:** That's it. Add "that IT can actually integrate". Otherwise you'll recommend something beautiful
that needs a year of Notara work.

**Interviewer:** Added. And the CEO's version, "an ambient scribe for every doctor by Q2", I'd like to write
as the claim we're testing, not as the objective. Is that something you can live with?

**Igor:** I can. Shlomit will hear "testing" as "delaying". You'll have to handle that.

**Interviewer:** Understood. Who decides what? I'd like to write it down.

**Igor:** Clinical safety is Wasim's. Anything with patient data is Hagit's, she's also our privacy
officer. Money above three hundred thousand goes to the board, below that Shlomit. Which vendor: in the
end, Shlomit, with the steering group.

**Interviewer:** And integration?

**Igor:** Me. Three regions on Notara 9.2: Western Galilee, Karmiel–Misgav, Nazareth. Upper Galilee and
Kinneret are on 7.4. Upgrading them is about four hundred and twenty thousand and four months, not
budgeted. MedNote doesn't run on 7.4 at all.

**Interviewer:** And an outside vendor?

**Igor:** Through Notara's interface. Notara charges for interface projects. For something like Rishma,
they estimated three months and around a hundred and sixty thousand.

**Interviewer:** That's important for the RFP. Your draft asks for "accuracy %". What would you want it to
ask?

**Igor:** Honestly, I wrote it in an evening. I want integration with both versions, a real price for
three years, and something that tells Wasim what happens when it's wrong.

**Interviewer:** Has Galil tried anything like this before? Dictation, voice, anything?

**Igor:** ... Yes. In March last year we tried MedNote Dictation. Nine doctors in Nahariya and Nof
HaGalil, twelve weeks. It died. By week seven two doctors were still using it.

**Interviewer:** Why did it die?

**Igor:** Correcting took longer than typing, and there were two near misses: a drug name wrong, and
something a daughter said that wasn't in the summary. Wasim knows the details. I closed it quietly. I
should have written it up.

**Interviewer:** Is the usage data still there?

**Igor:** It's in the data room already, the weekly export per doctor. I put it there without a word of
explanation. That was cowardly.

**Interviewer:** Thank you, that helps more than you think. What can I get on documentation time? The KPI
report says "not measured".

**Igor:** Notara's audit log knows when the note window was active and when the note was signed. Nobody
has ever used it for this. I can give you an export, de-identified, a sample of visits for September and
October, and a count of visits per doctor per day.

**Interviewer:** Perfect. Exits: phase 1 ends on 3 December. If the evidence says "don't buy anything
now", is that an acceptable outcome for you?

**Igor:** For me, yes. For Shlomit, you'll need a very good sentence.

**Interviewer:** Then I'll ask for twenty minutes with her before the readout, not after. Change control
through you, in writing?

**Igor:** Through me.

**Interviewer:** Last one: the proof of concept. Can you fund it?

**Igor:** Up to seventy-five thousand on my signature. Wasim's people will want to be involved.
