# Transcript — Adv. Hagit Peretz (legal counsel and DPO)

*Pre-run interview, used in lessons 10.4 and 10.5. Tuesday 17 November 2026, 11:00, Hagit's office in
Karmiel, 45 minutes. The interviewer brought the four vendors' data documents, the ScribeWave quote and
Igor's draft RFP. Read it as if you were sitting in: note what the interviewer found out, what they
missed, and which questions worked.*

---

**Interviewer:** Thank you. I'm not asking for an opinion on any vendor today. I'd like to understand
which rules you think are in play, what you need from the selection process, and by when.

**Hagit:** Good. Because nobody has sent me a single data-processing agreement.

**Interviewer:** I've brought what the vendors left behind. Before that: which rules do you have in mind?

**Hagit:** My view, not a ruling. Health information is information of special sensitivity under the
Privacy Protection Law, since Amendment 13. Our patient databases fall under the Data Security
Regulations; before an outside party gets access, we assess the risks and the contract needs specific
terms, regulation 15. Medical confidentiality under the Patient's Rights Law binds us. And the Ministry of
Health's cloud circular, 2/2021, applies to clinics.

**Interviewer:** What does the circular ask of you in practice?

**Hagit:** A cloud committee, a risk assessment per application, minimum contract terms, and reporting
new cloud uses to the Ministry's information-security officer.

**Interviewer:** How does that work at Galil today?

**Hagit:** On paper there's a committee from 2022. It met once. The policy is still a draft. Nothing has
been reported. That will have to be fixed before this, not after.

**Interviewer:** Noted. Is there anything outside Galil that gives someone else a say?

**Hagit:** Yes. The health funds. Fund A's agreement, Appendix D: any outside party that processes its
members' medical information needs Fund A's prior written approval. They have forty-five days to answer.
And processing in Israel unless they approve otherwise. Fund B wants thirty days' notice.

**Interviewer:** So signing by the thirty-first of December...

**Hagit:** Nobody signs anything that processes Fund A members' data before Fund A says yes. That alone
rules out the thirty-first.

**Interviewer:** What else should be a must-have in the RFP?

**Hagit:** No training on our data, de-identified included. Audio deleted as soon as it's no longer
needed. Location in Israel or where I approve in writing. The full sub-processor list with notice and a
right to object. Exit with deletion. Incident reporting to us.

**Interviewer:** And the recording itself?

**Hagit:** The doctor is a party to the conversation. Patients must still be told, clearly, in their
language. The wording is mine and Wasim's, not a vendor's default.

**Interviewer:** Is the audio part of the medical record?

**Hagit:** That's the question I haven't answered. The record is what the clinician documents. Is the
recording part of it, or a working file we can delete once the note is signed? Don't build anything that
assumes either answer. I want external counsel on it.

**Interviewer:** One more thing: is a scribe a medical device?

**Hagit:** Depends on what it's for. A transcript is one thing; a tool that suggests diagnoses is another.
I'd want each vendor's regulatory position in writing, and I'd check with the Ministry's medical devices
division if needed.

**Interviewer:** What do you need from me, and when?

**Hagit:** Every vendor's DPA and sub-processor list as soon as the RFP answers arrive. A short list of
which vendor refuses what. Two weeks for me after that. And I'll need to start Fund A's approval request
the moment we have a name.
