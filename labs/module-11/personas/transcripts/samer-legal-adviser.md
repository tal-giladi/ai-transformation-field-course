# Transcript — Adv. Samer Haddad (legal adviser)

*Pre-run meeting for lesson 11.4. Monday 21 December 2026, 10:00, the legal adviser's office, 55
minutes. The interviewer is the Head of Digital Services and sent five written questions the day before.
Translated from Hebrew.*

---

**Samer:** Let me guess. The mayor wants it by the conference.

**Interviewer:** He wants something by the conference. I sent you five questions so you can answer what
you can and tell me what you'll put in writing. I'm not asking you to approve a chatbot.

**Samer:** Good. Then first question: the procurement route.

**Interviewer:** Yes. If we test an assistant ourselves, on our own Microsoft tenant, with a small
purchase of model usage below the regulation 3(3) amount, is that a tender question?

**Samer:** My preliminary view, not an opinion: a purchase below the threshold needs price quotes under
our procedure, not a tender. Check the current indexed amount with the treasurer; it moves every month.
If it grows into a service residents use, that's a different contract and probably a tender.

**Interviewer:** Second: CivicTalk. Here is their proposal. Free for 90 days, then ₪14,500 a month for
36 months, auto-renewal.

**Samer:** *(reads)* That is not free. Thirty-six paid months is ₪522,000 before VAT, and it renews. That's
far above the tender amounts. Who agreed to this?

**Interviewer:** As far as I know, nobody signed. Eyal wrote "go ahead with the free pilot" in October,
and they crawled our website.

**Samer:** Then two problems. Nobody but the mayor and the treasurer can commit the city, so nothing is
agreed; we tell them so in writing. And a supplier who gets our data and a head start before a tender
creates an equal-treatment problem for the tender. I want the emails.

**Interviewer:** And their claim that they're in a framework, so no tender?

**Samer:** Show me the framework and its scope. A framework for forms doesn't buy a chatbot.

**Interviewer:** Third: languages. If we launch anything public, does it have to work in Arabic?

**Samer:** Hebrew is the state language, Arabic has a special status under the Basic Law of 2018, and the
Supreme Court required Arabic on municipal signs in mixed cities in 2002. I'm not telling you the law
requires a chatbot in Arabic. I'm telling you I won't sign off on a service "in every language" that
serves Arabic speakers worse than Hebrew speakers in our city.

**Interviewer:** Fourth: accessibility.

**Samer:** Regulation 35: the site at level AA, the statement accurate. Anything you embed is part of
the site. Our statement says 2021 and "everything is accessible". Fix the statement too.

**Interviewer:** Fifth: transparency. I'd like to publish a register entry: what the assistant does,
on what sources, who owns it, how to complain.

**Samer:** I like that. Send me a draft. While we're on transparency: in September we answered an FOI
request that we use no AI. Is that still true?

**Interviewer:** No. There's a chatbot on the business-licensing pages since March, on a free platform.
And the parking cameras read plates.

**Samer:** *(pause)* Then we correct the answer. In writing, this month. Send me what you know by
Thursday.

**Interviewer:** I will. What will you put in writing, and when?

**Samer:** A note on CivicTalk this week. A note on the FOI correction next week. Comments on your
register draft within five working days of receiving it.
