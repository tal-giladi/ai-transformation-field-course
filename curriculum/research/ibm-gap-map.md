## What IBM gives (re-use as vocabulary, don't re-teach)

IBM is a judgment course. Every exercise is one decision on one pre-written case card, from the seat
of a C-suite leader in a ~50,000-person multinational, with every number already measured. The
vocabulary is good and our lessons name it in one line where they build on it:

- Literacy: predict vs generate vs act, AI-fit score (5 criteria), consequence-if-wrong, binding
  constraint, AI-ready ≠ BI-ready, Responsible-AI Gate.
- Strategy: north star, bet vs experiment, value–feasibility–risk sort, sequence for absorption,
  $X problem, TCO iceberg, conservative case first, cost of doing nothing.
- Operating model: operating-model drift, paved roads, delegation ladder, single accountable owner,
  build/buy/partner, hub constants vs spoke variables, fusion team.
- Governance: oversight postures ("the ladder"), designed vs effective posture, unearned green,
  control-credibility test, capacity screen, auditable claim card, actor–source–date check,
  recoverability sweep, last reversible moment, pass/flag/block gate, ship / ship with conditions / wait.
- Change: licence ≠ adoption ≠ value, go-live is not the goal, train → transfer → sustain,
  qualified-use rate, opportunity-aware population, Goodhart on the dashboard.
- Value: net the claim, capacity vs cash, credible counterfactual, goal–driver–indicator chain,
  pilot isn't scale, non-compensable gates, scale / sustain / retire, proportionate monitoring.
