## The company and the seat

- **Sample Global Enterprise (SGE)**: ~50,000 employees, ~20 countries, revenue in the low tens of
  billions USD. Three BU groups: Customer & Commercial; Operations & Supply Chain; Corporate &
  Shared Services. IT and data are **federated** (each group has its own delivery and data estate;
  central function sets standards) — the stated reason pilots don't transfer.
- **Learner = Elena Marsh, Chief AI & Transformation Officer**, reporting to the CEO, writing to CEO
  and board. Named executives: David Osei (EVP Customer & Commercial Ops), Amara Njoku (Chief Risk &
  Compliance Officer, with Legal), Sofia Alvarez (Chief People Officer). All other people by role.
- **Commissioning question:** "what is the return on our AI investment?" — leadership could show
  activity, not value.
- GenAI arrived bottom-up. Workforce: 45% novice, 35% experimenter, 15% proficient, 5% expert; 9%
  completed role-based enablement; 21% of leaders completed leadership enablement; 3 of 41 role
  families have a "good use" statement.
- Six board measures: pilots reaching scale 2 of 18; value $2.3M documented vs $9.75M reported by
  BUs; median 14 months to documented value, 19 to production; 3 consequential-decision systems, 1
  with a named owner, 22% of 506,800 consequential decisions/yr documented-reviewed; fluency 9%;
  adoption depth as above. Regulatory: EU, UK, US; GDPR, US state privacy laws, EU AI Act —
  "conditional on market and use".
- Five questions leadership can't answer (value where/how much; where AI shapes consequential
  decisions; what's ready to scale; what "good" looks like; how much agent autonomy).
- **Disposition vocabulary:** invest / govern / defer / policy / retire.

## Stage by stage

### Stage 1 — Choose the bets (use cases and readiness)
- **Task.** Score 7 × 5 cells (Data, Talent, Infrastructure, Workflow, Value: high/medium/low/"not
  attributable") each citing a card passage, no totals; one-sentence readiness read per candidate;
  portfolio table with one disposition each, 2–3 "invest"; ≤250-word surface (framing sentence,
  portfolio, bets and why, cost/return, ask), plus appendices Assumptions / Evidence and findings not
  documented / Open questions. Use observed not claimed benefit unless justified.
- **Habit taught.** "Executive surface, rigor beneath" — every surface sentence decides, reasons,
  costs, returns or asks; hedges go to the appendix. Worked example shows a forensic draft vs the
  board-ready rewrite; pitfalls: backing the biggest number, observed vs incremental value, hedging.
- **Exemplar path.** Invest: customer-service assist, demand forecasting, finance document
  processing (quick win). Govern: operations agent, hiring screen. Policy: marketing. Defer: knowledge
  search. Programme $17.3M over two years → $29.6M incremental benefit, net $12.3M (1.71×).

### Stage 2 — Sequence the roadmap (roadmap and operating model)
- **New evidence.** Readiness pack: (1) the assist was copied unchanged into commercial/sales-support
  and stalled — different unit of work, step not in any procedure, entitlement system not
  connected, no trained supervisor ("the copy moved the tool, not the conditions"); (2) regional data
  unevenness (Americas consolidated; Europe partly, shorter retention; APAC least, languages) with
  weakest-region coverage per candidate; foundation items each candidate requires; forecasting and
  knowledge search compete for the same data platform capacity; nobody costed the foundation
  separately. Investment/benefit sheet by phase (fully loaded, incremental benefit; two zero-benefit
  lines that buy control).
- **Task.** Place bets into three fixed phases — Ground and Prove (0–6), Scale What Works (7–12),
  Extend and Automate (13–24); quick win first; one operating-model decision per phase from four
  menus (delivery model: central / federated with central standard / unit-autonomous; foundations:
  extend / consolidate / per unit; funding: fund the phase / fund on evidence / fund annually;
  enablement: central academy / role-based in units / hire in); what each phase must prove to
  release the next; ≤300 words.
- **Habit taught.** "A phase is a decision point, not a container"; sequence from readiness, not size.

### Stage 3 — Govern the risks (governance and risk framework)
- **New evidence.** Hiring-screen incident note: an auditor asked how the screen influenced one
  rejection; nothing was logged (output, inputs, version, recruiter's agreement, review, which
  requirement, retention by market); a regulator then asked about the process; answer missed the
  review window. Supplied obligation statements: EU AI Act Annex III / Art. 6(2) applies from
  2 Dec 2027 ("will be, never is today"), GDPR Art. 22, NYC Local Law 144. Operations-agent proposal:
  what the agent may/may not do, oversight table (log without context, unstratified weekly sample,
  18% review, no alerting, no per-action stop, no retention standard, no named owner), rung of record
  = rung 2 but nobody holds the right to change rung; ask = extend to 5 units, raise thresholds, move
  up the ladder, fund oversight; extension is a market-entry decision; most value lies beyond the
  window. Trust ladder card (4 rungs with the conditions each must pay for; rung of record vs rung
  its evidence supports vs control effectiveness). Risk catalogue: 10 anchored risks R1–R10, pick 5.
- **Task.** Accountable owner (person or role, not a committee) per bet and for the programme;
  decision-rights setting per consequential system (advisory / informs a binding decision / binding
  with review) — today and target; the agent's rung of record vs supported rung and who may move it;
  5-row risk register (risk, anchor, mitigation as a *condition that becomes true*, owner, phase);
  governance line for every non-invested candidate; start the change-plan draft.
- **Habit taught.** "Governance is a set of conditions, not a set of intentions."

### Stage 4 — Prove the value (change and value)
- **New evidence.** Change-plan draft (frozen middle: 21% of managers enabled vs 20% proficient
  employees; enablement by role family — supervisors 46/180, planners 24/90, campaign leads 0,
  executives; adoption indicators left open). Adoption pulse survey (self-reported; blockers by
  population: service — nothing written about when to use it; marketing — no rule; planning — no
  trust where data thin, no way to record overrides; exception handling — don't know what the agent
  did; recruiting — unsure what's allowed since the incident; "heaviest use is least governed").
  Baselines (one operating metric per candidate + board measures). ROI workbook (Inputs: select,
  classify; Phases).
- **Task.** Finish the change plan (fund the frozen middle in a phase, a capability per role family,
  adoption indicators that aren't licences/courses); classify every non-zero benefit line as hard
  ROI (must name the ledger/meter) / soft ROI / strategic option value; ramp and phase timing per
  bet checked against its own adoption evidence; name the assumption closing each claimed-vs-observed
  gap; KPI table with baseline + year 1 + year 2, ≥1 leading adoption KPI, ≥1 governance KPI.
- **Habit taught.** "A value claim is a claim about a number somebody else can check."

### Stage 5 — Assemble and present
- **Task.** Assemble four sections into the brief template; run an integration check by category
  (candidate names, phase names, phase ranges, dispositions, owners, rungs, KPI names, figures) and
  repair at the source; give each of the five leadership questions a location; write a 400-word
  executive summary last, opening with the answer.
- **Habit taught.** "The last thing you write is the first thing they read."

## What the capstone makes the learner produce

A ~5,000-word board brief: readiness scorecard, portfolio, roadmap and operating-model choices,
accountability table, decision rights, risk register, change plan, benefit classification, ROI
workbook selection, KPI table, executive summary, plus a reflection per stage. All from supplied
evidence; all figures fixed; choices limited to menus and fixed strings.

## Gaps (what the learner still can't do on Monday)

1. **The data is handed over clean and complete.** Every number (coverage %, weekly actives, observed
   value, review rates, supervisor counts) arrives pre-measured on a card. In a real org, producing
   that inventory *is* the first three months: finding the 18 pilots and the shadow use, getting
   access, interviewing owners, pulling logs, reconciling $9.75M vs $2.3M. None of it is practised.
2. **No people.** The executives never push back; no sponsor meeting, no board Q&A, no negotiation
   with the CIO over the $7.2M ask, no conversation with recruiters or the exception handlers.
3. **Menu decisions with fixed strings.** Operating model is chosen from four menus; phases, rungs
   and dispositions are fixed vocabulary; the integration check is a string match. Useful
   discipline, but it trains document consistency, not judgment under ambiguity or designing the
   options yourself.
4. **No execution after the brief.** It ends at board approval. Nothing on running Ground and Prove:
   standing up the governance forum, writing the hiring-screen logging requirements, instrumenting the
   agent (stratified sampling, alerting, per-action stop), re-writing the commercial team procedure,
   training 134 supervisors, publishing the marketing policy, the first 30 days after funding.
5. **No building or testing.** The agent's supported rung, the forecast's accuracy, the assist's
   resolution rate are never tested by the learner; no eval, no pilot, no sample of outputs.
6. **One archetype.** A 50,000-person federated multinational with a C-level seat and board
   audience. No mid-size company, public body, regulated SME, or "consultant with no authority"
   situation.
7. **Documents not artifacts.** The policy for marketing, the decision-rights charter, the logging
   spec, the change-communication pieces are named as dispositions, never written.

## Course-level verdict

**Reuse:** the SGE case structure (pilot inventory cards with claimed vs observed, readiness signals,
sponsor asks as advocacy), the disposition vocabulary, the three-phase gate logic, the trust ladder
with "conditions as a bill" and rung-of-record vs supported rung, anchored risks with mitigations
written as conditions, hard/soft/option value with "name the ledger", KPI table rules, "executive
surface, rigor beneath", integration check. The capstone's design (fixed evidence packs, worked
example, exemplar path, assistant instruction block that forbids judgment) is a good model for our
engagement data rooms.

**Fill:** making the inventory and evidence yourself from a messy data room; live stakeholder
conversations (persona packs); building and testing the pilots; writing the actual policies, specs
and comms; executing phase 1 after approval; and running this across several org types and seats
(internal lead without board access, outside consultant, small org).
