## Module 1 — Turn Business Goals into AI Priorities

- Concepts / IBM terms:
  - "Use AI is the whole strategy" → "shallow and broad" pilots; root cause: starting from technology.
  - North-star principle — start from goals and KPIs the business already has; AI is fuel.
  - AI bet vs AI experiment — bet = goal + KPI + accountable owner; experiment = novelty/convenience.
    "Deep and narrow" — few bets, go deep.
  - Strategy (why/where) vs framework (how to decide) vs roadmap (when) — don't confuse.
  - 10-20-70 heuristic (BCG): ~10% algorithms, 20% data/tech, 70% people and process.
  - Cited: BCG leaders pursue fewer opportunities and expect >2× ROI; Gartner — ≥50% of GenAI
    projects abandoned after PoC by end of 2025 (verify before reuse).
  - "The North-Star Translation" skill.
- Concrete how-to: the expert tutorial gives the most practical content in the course so far —
  a 60–90 minute leadership session producing one page: 3–5 business objectives, each with a primary
  KPI, guardrail metric and executive sponsor; map each to the one workflow/decision point where the
  metric moves; bring AI candidates in one at a time with "which metric improves and by how much?";
  only then name solution type (ML / GenAI / rules / hybrid); keep 2–3 bets; park the rest with a
  written reason; set baseline (write "to be confirmed", never estimate) and a go/no-go gate before
  building. The expert mentions running stakeholder interviews and a stack review first ("opportunity
  audit") but does not show how.
- Exercise: 15-minute "AI-priority statement" card from one goal of your own, with a browser AI
  assistant; template sentence "To [goal], improve [KPI] from [baseline] by changing [workflow] through
  [bet], measured by [success]" + solution type + one parked item. Worked examples: membership store
  recommendations; consulting-proposal turnaround (demo).
- Gaps: how to get leadership into that 60–90 minute session and facilitate it (agenda, pre-reads,
  handling the CEO's pet idea); how to actually find the baseline the card leaves "to be confirmed";
  the stakeholder interviews and stack review are named but never taught.

## Module 2 — Prioritize AI Use Cases

- Concepts / IBM terms:
  - "Negotiation trap" — selection by loudest sponsor, feasibility mistaken for priority.
  - Value–feasibility–risk matrix → quadrants: Priority Now, Strategic Bets, Quick Wins, Kill or
    Defer; risk as weight, overlay or hard gate.
  - Weighted composite scoring; the real value is the weight-setting conversation.
  - Clustering trap (everything top-right) and central tendency; fixes: median boundaries, absolute
    gates, use full range, demand evidence for top-right.
  - Use-case canvas to normalise candidates; score independently, discuss divergence not average.
  - Portfolio across horizons (quick wins / strategic bets / moonshots); order by dependency;
    "value first — feasibility and risk shape sequence, not selection".
  - "Defensible prioritization"; "The Value–Feasibility–Risk Sort" skill.
- Concrete how-to: the scoring method is described, no rubric anchors (what a "4" on data readiness
  means), no canvas template shown in text, no worked portfolio.
- Plugin: "Score One AI Use Case" (single case).
- Gaps: running a real scoring workshop (independent scoring, calibration, dealing with a
  sponsor whose idea lands in Kill); evidence gathering per criterion; a full portfolio with 10–15
  candidates; how to communicate the ranking back to losing sponsors.

## Module 3 — Build the AI Transformation Roadmap

- Concepts / IBM terms:
  - "Everything looks like Phase One"; a prioritised list is not a roadmap.
  - Roadmap = phased, milestone-driven; strategy, data, governance, org design as parallel tracks.
  - Organizational absorption — the usual binding constraint once value/fit are known.
  - The absorption question — 4 conditions: data pipelines in place, governance to approve/monitor,
    team change capacity, integration dependencies resolved.
  - Momentum and the momentum test — does a Phase-One success fund Phase Two?
  - "Sequence for momentum, not speed"; people/process tracks start in Phase One.
  - Phase-one exit = measurable improvement through use, not technical completion.
  - Interview answer: 90-day foundation phase ending with a ranked portfolio, a lightweight
    governance foundation and one documented evidence point.
  - "Sequence for Absorption" skill.
- Concrete how-to: a scoring matrix for placement (value, measurement clarity, data readiness,
  complexity, risk, integration, absorption) — described verbally, no template.
- Plugin: "Phase One or Phase Two?" (placement decisions).
- Gaps: building the actual roadmap artifact (Gantt/now-next-later, workstreams, owners, budgets,
  gates); dependency mapping in practice; what the "lightweight governance foundation" contains;
  what a 90-day plan looks like week by week.

## Module 4 — Build the AI Business Case

- Concepts / IBM terms:
  - "Case written for the wrong reader" (tech language for P&L people).
  - Structure: problem ($X problem) → solution → financial model → risks → ask.
  - Measured baseline (cost, time, error rate, capacity from operational data).
  - TCO iceberg — data prep, integration, infra, talent, change, maintenance beyond licences.
  - Three scenarios; conservative first; 12-month proof point + multi-year value.
  - Named accountability (risk register with owner, cadence, review trigger); do-nothing scenario.
  - "The Fundable Case" skill.
- Plugin (opened): invoice-exception process; baseline 12,000 exceptions × 18 min × $42/h =
  $151,200; year-1 TCO $75,000 (software 18k, data/integration 34k, change 16k, contingency 7k);
  conservative realisation 40% → $60,480 savings → ROI −19.4%; learner computes it, then plays with
  sliders (data cost, change cost, realisation rate) to see when the floor clears breakeven.
- Gaps: where the 40% realisation rate comes from; turning minutes saved into real money (capacity
  vs cash — only touched in course 6); token/inference running costs and human-review cost; an actual
  one-page case template; how to pull the baseline out of a ticketing/ERP export; presenting to a CFO.

## Course-level verdict

- Reuse as vocabulary: north star, bet vs experiment, deep and narrow, strategy/framework/roadmap,
  10-20-70, negotiation trap, quadrant names, clustering trap, absorption question, momentum test,
  $X problem, TCO iceberg, conservative-first, do-nothing scenario, "to be confirmed" beats a guess.
- Fill: facilitating the leadership session and the scoring workshop with real people; measuring
  baselines from data; a full worked portfolio and roadmap; a real ROI spreadsheet with token and
  review costs; the 90-day foundation phase as a concrete week-by-week plan. Only one module has a
  guided exercise; the rest is concept videos plus a single-case widget.
