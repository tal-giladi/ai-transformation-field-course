# IBM "Lead AI Transformation: Strategy, Governance & Execution" — what it covers

Source: the public Coursera pages, read 2026-11-02. Tal completed the certificate. The exercise
guides, plugins and capstone materials are behind login and were not read (Tal offered to log in).

Program page: https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/ibm-lead-ai-transformation-strategy-governance-execution
7 courses, ~41 hours. Every module has the same shape: a problem, terms, an expert tutorial/demo, a
short graded assignment and a 15-minute "plugin" exercise. All work happens inside one fictional
company, from the seat of a senior leader who already has the mandate, the data and the people.

## Course by course

## What it does well (keep, don't repeat)

- Good judgment vocabulary: "licence is not adoption", "pilot is not scale", "test the control,
  earn the rating", "last reversible moment", "net the claim", "sequence for absorption".
- Decision gates and verdicts (pass/flag/block, scale/sustain/retire).
- Regulatory reading method (actor, instrument, date, auditable claim).

## The gap Tal felt (why this course exists)

1. **No entry.** It starts with the mandate, the data and the trust already in hand. Nothing on day 1:
   how you get the mandate, who to meet first, how to get data access, what to do in week 1.
2. **No discovery.** Use cases arrive pre-written. Nobody teaches finding them: interviews, process
   walks, task inventories, measuring a baseline, finding shadow AI.
3. **No building.** Nobody builds, evaluates or runs a pilot. "Ship / don't ship" is judged from a
   description, never from an eval you ran on real data.
4. **No documents you can hand over.** No AI policy, acceptable-use policy, vendor review, DPIA,
   pilot charter, training session plan, steering pack — only 15-minute single-row exercises.
5. **One company.** Every judgment is practised once, on one scenario. No contrast between a
   hospital, a municipality, a law firm and a factory, where the same move is right in one and wrong
   in another.
6. **No people.** No real conversation practice: resistant managers, a blocking CISO, a sponsor who
   wants a chatbot by next month, a union.
7. **Mostly "what fails".** Lots of failure patterns, few step-by-step "do this, then this".
