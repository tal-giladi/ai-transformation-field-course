## Module 1 — Structuring the AI Business Case ("the admissions gate")

- **Scenario/role.** Function head's quality team reads supplier documents to find a few exceptions;
  an 11-page draft case mostly explains the system and ends with one ROI figure and a payback. The
  committee deferred it. Four readings: function head (prices full recovered hours), finance partner
  (needs the landing line and an owner), case analyst (pilot discount hidden in a footnote), executive
  sponsor (wants strategic option value stated).
- **Concepts.**
  - The case must describe a movement of money, not a capability.
  - Hard return (cash effect visible on a statement, e.g. cancelled licence) vs soft return (real but
    estimated, e.g. better forecast) — graded by observability, not size.
  - Strategic option value as a labelled qualitative overlay at decision points (expand / delay /
    stop), not priced with option maths.
  - Landing line and owner for every benefit; recovered hours are capacity until headcount, contract
    labour or another line changes; producing ≠ monetising; in a services relationship gains usually
    go to the client.
  - Efficiency claims (have a baseline) vs creation claims (new capacity/revenue, no baseline, later
    evidence); label every claim E or C with its evidence and month.
  - Admissions gate: frame in the work's own units → scope until costable → test family, observed vs
    estimated, landing line → discount pilot figures → named scenarios → keep "no" possible.
- **How-to given.** Yes, procedural (frame, test, adjust, keep the no available) with drills.
- **Gaps.** No spreadsheet, no worked numbers, no template for the case document; no method for
  getting finance to name the line or for getting an owner to *accept* a reduction.

## Module 2 — Building the ROI Model ("where the saving lands")

- **Scenario/role.** A drafting assistant for customer service responses; sponsor's case = hours ×
  loaded rate − vendor quote. Quality team now works overtime re-reading drafts that look finished;
  customer noticed faster turnaround. Four readings: finance partner, quality lead, internal audit
  lead (a "human review" is not a control unless performable at the volume), commercial account lead
  (customer will claim part of a visible efficiency at renewal).
- **Concepts.**
  - Model boundary: value created vs effort relocated; price each relocation and name its new owner.
  - Costs beyond the invoice: enablement, maintenance, technical support, review labour.
  - Retained value = gross efficiency − the customer's share.
  - Present value: discount each year's benefit by the organization's rate.
  - Staged investment: gated commitments; option left unpriced; NPV + scenarios + sensitivity as the
    defensible standard.
  - Cost frame: inference spend as *capacity* (budgeted against hiring deferred) vs *technology*
    (portfolio position with a defined return) — state the frame explicitly.
  - Gate placement: review at the front / before commitment / conditional on a risk signal; the gate
    sets review volume × minutes = verification labour, a cost line.
- **How-to given.** Six moves: split, price, complete, govern, test (the reviewer at real load), then
  discount and stage. Drills, no worked spreadsheet.
- **Gaps.** No token/inference cost estimation, no cost catalogue with real price points, no sample
  model, no sensitivity table built. Never models a conditional gate's trigger rate from data.

## Module 3 — Defining Value Metrics ("name the proof first")

- **Scenario/role.** Service and warranty operation, assistant live two quarters: high usage, steady
  satisfaction, lower handling time. Finance asks what it saved; three different "before" figures
  from three systems; a report definition changed near rollout; a seasonal peak and a routing change
  in the same period; a budgeted analyst backfill quietly never requested. Four readings: operations
  lead, finance partner, first-line supervisor (repeat contacts and correction time rising), BI
  analyst (fidelity).
- **Concepts.**
  - Activity metrics vs value metrics.
  - Baseline taken by the same instrument and definition as every later reading; self-report ≠
    instrument; measure locally; if no baseline exists, say so, name the measurement and the start
    date.
  - Paired quality metric that moves the wrong way if the efficiency gain is fake (invoice time ↔
    correction rate).
  - Attribution levels and claim fidelity labels: workflow-level before/after → attributed;
    team/function KPI or heavy vs light users → directional; population-level assumed effect →
    assumption.
  - Adoption gate: a floor of real use before value metrics are read.
  - Evidence maturation: observational → team-level comparative (absorbed work, declined backfill,
    lower external spend) → financial; name the transition date.
  - Second-order measures (decision quality, manager bandwidth, retention of scarce experts) — one or
    two, instrumented and labelled, never monetised with invented arithmetic.
  - Have teams re-justify budgeted hires so absorbed capacity shows up as declined spend.
- **How-to given.** Seven things to name before anyone asks: indicator, target, baseline, pair,
  comparison, gate, date.
- **Gaps.** No instrument-building (which report, which query, which export), no comparison design
  details (matched teams, staggered rollout, how long), no metric-plan template, no dashboard.

## Module 4 — Making the Decision-Ready Case ("reader-order assembly")

- **Scenario/role.** AI-assisted quality inspection; readiness, roadmap, model, metrics all exist;
  the committee deferred and asked for a shorter paper. Four readings: advisor (the reading order
  failed), finance partner (traceability), sponsoring executive (cost of delay, option that closes),
  plant quality operations manager (which claims must my team prove, by when).
- **Concepts.**
  - Decision-ready case: one-page front (ask, return and timeline, conditions) + back layer of
    detail; same analysis, different order.
  - Evidence labels on every claim: measured/attributable, directional, assumed (with a date).
  - Inherited constraints: readiness sets what may be claimed, the roadmap sets when; "source tag"
    each claim.
  - Explicit decision request: what, who approves, by when; two "and"s = two decisions.
  - Risk of inaction as a two-column comparison in the same units, not a warning.
  - Case credibility: strategic rationale and the quantitative case in separate sections;
    conservative scenario first.
- **How-to given.** Nine moves ("three threes"); one-page front drill; two-reader check (finance and
  sponsor).
- **Gaps.** No document template, no example of a full decision-ready case, no rehearsal of the
  committee meeting itself.

## New vs first certificate

Mostly a deeper repeat of the first certificate's course 2 module 4 (business case) and course 6
(net the claim, capacity vs cash, counterfactual, goal–driver–indicator chain, conservative first,
cost of doing nothing). Genuinely new or much sharper:
- **Cost frame** (capacity vs technology line) for inference spend.
- **Customer's share of a visible efficiency** (retained value) — matters for services firms and SaaS.
- **Efficiency vs creation claims** with different evidence obligations.
- **Gate placement as a cost line** (front / before commitment / conditional trigger).
- **Evidence maturation stages** and the "declined backfill" as team-level evidence; re-justifying
  budgeted hires.
- **Claim fidelity labels** tied to attribution level; **adoption gate** before reading value.
- **Decision-ready case structure** (one-page front, one-sentence ask, source tags, two-column
  inaction, two-reader check).

## Implications for our plan

1. **Extend 03.5 (ROI model)**: add columns/sheets for effort relocated (new owner + price), customer's
   share, cost frame statement, verification labour from gate placement (trigger rate × minutes),
   E/C claim family, hard/soft/option label, discounting by period, staged gates. Update the
   **ROI model template** accordingly; the code track computes NPV, scenarios and sensitivity.
2. **New template: value measurement plan** (indicator the budget owner already reviews, target that
   can be missed, baseline instrument + definition + period, paired quality metric, comparison +
   fidelity label, adoption gate, evidence-maturation transition date, 1–2 second-order measures).
   Taught in **05.1** (with the counterfactual) and used in every NN.3 and 07.2.
3. **Extend 07.1 (steering pack)** into a decision-ready case: one-page front, one-sentence ask (what /
   who / by when), measured–directional–assumed labels, source tags to readiness and roadmap,
   conservative first, two-column cost of waiting, verification metric and date, and the two-reader
   check. Rename the template **decision-ready case** (front page + appendix).
4. **Planted findings for a data room** (Meridian Mutual claims or Northwind support): three
   conflicting baselines from three systems, a report definition changed near rollout, a seasonal
   peak overlapping the pilot, handling time down while repeat contacts rise, and an unrequested
   budgeted backfill as hidden evidence. Excellent for NN.2–NN.3 and 07.2.
5. **Atlas & Rowe (M12) and Lumen SaaS (M14)**: make the customer-share effect explicit in the
   debrief (faster turnaround gets renegotiated at renewal).
6. **Simulation `roi-defender`**: add the four-ledger view (sponsor, receiving team, audit/control
   capacity, customer) so a claim is netted across ledgers before it is "banked".
7. Persona types: **internal audit lead** and **commercial account lead** are new to our packs; add
   them to M9 (insurer) and M14 (SaaS) respectively.
