# Capstone: Tzurim Academic College

Run a whole engagement, from the first call to the handover, in an organization you have never seen and with no lesson telling you what to look for. Tzurim Academic College is a fictional private college in the southern Shfela with about 6,840 students and 712 staff. Its CEO has told the board that first-year dropout jumped from 12% to almost 20% and wants an AI early-warning system from a US vendor; the board wants one recommendation on Tuesday 4 May 2027. You are the outside consultant. This is the course's final practical: the full deliverable set, consistent across every document, and a recorded ten-minute presentation.

## The brief

Read [the engagement brief](../labs/module-18/brief.md) (in the module download). In short: you start on Sunday 7 February 2027; fixed fee ₪186,000 in three four-week phases with an exit after each; you report to the CEO, Yaron Bar-Lev, day to day through the VP Administration; a ₪1.2M "Digital transformation and AI" budget line has no approved content; the vendor wants a data extract by 25 February for a free pilot on 1 March; the board decided that any vendor pilot using student data needs the DPO's review first. The data room is `labs/module-18/data-room/` (start with its [INDEX](../labs/module-18/data-room/INDEX.md)); the people are in `labs/module-18/personas/` ([the cast](../labs/module-18/personas/README.md)).

## Steps

1. Read the brief and the INDEX; plan the engagement to 4 May; meet the sponsor; write the charter, the first read, the access request and the hypotheses log (18.1).
2. Decide whom to meet and in which order; run the interviews (roleplay or transcripts, coach after each); profile the data and measure the baselines; write the findings memo (02.1–02.7).
3. Write the readiness bar before you review any vendor evidence; grade the evidence; give a verdict per use (02.6).
4. Map, screen and score the opportunities; net the money with finance; write the roadmap with gates (03.1–03.8).
5. Write the policy pack for what is already in use and what you propose (04.1–04.8).
6. Choose the pilot; date its thresholds; build the test set from the data room; run the baseline, the pilot and the negative tests; decide (05.1–05.7; Track B with `fieldkit` if you like).
7. Write the adoption plan with the people whose work changes (06.1–06.6).
8. Write the case; build the register; run the integration check; fix, flag or disclose (18.2).
9. Choose the hardest finding and the executive most likely to resist it; rehearse privately twice; record the ten minutes; score it; run the board's questions; write the decision record and the handover (18.3, 07.2, 07.9).
10. Score the whole set with the rubric below. **Then** open the reveal block and the expert set and compare.

Time: about 15 hours beyond the exercises in lessons 18.1–18.3 (which cover about 20 hours of the work), most of it in steps 2, 6 and 8.

### What was really going on at Tzurim

The stated ask was a prediction system. **Tzurim did not have a prediction problem.** It had a broken measure, a process that pushed students out, and an AI risk score already running without anyone deciding it should.

- **The number counted students who had not left.** The registrar's query (BI-07, 2019) counted every status except 10, 21, 41 and 60 as dropout. Status 47, "frozen — reserve duty", added in November 2024, and 33, "moved to another programme in the college", were counted; cohort 2023's reservist freezes had been coded 41 and were excluded. Students who actually left (51, 52, 58): 11.1%, 13.3%, 11.5% for cohorts 2023–2025 — flat — against the report's 12.2%, 19.5%, 17.7%. The registrar had asked IT to fix the query in December 2024; nobody asked her before the board meeting.
- **The college blocked its own students out of exams.** The finance system blocked exam registration for any balance over ₪1,500 on 4 January 2026; registration closed seven days later; the median block lasted eleven days. 60% of blocked students had a scholarship or reservist grant approved but not yet posted — the college's own monthly batch. Reservists were blocked at 61%, others at 13%. First-years who missed registration through a block left at 33.7% against 7.6%; 45% of the year's first-year leavers had been through it.
- **The help desk sent them to the wrong place.** Students who could not register picked "exams"; 48% of the inquiries finance eventually resolved started elsewhere, and reassigned inquiries took a median of nine working days. Arabic inquiries waited longest, were reopened 30% of the time against 11%, and 92% were answered in Hebrew.
- **AI was already deciding whom to call.** The LMS vendor's February 2026 release switched on a risk score by default. It flagged Haredi-track students (58% red) and reservists (46%) far more than others (7%) with no higher leaving rate, used "medical or learning accommodation on file" as a driver, and caught 19% of later leavers; 30% of the year's leavers had gone before it existed. No inventory, no DPIA, a 2022 privacy notice, a DPA signed before the feature.
- **The urgent finding.** The red list — 288 students with names, numbers and reasons, 38 of them a medical or learning accommodation — had been sent in March 2026 to 61 lecturers as an "anyone with the link" sheet, forwarded to teaching assistants, and still opened on 4 February 2027. The right move was a phone call to the VP Administration and the DPO **the day you read the e-mail thread**, the link closed that evening, and the DPO left to assess whether it was reportable. Holding it for the findings memo, or investigating it yourself, was the wrong move.
- **The vendor's evidence and terms.** "85%" was recall in the top 30% of scores on one held-out cohort at one US community college; its retention gain came in the year a statewide free-tuition programme started. The pilot terms asked for three years of data including accommodations flags and identifiers, processing in the US, a licence to train on de-identified data that survives termination, and automatic conversion to a 36-month subscription. The CEO had already written "let's start on 1 March" and offered to be a reference site.
- **A procedure that existed on paper.** The early-contact procedure reached 24% of eligible first-years, 12% on time; from March the advisers worked from the red list instead, and 15% of those calls ended with the student objecting.
- **Shadow AI where the load was.** Adjuncts were the heaviest AI users (63% weekly or daily), a quarter of them entering student work with names; HR's "65% never use AI" covered administrative staff only. Students were referred for misconduct on an AI detector's score alone in 19 of 46 cases, nine of them non-native Hebrew writers; six of eight appeals were overturned. The office AI licences sat with managers (3 of 120 in student administration; 39 never used).
- **One join trap.** Finance exported student numbers as numbers: new-system numbers lost their leading zero, so a naive join matched 1,906 of 6,128 accounts.
- **People who knew.** The VP Finance knew the grant batch caught "a few hundred"; the registrar had reported the query; the head of Student Success had a bad feeling about the sheet; the student union's welfare officer had been blocked herself and then called because the system "flagged" her accommodation.

### What a strong answer looked like

One recommendation: **fix how Tzurim counts and how it pushes students out before buying prediction.** Restate the measure (first-year leaving 11.5%, freezes and transfers reported apart; target ≤ 10% for cohort 2027); a block fast lane before the June 2027 check (approved grants never block, arrangements decided in three working days, block inquiries routed straight to collections, a rule-based adviser alert); an inquiry assistant that routes and drafts in the student's language for staff to send, in shadow from May; the risk score paused for outreach until a DPIA, a fairness test and a new notice; a policy pack with an approved staff tool and no detector-only cases. **Against:** no vendor pilot or contract in 2027; re-decide in March 2028 on a proof of concept under the college's conditions. Money: ₪540,000 of the ₪1.2M line, released in two stages with a stop rule. The hardest finding went privately to the CEO on 2 May: the number he gave the board, the vendor he said yes to, and a tool already in use.

### Six months later: three futures

**Future 1 — The free pilot.** The extract went to the vendor on 25 February; the board approved the subscription in May "to keep the momentum". In October the student union learned from a teaching assistant that the red list named accommodations; a complaint reached the Privacy Protection Authority, and the college paused the vendor and the LMS score at once. Advisers had called about 900 "at-risk" students that autumn, a third of them reservists. The June and January blocks ran as before; first-year leaving stayed at 12.1%; the December report still said 18%.

**Future 2 — Measure, fast lane, assistant (the expert path).** The board adopted the corrected measure on 4 May. The June 2027 check blocked 251 students instead of 643; block-missed registrations fell by 62%; bad debt did not rise. Routing went live in July and Arabic drafts in September; 84% of Arabic inquiries were answered in Arabic by October, and their reopen rate fell to 13%. Cohort 2026's first-year leaving was 9.9% on 1 October 2027 (one year: directional). The LMS score stayed paused pending the December review. The vendor accepted an EU region and no automatic conversion, not the end of the training licence; the March 2028 re-decision stands.

**Future 3 — Half-way.** The board approved the assistant and the policy pack, which were visible and cheap; the VP Finance would not touch the block before the 2028 budget, and the LMS score stayed on "because it's free". The assistant got block inquiries to collections faster but could not lift a block; leaving did not change, and advisers kept calling reservists from the list.

**What decided which future happened:** whether the money went to the block process and the measure rather than the visible tool, and whether the open list and the running score were stopped before anything new was added.
