# Readout — Harbor & Pine

Take Harbor & Pine from the steering decision to a service that runs without you: the steering pack and the meeting, the bad news upward, the gate-1 decision, the incident, the operating rhythm, the pressure test, the phase-1 plan and the handover. This is module 7's practical and the end of Stage 1: one integrated readout of the whole warm-up engagement, every document consistent with every other.

## The brief

You are Harbor & Pine's Head of AI and Operations Improvement. Since November 2026 you have found what really drives support contacts, put guardrails in place, and built and evaluated a reply-drafts pilot. Between January and June 2027 the pilot has to be decided, run, judged at its first gate, survive an incident, become a service and be handed to the people who run it. Your readout is the set of documents that carry those decisions, plus the pressure test of the roadmap the CEO was sold before you arrived.

The fixed points: steering committee Tuesday 19 January 2027; board Thursday 28 January; pilot live Monday 1 February; the incident Monday 15 March; gate 1 Thursday 1 April (₪48,000 released in January, ₪72,000 at stake); handover by Wednesday 30 June; WhatsApp re-gate Thursday 1 July. The expected case in January: about ₪113,000 a year in cash and about 570 agent-hours a year of capacity, three-year cost about ₪267,000.

## Self-assessment rubric

| Criterion | What "strong" looks like | What "weak" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Decision first | Every document opens with the decision it supports, who can stop the work and what a "no" means; asks are in minutable words | Background first; "approved in principle"; the ask on page five |
| Evidence labels | Every number is measured, directional or assumed, with its source; test-set results never called production results; capacity never shown as cash | One label for everything; a vendor or sponsor figure stated as a forecast |
| The weak line | The case's weakest assumption (the second temp) is on page one in January, tested at gate 1 and re-forecast plainly in April | The weak line in an annex; the April numbers explained away |
| Bad news | The finding stated once, early, privately to the CEO before the room, with options; the sponsor told first; the relays found | Softened until it disappears, or delivered as a correction of the CEO in front of the board |
| Gate reading | Each condition read per segment; non-compensable gates first; a written departure if money moves past a failed gate | Verdict on the pilot-wide average; money released without a sentence |
| Incident | Narrowest switch first; "what changed?" before any fix; privacy to counsel the same day with no legal deadline asserted; a review that changes controls, not people | Prompt patch over the wrong source; warning letters; a deadline guessed; "resolved, no further action" |
| Service ownership | One accountable owner with the switch; RACI with one A per line; calendar with hours traced to the case; triggers separate from cadence; retirement criteria written | "The committee owns it"; an uncosted rhythm; no retirement criteria |
| Pressure test | Findings with evidence, typed wrong or fragile, with dispositions and owners; controls load-tested; what survives named; sponsor first | Risk categories; everything killed; sent over the sponsor's head |
| Running phase 1 | The yes made executable in 72 hours; hours agreed; payments against deliverables; stage dates treated as earliest dates | The team hears from the rota; money by date; a skipped exit condition nobody recorded |
| Handover | Every living document owned; known limits on page one; acceptance tests passed without you | A walkthrough and a folder link; the builder still in every decision |
| Integration | Numbers, dates, scope and controls agree across all deliverables; contradictions that changed a decision were put to the forum | 109, 117 and 93 hours in three documents; a control nobody pays for |
| Privacy of people | Adoption reported by queue; nobody named in a board paper without consent | Three names in a decision record; an enthusiast's quote as adoption evidence |

**One-page readout — Harbor & Pine, board of July 2027 (model)**

> **What was asked, and what was going on.** In September 2026 the board heard "an AI bot and 40% lower support cost". About 38% of support contacts were customers asking where their order was, because the web store promised a fixed delivery date that containers from Vietnam and China missed by a median 11 days; most written contact arrived on WhatsApp, which no report counted. *(Measured: findings memo, 1 December 2026.)*
>
> **What changed.** Since January the company tells customers the real date before the promised one passes: "where is my order?" fell from about 44% to about 22% of written contact by March *(measured share)*. AI drafts on email and the web form, in Hebrew, Arabic, Russian and English, became a service on 4 April, owned by the Customer Service manager: 82.5% of drafts accepted, 3.3 minutes saved per drafted contact against a holdout *(measured, attributed)*; every reply is still read and sent by an agent. The translation seats it replaced are cancelled: ₪17,000 a year *(banked)*. Guardrails: an AI policy, approved tools, a data boundary and a register of every AI use since November.
>
> **What did not work, and what we did.** WhatsApp drafts failed gate 1 (58% accepted); they were fixed with ₪30,000 of the ₪72,000 available, and passed their re-gate on 1 July for Hebrew and Russian; Arabic stays in shadow mode until August. A March incident (a draft quoting an out-of-date policy, and one disclosure of a customer's address) was contained in 15 minutes; customers were honoured, counsel handled the reporting question, and source changes now require tests and approval.
>
> **What it is worth.** About ₪65,000 a year in cash if the WhatsApp numbers hold through September *(directional)*: the translation seats and one winter temp, not two; plus agent capacity of roughly 350 hours a year after the cost of running the service. This is a capacity investment that pays for itself, not the 40%.
>
> **Recommendation.** Keep the service and decide the winter staffing at gate 2 on 30 September on the July–September numbers. **Recommend against** the chatbot proof of concept this year: its re-screen condition (WhatsApp drafts proven, six months of correct dates) is not met before October. **De-scope** B2B quote drafts until the price agreements are in the ERP (12 of 30 today).
>
> **At gate 2 the board will see:** acceptance and minutes saved per channel and language; wrong replies against today's rate; hours at peak against one temp; the cost of running the service.
>
> **The three numbers.** AI uses in production: 3 (drafts service, office assistant, catalogue copy). Median days from request to decision: 6. Declined or not yet, with reasons: 5 (chatbot, Instagram drafts, B2B quotes, AI candidate ranking, assembly answers until 30 June).

**Integration points a strong set gets right:** the date fix's live date (14 January) in every document; one hours figure as the plan with its labelled range; the operating rhythm's hours netted from the capacity line; stop rules per channel × language from charter v1.1 on; the WhatsApp pod reported as a queue, never as names; the board's three numbers from the intake register, not from memory.
