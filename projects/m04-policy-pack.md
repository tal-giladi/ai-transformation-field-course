# The policy pack for Harbor & Pine

The module 4 practical: Harbor & Pine's complete AI policy pack, from the acceptable-use policy to the governance charter and intake register, plus one vendor claim read-back — made consistent, so that every rule traces to a census finding, every approved tool to a vendor review, every High use to a DPIA and tested risks, and every legal statement to a source and to counsel. Most pieces start as your answers to lessons 04.1–04.8; the practical asks you to finish them, make them agree, and present them as one pack the review group can adopt at its first meeting. Plan 6 hours if you completed the lessons' "Your turn" tasks, longer if you did not.

## The brief

It is Thursday 3 December 2026. The AI review group meets for the first time on 10 December, and Michal Peretz, the COO, wants the pack in members' hands three working days before. "I want one folder we can adopt, not twelve drafts. If Ronit asks why a tool is approved, or Avi asks who can switch on automatic replies, or our lawyer asks where a date came from, the answer has to be in there." The pack also goes, in summary, to the board on 28 January 2027.

You have the Harbor & Pine data room, the persona pack, and everything you wrote in modules 1–4: the shadow-AI census and the interim rule (01.6), the AI inventory (01.7), the findings and data profiles (module 2), and the portfolio decision to run the reply-drafts pilot (module 3). The pilot drafts replies for all written support contact — email, web form, and WhatsApp once routed into the helpdesk — and a person sends every reply.

## Steps

1. **Collect** deliverables 1–12 from your lesson work. Where you skipped a "Your turn", do it now; the lessons' reference answers show the standard but are not yours to copy.
2. **Trace every rule and every approval.** Each AUP rule → a census or inventory finding. Each tool on the approved list → its vendor review and data boundary. Each High use → its DPIA and its risk-register rows. Each legal row → a source with a date, or "counsel".
3. **Make the documents agree.** One set of data classes; one tier rule; the same inventory IDs in the inventory, DPIA, risk register, regulatory map and intake register; the same dates (policy effective date, extension removal, opt-out, first group meeting). List every inconsistency you fix.
4. **Check the statuses.** No green in the risk register without sampled or tested evidence. No draft guidance or proposal presented as law. No legal conclusion stated as settled.
5. **Check the promises to staff.** The amnesty is kept; the logging section matches what IT actually collects; nothing reports AI use by person.
6. **Write the cover note** and the three numbers as of today.
7. **Self-assess** with the rubric below, then run the [coach prompt](../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) on the cover note and the AUP with the rubric as the checklist.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Strong | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Acceptable-use policy | Two pages; rules trace to the census with reasons; points to the list; concrete checks before sending; honest logging section; owner, review date, translations | Long, generic, names tools; "use AI responsibly"; silent or vague on monitoring |
| Data classes and list | Company examples; highest-class and identifier rules; plans not brands; uses in three tiers; restricted uses with who may lift them; transition with dates | Abstract classes; "ChatGPT approved"; no route to say yes; free extension still installed |
| Vendor reviews | Same ten sections for all; clauses cited; each finding becomes a setting, ask or condition with owner and date; verdicts with pre-conditions | Marketing pages read as terms; "looks fine"; certifications taken from logos |
| Inventory, tiers and DPIA | Rule in checkable conditions; overrides with reasons; DPIA from triggers; risks to people as mechanisms; residual risk accepted by a named person; notice gaps to counsel | Tiers by feel or by tool; DPIA about the company's risks; signed by the author |
| Risk register | Pre-mortem plus starter and security risks; mechanisms; control status honest; five controls tested; failure signals on every green | Category list; owners' colours unchanged; "asked the owner" as evidence |
| Regulatory map | Israel first; actor, obligation, application date, status, source and date per row; EU chains of facts; questions for counsel | A list of laws; entry-into-force dates as application dates; legal conclusions asserted |
| Governance | Six parts pointing to documents; small group with decision rights and a stop right; intake with overlaps and days; three numbers | Big committee; no time limits; every request through a business case |
| Claim due diligence | Five conditions per claim; hop-traced; fit table; decision first; proof of concept on own data with criteria fixed in advance | Arguing with the number; discount sets the timeline; vendor demo accepted as evidence |
| Consistency | Same IDs, classes, tiers and dates across the pack; inconsistencies listed and fixed | Different numbers or classes for the same thing in different documents |
