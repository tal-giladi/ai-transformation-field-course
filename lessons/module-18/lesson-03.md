# 18.3 · The executive presentation

Ten minutes, a board of directors, and one decision. Everything you found at Tzurim has to fit into one recommendation stated in the first minute, with the evidence labelled and the hardest finding already delivered to the person who most needs to own it. In this lesson you choose that finding and that person, rehearse with them privately, build and record the ten minutes, and score the recording against a rubric. At the end you have a recorded presentation, your rehearsal transcripts with coaching, a scored sheet with timestamps and a draft decision for the minutes.

## The situation

Tuesday 4 May 2027, 17:00–18:30: the Tzurim board. Your item has ten minutes of presentation and twenty of questions. The chair, Dr. Eitan Rozen, told you on the phone: "One recommendation. They will have read the pre-read. Don't read it to them." The CEO, the President and the VP Finance sit at the table; the board has a finance committee and, from the January minutes, a chair who does not want the college in the newspaper. Your pre-read went out on 20 April.

This is module 7 under a time limit and without a script: the [steering pack](../module-07/lesson-01.md), [delivering bad news upward](../module-07/lesson-03.md) and [the steering meeting](../module-07/lesson-04.md), with the decision-ready case from [03.9](../module-03/lesson-09.md) as its front page. IBM's consulting certificate ends its governance course with executive communication and "the advisory story"; here you tell it to people who can say no, and you record it so you can see yourself do it.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Write the recommendation as one sentence a secretary can minute

Start from your decision-ready case's ask. Test the sentence against four questions: does it say **what** the board approves, **how much**, **who** owns it and **by when**? Then write the draft resolution the company secretary would put in the minutes, with the stop rule and the re-decision date. If you cannot write the resolution, the presentation will end in "thank you, we'll consider it".

A worked example from Kinneret Plastics ([13.6](../module-13/lesson-06.md)): "Kinneret loses deliveries because stops last too long — half of the lost hours are moulds, mostly at night — and because nobody records what failed; so we fix the night response for Arava now, build the records any AI needs this year, give the night technicians a tested assistant in their language, and decide on predictive maintenance in January 2028 on our own data." The resolution followed from it: Arava items approved and sent on 30 March; ₪730,000 in three stages with a stop rule on 30 June; the assistant in shadow use from 4 April; the vendor declined for 2027 with a re-decision in January 2028.

### Step 2 — Choose the hardest finding, and the executive most likely to resist it

List your findings that change a decision. For each, ask who loses something if it is true:

| Finding | Who made a commitment it contradicts (to whom, when) | Who owns the process it criticises | Who loses money, authority or face | Resistance (1–3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The hardest finding is usually the one that contradicts a **public commitment**: a number given to a board, a promise to a vendor, a target in a strategy. The executive most likely to resist is the one who made it. At Kinneret it was the owner: he had announced predictive maintenance at the annual meeting and been told by a vendor that his plant could be its first reference. The finding — not this year, and the deadline was not Kinneret's — was delivered to him alone on the Sunday before the meeting.

Choose yours for Tzurim from your own findings. The rubric does not reward a particular choice; it rewards a choice you can justify from who committed to what.

### Step 3 — Rehearse privately, change the delivery, keep the finding

The method is [07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md): answer first, the finding stated once, evidence separated from inference, live options, and no softened version. Prepare four things before the rehearsal:

1. **Their goal in their own words.** Find the line from your first meeting where they said what success means. Start there.
2. **Evidence they can see.** One table or one person's story, not twelve charts.
3. **Something they can win.** A role in the fix, a message they can give the board, a date for the thing they wanted.
4. **The line you will not move.** Write it down. If the second rehearsal goes better because you moved it, you have softened the finding.

Roleplay: paste the persona of the executive you chose from the [cast](../../labs/module-18/personas/README.md) into a new chat and say you are meeting them privately on Sunday 2 May, before the board. Deliver the finding. Then paste the transcript into the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) with this lesson's "Done when" list as the checklist. Run it twice. The Kinneret rehearsal is the pattern: the first attempt opened with a data-quality table and the owner heard "your factory is a mess"; the second opened with "Arava back to 95%", then a night mould stop and a short-shot story, then what would make the vendor worth buying in 2028, and he asked "so what do I tell the vendor?" — the moment he owned it.

### Step 4 — Build the ten minutes

| Minutes | What | One visual |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | The recommendation in one sentence | Front page |
| 1–3 | The finding that changes the board's view of the problem | One chart or one table |
| 3–5 | Where the problem really is, shown through one case | A time line or a flow |
| 5–6 | The risks the board must know about, and what has already been done about them | One table |
| 6–8 | The recommendation in parts (owner, date, money) and the recommend-against | The table from your case |
| 8–9 | Risks, gates, the stop rule, what a "no" means | Gates |
| 9–10 | The decision asked, in the words of the draft resolution | The resolution |

Rules for the visuals: one point per slide; every number labelled measured, directional or assumed; no logo or real-person data from any source; a chart that a director can read in five seconds. Prepare, but do not show, an answer to every question you can predict: the cost per head, the vendor's response, what the competitors do, what the staff and students will be told, what happens if the gate fails.

### Step 5 — Record it

Record yourself presenting for ten minutes, in one take, as if to the board. Any video-call tool's record button, a phone on a stand, or a free recorder such as OBS Studio is enough. Rules:

- **One take, no edits.** You are scoring the delivery, not the editing.
- **A timer visible to you,** not to the camera.
- **Synthetic content only.** Tzurim is fictional; if you rehearse with colleagues playing the board, ask before you record them, and keep the file to yourself.

### Step 6 — Score the recording

Watch it once without stopping, then score it with the [presentation scoring sheet](../../labs/module-18/presentation-scoring-sheet.csv) (module download): seven criteria, 0–3 each, a timestamp for the evidence of every score, and one change for the next take.

| Criterion | 3 looks like |
|---|---|
| Answer first | The recommendation in one sentence inside the first minute |
| One recommendation | Parts with owners, dates and money; never a menu |
| Evidence labelled | Every number with its label and source |
| Hardest finding | Plainly, once, already owned by the person it concerns |
| Recommend-against | On the front page, with evidence and something to do instead |
| The ask | The decision in minutable words: what, who, when, money, stop rule, re-decision date |
| Time and delivery | Ten minutes or less; slides not read aloud; each answer one sentence first |

Below 15 of 21: change the one thing the sheet names and record again. Most people improve most between the first and second take.

### Step 7 — Take the questions

For the question period, paste the personas of the people in the room (the CEO, the President and the VP Finance from the [cast](../../labs/module-18/personas/README.md)) into one chat with: "Play a board meeting chaired by Dr. Eitan Rozen, chair of Tzurim's board, using the January minutes; each person speaks only from their own prompt; the chair asks for a decision in words that can be minuted at the end." Answer each question in one sentence, then the evidence. When a question is one you cannot answer, say who will answer and by when. End with the draft resolution read back and corrected in the room.

## Watch out

- **Starting with the method.** "We interviewed nine people and profiled six datasets" is minute 1 spent on you. Start with the answer.
- **Delivering the hardest finding for the first time in the room.** The person it concerns hears it in front of their board and defends instead of deciding.
- **Softening between rehearsals.** If the finding changed, you rehearsed a different finding. Keep the line you wrote down.
- **A menu.** "Option A, B or C" hands your judgment back to the board. One recommendation; alternatives belong in the appendix with why they lost.
- **No recommend-against.** A board that hears only "yes" assumes nothing was tested.
- **Ending on "questions?"** End on the decision you ask for, then take questions.
- **Scoring from memory.** Score from the recording, with timestamps.

## Your turn

### Inputs

- Your decision-ready case, the integration check and the register from [18.2](lesson-02.md).
- The [cast](../../labs/module-18/personas/README.md) (the persona of the executive you choose, and the steering group for the questions) and the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md).
- [Presentation scoring sheet](../../labs/module-18/presentation-scoring-sheet.csv) (module download); templates: [decision-ready case](../../templates/decision-ready-case.md), [steering pack](../../templates/steering-pack.md), [decision record](../../templates/decision-record.md).

### Steps

1. Write the recommendation sentence and the draft resolution with stop rule and re-decision date.
2. Fill the hardest-finding table; choose the finding and the executive, and write two sentences on why.
3. Prepare their goal, the evidence, what they can win and the line you will not move; roleplay the private meeting twice, coaching after each run.
4. Build the ten minutes and the predictable-question answers.
5. Record one take; score it with timestamps; if below 15/21, change one thing and record again.
6. Run the question period (group roleplay) and correct the draft resolution in the room.

### Time

150 minutes: 15 for the sentence and resolution, 15 for the choice, 40 for two rehearsals with coaching, 35 for the ten minutes, 30 for recording and scoring (once), 15 for the questions.

### Done when

- [ ] The recommendation is one sentence, and a draft resolution exists with owners, money, a stop rule and a re-decision date.
- [ ] The hardest finding and the executive were chosen from who made which commitment, and the choice is written down.
- [ ] Two private rehearsals were run (or the persona's transcript annotated), the coach's verdict improved, and the finding is word for word the same in both.
- [ ] The recording is ten minutes or less, one take, with the recommendation in the first minute.
- [ ] Every score on the sheet has a timestamp and the sheet names one change for the next take.
- [ ] The question period ended with the resolution read back and corrected.
