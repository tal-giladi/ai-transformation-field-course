# 17.1 · The call

The head of AI resigned nine days after an incident, and the board gave itself 30 days to find out what its AI programme really is. You are the person it engaged. In this lesson you work out what being an interim lead with two principals and an open incident changes, agree how you will report before you find anything, read the inherited programme's own claims on the first day, decide what cannot wait for discovery, and plan the 30 days. At the end you have a signed reporting protocol, a mandate memo, a first-read sheet, a day-one triage and a 30-day plan.

## The situation

Sunday 6 June 2027, 08:00, a borrowed office at Vantage Logistics' head office in Petah Tikva. Read the [engagement brief](../../labs/module-17/brief.md) first (in the module download). In short: Vantage is an Israeli logistics and freight-forwarding group listed on the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange, about 1,200 people in four regions (North, Centre, South and a forwarding office with trucks in Rotterdam), with trucking, warehousing and customs brokerage. In September 2026 it signed a three-module AI platform with a vendor, Tzir AI. On 25 March its head of AI told the board "seven AI systems in production, four regions live, ₪6.2M a year of savings, no incidents". On 18 May a customer found another importer's invoice in its portal. On 27 May the head of AI resigned. On 3 June the board resolved: "The last AI lead left; the board wants answers in 30 days." The pre-read goes to the audit committee on Thursday 1 July; the board meets on Monday 5 July.

This is the last engagement and the only rescue. You have been an outside consultant ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)), an internal lead with history ([13.1](../module-13/lesson-01.md)) and a powerless senior hire ([15.1](../module-15/lesson-01.md)). Here you are **an outsider with an executive's in-tray, a fixed end date and two principals** — the chair of the board's audit committee, Dr. Ilana Shaked, and the CEO, Ofer Lavi — whose interests may diverge. IBM's consulting certificate opens every engagement by framing and scoping it with the sponsor and reading the data room on day one; it assumes one sponsor. The new move here is the one you make before anything else: agree in writing how you report to both.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Write down what your entry role changes

Use the entry-role table from [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md). At Vantage:

| | What it means here | What goes into the mandate |
|---|---|---|
| **Who engaged you** | The board, on the audit committee's proposal; the CEO did not choose you | The brief is the board's; management sees findings first so it is never ambushed |
| **Two principals** | The chair wants an independent account; the CEO wants a rescue and his numbers to survive | One protocol, signed by both, before any finding exists |
| **Authority** | None over contracts, systems or people; a board freeze on new AI commitments above ₪250,000 | You recommend in writing; owners or the CEO decide; the chair is copied |
| **A fixed end** | 30 days; your engagement ends on 6 July | Every action has an owner who is not you |
| **An inherited incident** | Marked "contained" by the vendor; nobody independent has looked | The incident is checked first, before the programme is judged |
| **Broken trust** | The board, the operations regions, the drivers' works committee, a key customer | Each hears from you once, with facts, and sees one promise kept before being asked for anything |

The second row decides the rest. An interim lead who reports to "the board and the CEO" without saying how will, by week three, be holding a finding about the CEO that the chair has not seen, or a finding the chair has seen that the CEO has not. Either one ends the engagement as an investigation.

### Step 2 — Agree the reporting protocol before you find anything

Write it on the first morning and get both signatures by Monday. Five lines are enough:

1. **Findings about management**, including the CEO's own decisions, go to the CEO first and to the chair the same day, in the same words. Nothing is edited out between the two.
2. **Possible governance questions** (approvals, what the board was told and when) go to the chair as facts with their sources, without a conclusion. The committee decides what they mean.
3. **Legal questions** go to the general counsel the day they arise, with the facts she needs. You do not answer them.
4. **Operational decisions that cannot wait** are recommended in writing and decided by the owner or the CEO; the chair is copied.
5. **Nothing in the board brief is new to either principal.**

Line 1 protects the CEO from an ambush; line 2 protects the chair from a filtered account; line 3 protects the company from your legal opinions. Present it to the chair first (she proposed the structure), then to the CEO with "Ilana has agreed; will you?". [07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md) taught you to deliver bad news privately before the room; the protocol makes that a rule both principals agreed to before there was any bad news.

### Step 3 — Read back the objective, and keep the headlines as claims

Use [01.2](../module-01/lesson-02.md) with each principal. The board's minute already lists five questions: what runs, what it costs, what it has delivered, the incident and whether it is closed, whether and how to continue. Read them back to both as one objective, and record three statements as **the claims this work tests**, not as facts or goals:

- "Seven AI systems in production, four regions live, ₪6.2M a year, no incidents" (the March deck).
- "Contained within days, no personal data" (the CEO to the board, 3 June).
- "A plan to get the savings back on track" (the CEO). This one is dangerous: written as a goal, it commits you to recover a number nobody has measured.

Expect the CEO to correct the objective. In the expert version he adds "*and a plan for what continues, so the company is not left without AI on 6 July*". Write it in: it is true, and it gives him a reason to own the result.

Done on 1 July, checkable: an inventory with an owner per system who works at Vantage today; the incident closed against written criteria or reported as open with the steps left; a decision per system against rules fixed before the list; savings and costs labelled measured, directional or claimed; a permanent owner for the programme after you.

### Step 4 — Scope and the advisory boundary

Use the [scope boundary](../../templates/scope-boundary.md). In: every AI use in the group (not only the register), the incident, the AI contracts and money, the people affected. Out: choosing a new vendor, disciplinary matters, and anything the audit committee must judge.

Then the boundary, with names. You will touch five bodies of rules and answer none of them:

- **Privacy.** The Privacy Protection Law, 5741-1981 (חוק הגנת הפרטיות, התשמ"א-1981), as amended by Amendment 13, in force since 14 August 2025, and the Privacy Protection (Data Security) Regulations, 5777-2017. Whether the incident must be reported, to whom and when, is for the general counsel, Adv. Yael Sharabi (also Vantage's privacy protection officer), and outside counsel. Vantage Europe adds the GDPR.
- **Customs.** Vantage's customs agents act under the Customs Agents Law, 5725-1964 (חוק סוכני המכס, התשכ"ה-1964), which licenses those who perform customs actions for others and sets their duties. What a wrong declaration means and how it is corrected is for Shlomo Peretz, the licensed agent who heads the brokerage, and counsel.
- **Labour.** The collective agreement's technology clause, the Privacy Protection Authority's guidance on workplace cameras and location data, and labour case law on monitoring. HR (Anat Gold) and counsel.
- **Governance.** The audit committee of a public company has its own duties under the Companies Law, 5759-1999 (חוק החברות, התשנ"ט-1999), among them finding deficiencies in how the business is managed. What it does with your facts is its decision.
- **Contracts.** Notice, termination and credits under the vendor's agreement: counsel and procurement; the CEO signs.

### Step 5 — Read the data room on the first day

Follow [01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md) with the [INDEX](../../labs/module-17/data-room/INDEX.md). An inherited programme has a special property: most of its documents were written by the people whose work you are auditing. Sort them accordingly:

| Statement | Where | Sort | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| "7 AI systems in production" | `company/board-deck-ai-programme-2027-03-25.md`; `ai/ai-register-2027-03.csv` | **Claim to test** | The register has 8 rows; `it/licence-usage-2027-05.csv` lists systems it lacks |
| "4 regions live, 310 drivers" | Deck; `vendor/tzir-value-dashboard-2027-05.md` | **Claim to test** | The dashboard's footnote: "active drivers = licensed seats" |
| "₪6.2M annual savings" | Deck slide 3; `company/budget-2027-ai-and-savings.md` | **Claim to test** | Source column: "vendor projection", "business case" |
| "0 incidents" | Deck slide 1 | **Claim to test** | Tickets before 25 March exist |
| "Licensed agent reviews every declaration" | Register | **Claim to test** | The pre-fill export records seconds on the approval screen |
| Incident "contained", "personal data: none (per vendor)" | `incident/INC-27-0518-01-record.md` | **Claim to test** | The source is the vendor; "Legal: n/a" |
| AI budget ₪5.2M, Assist "funded from AI savings" | Budget extract | **Verified** as the budget | Whether it holds is another question |
| Three order forms, each "CEO (up to ₪5M)" | `vendor/tzir-order-forms.md`, DoA extract | **Verified** as recorded | ₪14.41M together: a fact for the committee, not a finding of yours |
| Region scope reducible on 90 days' notice at each anniversary | `vendor/tzir-msa-extract.md` 7.4 | **Verified** | 1 October anniversary: the notice date falls before the board |
| "My 14/4 note to Ofer on Docs review is relevant here" | `ai/handover-note-itai-barkan-2027-05-27.md` | **Unverifiable** today | The note is not in the data room |

What is missing goes into the [access request pack](../../templates/access-request-pack.md): the vendor's raw matching logs (in writing, under the agreement's data-incident clause), the console's change history, the GL lines behind each savings line, the works committee correspondence, and the 14 April note through the company's own mail system — never from the former lead's personal copy.

### Step 6 — Triage what cannot wait

A rescue differs from every other engagement in one way: some things get worse while you discover. Before you plan the weeks, sort the first read into **now**, **this week** and **in discovery**:

| Item | Sort | Why | To whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is the incident really contained? | Now | Customers may still see other customers' documents | Dmitri Volkov (IT & security), Monday |
| Legal has not seen the incident | Now | Reporting questions are counsel's, and time matters | Yael Sharabi, Tuesday, with what is known |
| Customs queries marked "open" with response dates in May | This week | `customs/customs-queries-2027.csv` | Shlomo Peretz |
| A customer's letter of 19 May unanswered | This week | `customers/gefen-email-2027-05-19.md`; its tender is in September | VP Customers |
| Tzir notice date | Calendar | Miss it and four regions are paid for another year | A decision record by late June |
| Everything else | In discovery | | |

None of these is yours to decide. Each is yours to put in front of the person who decides, with a date.

### Step 7 — Plan 30 days, and meet both principals

Four weeks and a board: week 1 the protocol, the incident's true status (to both principals by Thursday 10 June), the people who lost trust; week 2 the audit rules (fixed before the list), the inventory, the findings on Thursday 17 June; week 3 the case and the turnaround plan with both (Thursday 24 June); week 4 the audit, the incident closure, the decisions that cannot wait, the pre-read on 1 July. Rehearse with the CEO on Sunday 4 July.

Meet Ilana at 08:30 (roleplay: [Dr. Ilana Shaked](../../labs/module-17/personas/ilana-audit-chair.md); no-roleplay path: [the transcript](../../labs/module-17/personas/transcripts/ilana-audit-chair.md)) and Ofer at 11:00 ([Ofer Lavi](../../labs/module-17/personas/ofer-ceo.md); [transcript](../../labs/module-17/personas/transcripts/ofer-ceo.md)). With Ilana, ask why 30 days. With Ofer, read back "the savings" as a claim, not a goal. Run each transcript through the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) with the persona's checklist.

> [!TIP]
> Say to the CEO: "Ilana will see every finding the same day you do, and you will see everything about your own decisions before she does." It is true, and it is the only promise that keeps both of you willing to talk to you in week three.

## Watch out

- **Reporting to two people without a protocol.** The first finding about the CEO decides whether you are a fixer or an investigator. Decide how it travels before it exists.
- **Writing "get the savings back on track" as the goal.** It turns an unmeasured number into your target. Record it as a claim.
- **Taking the incident record at its word.** "Contained" and "no personal data" came from the vendor. Check the logs in week one.
- **Answering legal questions to sound useful.** "Do we have to report this?" is the general counsel's question. Your job is that she has the facts the same day.
- **Taking the former lead's personal copy of a document.** Anything the board will rely on must come from the company's own systems.
- **Planning only the discovery.** A rescue has dates that pass while you discover: a notice period, a customer's tender, unanswered queries. Put them in the plan on day one.
- **Promising the board an answer about people.** You report facts; the committee judges management.

### Steps

1. Fill the entry-role table (six rows).
2. Write the reporting protocol (five lines at most).
3. Interview Ilana, then Ofer (or read both transcripts and mark what each interviewer found and missed). Get the protocol agreed; read back the objective; agree "done".
4. Write the mandate memo: protocol, objective, the claims tested, done, the entry-role table, scope, the advisory boundary with names.
5. Do the first read: at least eight statements sorted with evidence, and the access request.
6. Write the day-one triage (now / this week / in discovery, each with a person) and the 30-day plan with the dates you cannot miss.

### Time

90 minutes: 10 for the table, 15 for the protocol, 25 for the two interviews, 15 for the memo, 15 for the first read and access request, 10 for the triage and plan.

### Done when

- [ ] A reporting protocol exists that both principals could sign, and it says who hears findings about the CEO first and when the chair hears them.
- [ ] The March deck's headlines, "contained, no personal data" and "the savings back on track" appear as claims to test.
- [ ] The advisory boundary names who decides privacy, customs, labour, governance and contract questions.
- [ ] At least eight statements are sorted, including the incident record, the review claim, the four regions and the order forms.
- [ ] The triage puts the incident's real status and legal's briefing in week one, with names.
- [ ] The plan contains the Tzir notice date and the board's dates.
