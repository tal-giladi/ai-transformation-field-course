# 17.6 · Readout and debrief

Thirty days ago the board asked five questions and the CEO asked for the savings back. You are about to tell the board that most of what it was told in March was not so, that the CEO was warned in April, that the incident is contained but not closed, and that the programme should continue — in parts, with owners. In this lesson you write the brief, check that it holds together, rehearse the hardest finding with the CEO alone, run the board item, record the decision and hand over. Then you see what was really going on at Vantage, what happened six months later on three paths, and how an expert ran the same 30 days. At the end you have the brief, a decision record, a handover and your rubric score.

## The situation

Thursday 1 July 2027: the pre-read goes to the audit committee; the region notice leaves the same day ([17.5](lesson-05.md)). Sunday 4 July, 17:00: an hour with Ofer alone. Monday 5 July, 10:00: the board, item 1, 45 minutes — Yehuda Karni in the chair, Ilana Shaked and five other directors, Ofer and Svetlana attending. Your engagement ends on Tuesday 6 July.

You leave after Monday: unlike every other engagement in this course, you will not run what the board decides. That changes the brief's last page: everything must have an owner who stays. This is module 7 compressed — the [steering pack](../module-07/lesson-01.md), [bad news upward](../module-07/lesson-03.md), [the meeting](../module-07/lesson-04.md) and [handover](../module-07/lesson-09.md). IBM's consulting certificate rewards recommending against when the evidence says so; here you recommend against what each camp in the room wants most.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Answer the board's five questions on the front page

Use the [steering pack](../../templates/steering-pack.md). The board asked; answer in its order, with labels:

| Question | Answer | Label |
|---|---|---|
| What runs? | 12 AI systems (8 in the March register, 4 outside it), audited as 19 lines; the route tool used in the North only | Measured |
| What does it cost? | ₪7.51M in 2027 against ₪5.2M budgeted; ₪7.17M with the stops; about ₪4.86M a year from 2028 | Measured |
| What has it delivered? | About ₪0.76M a year can be evidenced against ₪6.2M booked | Measured / directional |
| The incident: closed? | Contained since 14 June; open until counsel's decisions are recorded and every submitted declaration is checked (four of six criteria met) | Measured |
| Continue? | Yes — system by system, with owners and conditions, and a permanent head of AI | Recommendation |

Then one sentence that carries the recommendation, and three recommend-againsts, each with its evidence:

- **Replacing the vendor now.** About ₪10.0M of fees to the end of the three terms from July, the North works, and the fixes are in the contract. Decide at the November renegotiation on September's numbers.
- **Stopping all AI.** It ends the only measured value (the forecast, AP matching) while the vendor's fees stay payable and the shadow tools carry on unseen.
- **"Getting the savings back on track."** ₪6.2M was never measured; a plan to recover it repeats the error. The board gets measured value every quarter instead.

And what "no" means: the incident stays "contained"; three unused regions are paid for another year; the half-year carries savings the auditor has questioned; the works committee's dispute notice goes out.

### Step 2 — Put the governance facts to the audit committee, without conclusions

Protocol line 2: possible governance questions go to the chair as facts with sources. Write them as a separate section the committee owns. In Vantage's case three:

1. **What the board was told in March,** set against what the data showed on that date (South stopped 25 February; Centre in planner view from 14 March; Europe never connected; ten minor incidents in tickets).
2. **The warning of 14 April** and the reply, with its source (the company's mail system) and the date the CEO read the paragraph.
3. **The three order forms:** dates, values, the total, the approval recorded, and what the delegation of authority does and does not say.

No adjective, no "should have", no inference about intent. In a listed Israeli company the audit committee's role includes identifying deficiencies in how the business is managed and recommending corrections; it can only do that with facts it can test. What it concludes is its business.

### Step 3 — Check that it holds together

Run the [integration check](../../templates/integration-check.md) across the six deliverables. The questions that catch real contradictions at Vantage:

- Does any paper still say "no mismatches since 21 May" (copied from the vendor's analysis) while another says seven?
- Is the North's fuel effect "measured" in one paper and "directional" in another?
- Does the 2027 saving include months a contract does not allow (Nezek's, before August)?
- Does every system have exactly one owner who works at Vantage on 6 July?
- Do the incident numbers match across the status note, the memo, the closure record and the brief?

### Step 4 — Rehearse the hardest finding with the CEO

The hardest finding is Ofer's: **the board was told things in March that were not so, and he was warned in April.** Under the protocol he has already read the paragraph; Sunday is about how he hears it said in the room, and what he will say.

- **Start with what he asked for**, not with the note: "You wanted a turnaround story. Here is the order it happened in."
- **Facts in date order, no adjectives.** 25 February, 14 March, 25 March, 14 April, 2 May, 18 May.
- **Say what the committee will get and what it won't:** facts, sources, no conclusion.
- **Give him his own sentence,** and let him add one of his own to paragraph 2 — as his statement, not edited into the fact.

Roleplay it: paste the [Ofer Lavi persona](../../labs/module-17/personas/ofer-ceo.md) into a new chat, say it is Sunday 4 July and he has read the brief, and deliver it. Run the transcript through the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) with this lesson's "Done when" list. Run it twice; change the delivery, never the finding. The sentence he can say: "We scaled faster than we measured. The incident is contained and will be closed on these criteria. From today every AI system has an owner and a measure, and you will see the numbers every quarter."

### Step 5 — Run the board item

Plan the 45 minutes ([07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)): 5 for the five answers, 10 for the evidence, 20 for questions, 10 to minute the decisions. Prepare one answer for each concern you know is coming: the chair ("is the incident closed or not?"), Ilana ("which numbers survive the auditor?"), a director who wants it all off ("why keep anything?"), Ofer ("Tzir was the right decision"), Svetlana ("what goes into the half-year?").

For a group roleplay, paste [Ilana](../../labs/module-17/personas/ilana-audit-chair.md) and [Ofer](../../labs/module-17/personas/ofer-ceo.md) into one chat with the instruction "play both in a board meeting chaired by Yehuda Karni, who asks for decisions that can be minuted; each speaks only from their own prompt; Svetlana speaks from the cost file". The steering-committee simulation is Harbor & Pine's meeting; play it for the moves — answer first, concede, don't overclaim, ask for the decision:

[Simulation: Steering committee](../../simulations/steering-committee/index.html?preset=harbor)

### Step 6 — Record the decision and hand over

The [decision record](../../templates/decision-record.md): each decision with an owner and date; a **stop rule** (Docs' material error above 5% in any QA month suspends pre-fill until two months under 3%); re-decision dates (the vendor at the November renegotiation; Assist at 30 September); and dissent in the dissenter's words (Ofer: "I accept the restatement. I do not accept that Tzir was the wrong decision; I want the board to judge it in November on the North's numbers.").

The [handover pack](../../templates/handover-pack.md) and the [deliverable map](../../templates/deliverable-map.md): the register and contract owners (the CFO until the head of AI starts; the analyst maintains it); the incident (the incident lead, with the closure criteria); each system's owner; the measures (`discovery.py` and `audit.py` re-run monthly with identical rules); the works committee (the COO and HR); the customer (the VP Customers). Write the date you stop owning anything: Tuesday 6 July.

### Step 7 — The debrief

Score your deliverables with the rubric in the [module project](../../projects/m17-vantage-logistics.md) **before** you open the expert's set, then compare. Open the hidden truth only after your brief is written.

<details><summary>What was really going on at Vantage</summary>

The stated ask was "the last AI lead left; the board wants answers in 30 days", and the CEO's version was a plan to get the savings back. The real problem was that **nothing in the programme had an owner who measured it**, so claims stood in for results at every level — and the incident was the moment the claims met the logs.

- **The incident was not closed and was wider than recorded.** The vendor's release of 2 May stripped letters from invoice numbers before matching, with notice the same morning; 63 declarations were matched to another importer's documents before the fix and 7 after it, because invoice numbers without letters still collided. Switching off the assistant's documents feature left the ordinary portal page showing the same attachments. Seven parcel manifests with 2,462 records of private individuals were opened by other customers. The record said "no personal data" because the vendor said so, and legal never saw it.
- **The review existed on paper.** Agents spent a median 19 seconds on each pre-fill under a KPI raised from 9 to 12 a day; the customs team's own re-check found material errors in 7% of declarations and was sent to the COO, not the board. The vendor's "99.2% accuracy" was the share of fields nobody edited.
- **The CEO was warned.** On 14 April the head of AI recommended pausing batch approval and measuring the review; the CEO replied "Not before the Q2 numbers." The warning was in no register and no deck; it surfaced through one line in a handover note and one phone call.
- **The tool extended to four regions was pulled back.** The South stopped it on 25 February after it routed trucks onto roads closed since October 2023 and late deliveries doubled; Centre went to planner view in March; Europe was never connected. The March deck said four regions live because the COO asked the regions to "keep all four in the numbers until the board", and the vendor's dashboard counted licensed seats as active drivers.
- **The route model learned on pre-war roads and never adapted.** It was trained on 2021 to June 2023 traffic; its adaptation needed the telematics feed, which broke on 14 November when IT rotated a credential nobody updated in the vendor's system. Nobody owned the ticket.
- **The savings were the vendor's arithmetic.** ₪3.1M of fuel came from a naive baseline; ₪1.6M of customs time freed nobody; ₪0.9M of deflection deflected nothing. The CFO's caveat — "subject to validation after six months" — was in her draft and not in the board version. About ₪0.76M was real, most of it from an in-house forecast one data scientist ran alone.
- **The overrun followed from the savings.** A third of the programme was budgeted "from savings" that never existed. Behind it, three order forms signed on three consecutive days, each just under the line above which the board approves, ₪14.41M together, split at the CEO's request so that "the board wouldn't take three months".
- **Trust broke in four places at once.** The drivers' committee had voted to declare a dispute if cameras or scores returned; the key customer had a competitor's offer and a tender in September; the external auditor was coming back to the booked savings in August; the board had been told "seven, four, ₪6.2M, zero".

</details>

<details><summary>Six months later: three futures</summary>

**Future 1 — Blame the vendor, keep the savings.** The CEO's path won the room: Tzir terminated "for cause" in July without the 30-day cure period, the incident closed as the vendor's fault, a new head of AI hired to "restore the savings". Counsel was never given the manifests' scope. By January 2028: Tzir claimed its fees to the end of the terms in arbitration; Customs asked about a further batch of May declarations; Gefen moved its imports to a competitor after the September tender; Shuk Online learned of the exposure from one of its own customers and complained to Vantage in writing; the booked savings were written down in the Q3 report after the auditor's question; the new head of AI resigned in December. Every AI system stopped with the vendor, including the North's routes; the forecast survived only because nobody knew it was AI.

**Future 2 — Switch it all off.** Alarmed, the board stopped every AI system on 5 July, the forecast and AP matching included, and no reduction notice was sent. Customs went fully manual (overtime +22% in the third quarter); Modi'in overtime returned to its 2026 level within six weeks; Tzir's fees, about ₪4.3M a year, stayed payable with nothing running; the customer-service triage on a personal card kept running, out of sight, and grew; the works committee was satisfied; Gefen stayed on a 10% discount. In January the board asked why competitors' AI was "working" and Vantage's was not, and the programme restarted from zero with no inventory.

**Future 3 — Stop/keep with conditions (the path the expert recommended).** The notice left on 1 July; South and Europe came off the invoice on 1 October and Centre kept five planners. Ayin, Nezek and the quote add-in stopped; Kotev went to 150 seats in December. Counsel decided the reporting questions in June (in this story: the Privacy Protection Authority was notified and Shuk Online informed; Shuk Online told the people in its manifests); the brokerage checked all 47 submitted declarations with each importer and corrected those that needed it through the licensed agent. Docs resumed outside Gefen's files on 28 June with the consignee check and a 5% sample: material errors 2.1% in the September QA. The feed was fixed in June and the North retrained on 2024–2027 data in August: overrides fell from 29% to 11%, Kiryat Shmona from 56% to 19%. A works-committee agreement in September: route planning in every Israeli region, no individual scores, 30-day retention, no disciplinary use; Centre restarted dispatch in October. Gefen renewed in September with a quarterly QA report and its ₪85,000 covered. The savings were restated to ₪0.76M in the half-year; the 2027 AI cost closed at ₪7.2M; the audit committee commissioned the internal auditor to review contract approvals, and the board amended the delegation of authority to count all order forms under one agreement together. A permanent head of AI started on 3 October with a governance charter and the three numbers every quarter.

**What decided which future happened:** whether the incident was closed on facts before the programme was judged, and whether "stop" and "keep" were decided per system on evidence rather than for the programme as a whole.

</details>

## Watch out

- **Answering a different question from the board's.** It asked five; answer those, in that order, before your own.
- **Characterising the CEO.** The committee gets dates, sources and his own sentence. "He ignored a warning" is a conclusion the committee may draw; it is not yours to write.
- **Surprising the CEO in the room.** Under the protocol he has read every paragraph about himself. Sunday's rehearsal is about delivery, not news.
- **"Contained" as "closed".** Say which criteria are met and which dates remain.
- **A recommendation for "the platform".** Per system, per region; keep, stop and conditions in the same paper.
- **Handing over to yourself.** You leave on Tuesday. Every line needs an owner who stays.
- **Reading the hidden truth first.** The debrief is worth something only if your brief was written without it.

### Steps

1. Write the front page: the five answers with labels, one sentence, three recommend-againsts with evidence, what "no" means.
2. Write the section for the audit committee: facts, sources, dates, no conclusions.
3. Run the integration check; fix or flag at least three contradictions.
4. Rehearse with Ofer (roleplay twice, coach after each), or annotate his transcript with what you would change.
5. Plan the 45 minutes with an answer to each of the five concerns, or run the group roleplay.
6. Write the decision record (stop rule, re-decision dates, dissent) and the handover with the date you stop.
7. Score yourself with the rubric; then open the hidden truth and the futures; then compare with the expert set.

### Time

180 minutes: 40 for the front page, 20 for the committee section, 25 for the integration check, 30 for the rehearsal, 20 for the meeting plan, 20 for the record and handover, 25 for the debrief.

### Done when

- [ ] The front page answers the board's five questions in order, each labelled, with three recommend-againsts and what "no" means.
- [ ] The committee section contains facts with sources and dates and no characterisation of anyone.
- [ ] The integration check found and resolved (or flagged) at least three contradictions.
- [ ] The CEO read every paragraph about his decisions before the pre-read and has his own sentence for the board.
- [ ] The decision record has owners, dates, a stop rule, re-decision dates and dissent in the dissenter's words.
- [ ] Every line of the handover has an owner who stays after 6 July.
- [ ] You scored yourself before reading the expert set, and wrote one difference per deliverable.
