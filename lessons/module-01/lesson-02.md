# 01.2 · Getting a real mandate

A mandate is not the job title on your contract. It is four things a named person has agreed to in writing: the objective, the money, your authority and what "done" means on a date. In this lesson you prepare and run your first meeting with the sponsor, capture her objective in her own words, read it back, and turn the meeting into the first sections of your engagement charter before the end of the day.

## The situation

Your first morning at Harbor & Pine. Michal Peretz, the COO who hired you, has given you 30 minutes at 09:05 before she walks the warehouse. Everything you will do for the next three months depends on what you leave this room with.

You have already read one document that shapes the conversation: the board minutes of 24 September 2026 (`company/ceo-board-update-2026-09.md`). The CEO committed to three things within 90 days — an AI customer-service bot live on the website, an AI strategy, and 40% lower support cost — and an independent director asked whether 40% was "a target or a forecast". Nobody answered. The minority investor asked for a business case "in the company's standard format". The board meets again on 28 January 2027.

So you have a sponsor (Michal), a sponsor's boss who has made a public promise (Yoav), and a board that has already started asking for evidence. IBM's advisor course says to capture the objective in the sponsor's own words and read it back; this lesson is how to do that in a real 30 minutes, and what to write afterwards.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Prepare for 15 minutes, not 2 hours

Read three things and write three lines:

1. **The trigger:** the board minutes. Line: "The CEO promised a bot, a strategy and −40% support cost by the 28 January board."
2. **The structure:** `company/org-chart.md`. Line: "Michal owns customer service, logistics, purchasing, HR; Yoav owns marketing, sales and the web store."
3. **The money rules:** `company/finance-summary.md`. Line: "₪50k–150k CEO, >₪150k over 3 years board, business-case template above ₪50k, 12% hurdle."

Then write what you must leave the room with. If you leave without one of these, you do not yet have a mandate:

- [ ] The objective, in her words, read back and confirmed
- [ ] What "done" means on 28 January, as things someone could check
- [ ] Money: what she controls, and who approves above that
- [ ] Authority: where you can act, where you can only ask
- [ ] Constraints she cares about
- [ ] A next step with a date

### Step 2 — Use a question plan, in this order

Order matters. Ask about her objective before you mention AI, money before access, and what worries her before you leave. Eight questions fit in 30 minutes if you let her talk:

| # | Question | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In your own words: why did you create this role, and why now?" | Opens with her story, not your plan |
| 2 | "If we fast-forward to 28 January, what would make you say this went well?" | Gets "done" as she sees it |
| 3 | *Read-back:* "Let me read that back: … Did I miss anything?" | Confirms the words; shows you listened |
| 4 | "You said [the bot / the thing]. Is that the goal, or a way to get to the goal?" | Separates the means from the end |
| 5 | "What can I spend without coming back to anyone? And above that?" | Money and thresholds |
| 6 | "Which systems and people can I reach directly? Where do I have to ask?" | Authority |
| 7 | "What would make this fail? What keeps you up at night about [the operation she runs]?" | The worry behind the ask |
| 8 | "What do you need from me, and by when?" | The next step, on her terms |

Question 7 has two halves on purpose. "What would make this fail?" gets the political answer. "What keeps you up at night about *delivery*" (or *the warehouse*, or *the team*) points at the operation she runs every day, and that is where sponsors keep the worry they have not said to their own boss.

### Step 3 — Capture her words, not your summary

Write down what she says, word for word, especially the objective. Be a court reporter, not a consultant. Consultants hear "my people still trust us" and write "maintain stakeholder engagement"; three weeks later nobody recognises the sentence and the sponsor no longer feels it is hers.

In the pre-run transcript (`personas/transcripts/michal-coo.md`), Michal answers question 2 like this:

> "The board sees one thing that already works. They see a plan for the next six months that they
> believe. The numbers are signed by Ronit. And my people still trust us."

The interviewer reads it back as four items, asks "Did I miss anything?", and gets "No. That's it. Write it down exactly like that." That is a confirmed objective. Note what it does *not* contain: the word "bot", the number 40, or the word "AI".

### Step 4 — Ask whether the solution is the goal or a means

The CEO's commitment names a solution (a bot). Your sponsor's objective names outcomes. Ask directly, without attacking the solution: "Does the one thing that works have to be the chatbot?" Michal answers with a question ("What do you think the chatbot is for?"), then: "Fewer angry customers, and a support cost that doesn't keep rising. The bot is Yoav's idea of how to get there. If you find a better way, bring evidence. Yoav has to hear it from evidence, not from us."

Write both halves into the charter. The first sentence is the real goal. The second is your operating rule for the next three months: you will not argue with the CEO; you will bring him data.

### Step 5 — Pin down money and authority with sources

Money questions have three parts: what the sponsor controls, the thresholds above her, and what exists *before* the budget starts. At Harbor & Pine:

| Item | Amount | Who approves | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operations-improvement budget, H1 2027 | ₪120,000 | Michal | Michal, in the meeting |
| ₪50,000–150,000 | — | CEO | `finance-summary.md` |
| Above ₪150,000 over three years | — | Board | `finance-summary.md`, board minutes |
| November–December 2026 | No formal budget | Michal, case by case | Michal |

Authority has three columns: **direct** (Michal's departments and their systems: Priority, Deskly, CloudVoice, the logistics files), **by request** (marketing, sales and the web store, which report to Yoav — "there you ask, you don't take") and **may not** (sign vendor contracts, change production systems; Michal didn't say it, so you propose it and she confirms).

A sponsor who controls no money and no people is a supporter, not a sponsor. If that is what you find, say so in the charter and ask who the real sponsor is before you go further.

### Step 6 — Agree what you need from her

A mandate runs both ways. Ask for three things and write them down:

1. **Time:** 30 minutes a week, same slot (propose Thursday mornings after her warehouse walk).
2. **Unblocking:** when an export or an interview is stuck for more than three working days, she makes one call.
3. **Decisions:** scope changes are decided by her within a week (this becomes the change-request route in 01.3).

Do not ask her to "protect you from Yoav". Ask her to come with you when you take evidence to him.

### Step 7 — Write it down the same day and send the read-back

Before you go home, write charter sections 1–7 ([engagement charter template](../../templates/engagement-charter.md)) and send a short read-back email. It turns a pleasant conversation into a record she can correct:

> Subject: What I heard this morning — please correct
>
> Michal, thank you for the time. In your words, success on 28 January is: the board sees one thing
> that already works, a six-month plan they believe, numbers Ronit has signed, and your people still
> trust us. The bot is one way to get fewer angry customers and a support cost that stops rising, not
> the goal itself. Budget: ₪120,000 for H1 2027 under your control; ₪50–150k needs Yoav; above
> ₪150k the board. I can work directly with your departments and systems, and by request with
> marketing, sales and the web store. I will not commit to any vendor this month. Next: one-page
> 30-day plan to you by Thursday 5 November. If any of this is wrong, tell me and I'll fix it.

Michal's reply ("Correct. Add: no workshops with the support team before February.") is the written confirmation your charter records.

### Step 8 — Record the gap above your sponsor, don't resolve it

Your sponsor's objective and her boss's promise are not the same thing. Michal's "done" can be met without a chatbot and without 40%; Yoav's cannot. Do not try to settle that in week one, and do not let anyone think you have. Write it in the charter under "Open questions" ("The CEO's commitments and the COO's objective differ on the bot and the 40%; to be reconciled with evidence before the January board") and plan the conversation for when you have data (lessons 03.6 and 07.3).

## Watch out

- **Pitching AI in the first meeting.** The moment you describe what you plan to build, the sponsor stops telling you what she needs. Spend the 30 minutes asking.
- **Promising savings before data.** "We can probably get close to 40%" will be quoted at the board. Say "I'll tell you what the data supports by mid-December."
- **Your words instead of hers.** If the charter's objective reads like a consultant's slide, read it back again using her sentences.
- **Accepting the solution as the goal.** If you leave with "deliver a chatbot" as the objective, every later finding that points elsewhere looks like disobedience.
- **Criticising the CEO.** Michal is loyal to Yoav; any hint that his promise was foolish closes her down. Ask about outcomes, not about his judgement.
- **Leaving without a date.** A meeting that ends with "let's stay in touch" produced no mandate. Agree the next deliverable and the day.
- **Not hearing what was not said.** If the sponsor's worry never came up, you have the official mandate only. Ask the operational "what keeps you up at night about …" question before you leave.
