# 12.6 · Readout and debrief

The last week. You have the findings, a decision paper, a draft rule with counsel, a pilot measured on the firm's own documents and a revised business case. On Tuesday 12 January 2027 the management committee reads your paper; on Thursday 14 January twenty-six partners vote on it, and you leave. In this lesson you write the partners' paper, check that it holds together, rehearse the hardest finding with the partner who will resist it, run the meeting, and record the decision and the handover. Then you see what Atlas & Rowe was really dealing with, three futures, and how an expert ran the same engagement. At the end you have the readout pack, a decision record, a handover and your rubric score.

## The situation

Sunday 10 January 2027: the paper goes to the partners. Monday 11 January: private sessions with Shmuel Atlas and then Yoav Dagan. Tuesday 12 January, 08:00–09:30: the management committee (Yoav, Shmuel, Noa Friedman, Orit Levinson, Eli Ben-Hamo). Thursday 14 January, 18:00: the partners' meeting votes; your contract ends that evening. Carmel's panel review closes on 31 January; the insurer's questionnaire was due on 15 January.

This is module 7 in one week: the [steering pack](../module-07/lesson-01.md), [scale, fix or stop](../module-07/lesson-02.md), [bad news upward](../module-07/lesson-03.md), [the meeting](../module-07/lesson-04.md) and [handover](../module-07/lesson-09.md). Two things are different in a partnership. The decision is a vote, so the paper must be divisible into parts that can pass separately. And the bad news is not about one executive's idea; it is about how the partners themselves check work and get paid.

## What to do

### Step 1 — One recommendation, at least one recommend-against

Use the [steering pack](../../templates/steering-pack.md). One recommendation in parts that can be voted and minuted separately:

1. The interim AI rule (Orit's text after outside ethics counsel) from 1 February; one enterprise assistant for all lawyers; consumer accounts on firm work end; consent letters to the four clients with AI terms by 15 February.
2. Knowledge: the 78 research documents curated (done); the 300 most-used precedents by 31 March with a signing partner per group; a librarian from April.
3. The precedent-search pilot with the citation gate in Tech & IP and litigation research, 17 January to 30 April.
4. Pricing: fixed prices for Carmel's repeat work and a quarterly advice subscription, offered by 20 January; Tech & IP packages repriced on measured hours in March.
5. A platform decision in June, after an RFP with a proof of concept on the curated corpus in Hebrew and English.

The recommend-against, on the front page with its evidence: **against any whole-DMS platform contract before June.** Its index would not follow the firm's ethical walls; its own proof of concept failed a basic check on 28 of 80 citations; its 6.2 hours were self-reported at other firms; and with billing unchanged, the model shows −₪3.06M of cash against doing nothing in 2027.

Each part says what a "no" means (consumer tools stay on client documents; the insurer is answered without a rule; Carmel's renewal is negotiated on a discount, not a price model) and what it costs in 2027.

### Step 2 — Check that it holds together

Run the [integration check](../../templates/integration-check.md) across everything since November. The questions that find real contradictions at Atlas & Rowe:

- Does the pilot assume the top 300 precedents are curated by January, while the knowledge plan says March?
- Does the 1.4 minutes per citation (directional) appear anywhere as shekels saved?
- Do consent letters have an owner and a date, or only a sentence in the rule?
- Is the insurer's question about near misses answered with the facts in the findings memo, and before the vote?
- Is every "measured" number in the paper the same number as in the memo, the eval report and the ROI model?

### Step 3 — Rehearse the hardest finding with the partner most likely to resist it

The hardest finding is for Shmuel: *a research memo with an invented authority, a reversed ruling and a misstated one was approved for a client in his group in October; the firm's controls did not catch it; a person did.* He is the partner who warned that AI invents cases. Deliver it privately, before the committee ([07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)):

- **Start with his own words**, not your finding: "nobody checks at midnight" (1 December).
- **State the finding once, as facts, without names**: the memo's four citations and what the corpus says about each.
- **Make the gate his**: a rule for every research answer, with or without a tool, is what he asked for at the partners' meeting.
- **Do not open with the approval.** It is true, and it will make him defend the associate and the firm.

Roleplay it: paste the [Shmuel Atlas persona](../../labs/module-12/personas/shmuel-founding-partner.md) into a new chat, say you are meeting him on Monday 11 January, and deliver the finding. Then run the transcript through the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) with this lesson's "Done when" list. Run it twice; change the delivery, never the finding. Then, the same day, tell Yoav what you told Shmuel: the English Divisional Court said in 2025 that managing partners are among those who must take practical measures against fabricated citations ([Ayinde](https://www.judiciary.uk/judgments/ayinde-v-london-borough-of-haringey-and-al-haroun-v-qatar-national-bank/)); the gate is his to own.

### Step 4 — Run the committee, then the vote

Plan the committee's 90 minutes ([07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)): 10 for the recommendation, 20 for the evidence, 40 for questions, 20 to agree the wording the partners will vote on. Prepare one answer for each concern you know is coming: Noa ("by June the market will have moved"), Shmuel ("who checks the checkers?"), Orit ("consent letters before the pilot, not after"), Eli ("show me the model with my numbers"), Yoav ("what do I tell the partners about Carmel?").

For a group roleplay, paste the five persona files ([Yoav](../../labs/module-12/personas/yoav-managing-partner.md), [Shmuel](../../labs/module-12/personas/shmuel-founding-partner.md), [Noa](../../labs/module-12/personas/noa-tech-partner.md), [Orit](../../labs/module-12/personas/orit-ethics-partner.md), [Eli](../../labs/module-12/personas/eli-finance-director.md)) into one chat with the instruction "play all five in a management committee chaired by Yoav; each speaks only from their own prompt". End by asking for the decision in words that can be minuted, part by part.

### Step 5 — Record the decision and hand over

The [decision record](../../templates/decision-record.md): what was voted, part by part, with the count; the owners; the stop rules; the next decision date (the June partners' meeting); dissent in the dissenter's own words. Then the [handover pack](../../templates/handover-pack.md) and the [deliverable map](../../templates/deliverable-map.md) to the owners: the rule and its history; the curation list and the review cycle; the pilot (system prompt, test sets, scripts, reports); the ROI model and its assumptions; the value measures; and one follow-up call before the pilot gate at the end of April.

### Step 6 — The debrief

Score your deliverables with the rubric in the [module project](../../projects/m12-atlas-and-rowe.md) **before** you open the expert's set, then compare. Open the hidden truth only after your paper is written.

<details><summary>What was really going on at Atlas & Rowe</summary>

The stated ask was a platform choice between two camps of partners. The real problem was that the firm did not know what its own lawyers were doing, what its knowledge was worth, or what its prices would do when work got faster.

- **AI was already in client work, uncontrolled.** At least 52 of 90 lawyers reached consumer AI sites from the office in two months and 27 uploaded files; the firm's survey showed far less. One group had used personal paid accounts on client documents since 2024, reimbursed as "software" by its partner. A client-confidential draft SPA had gone into a consumer tool and been closed quietly. The engagement letter's "technology tools" clause was the kind of general clause the Bar's 2026 opinion, as reported, says is not consent.
- **A fabricated citation had already nearly reached a client.** A junior on loan to litigation, alone at 23:00 with a free chatbot, produced a memo citing a ruling that did not exist, a ruling reversed on appeal and a paragraph that said the opposite. The partner who approved it, "send to the client today", was Shmuel, the partner who had warned the partnership that AI invents cases. A senior associate caught it. Nobody reported it, and the insurer was about to ask.
- **The billable hour.** Research was a quarter of associates' time but six percent of the codes, because partners cut research lines; associates were at 81% of target, so saved hours had no buyer. On hourly work, which was half the fees, a faster firm earned less. Clients already knew: after the NDA automation Carmel moved to a fixed price and took most of the gain, while the firm, without noticing, earned two-thirds more per hour. Carmel's 15% request, which the managing partner had kept from the partners, was an opening position for exactly that model across its repeat work. And 15% of each partner's points depended on personal billed hours, which is why the vote split by practice type.
- **The knowledge was stale.** 71% of the precedent bank had never been reviewed; the export the vendor received contained 13 superseded documents, 2 of them marked. A search over it put the reversed answer in the top three every time. The vendor's whole-DMS index would also have ignored the firm's ethical walls.

The platform was never the decision. The decisions were a rule, the knowledge, the price list and a check that does not depend on who is awake at midnight.

</details>

<details><summary>Six months later: three futures</summary>

**Future 1 — Sign everything.** The partners voted 15 to 11 to sign Parshan before the discount. By February 70% of lawyers had logged in; by May 22% used it weekly, and associates kept their own tools for drafting. In April an answer citing a superseded non-compete ruling reached an employment client; it was corrected before filing. Carmel renewed at 12% off hourly rates and two more clients asked for the same. Billed hours on research-heavy matters fell 9%; the partners whose practices are hourly lost points, and the compensation fight returned in June. The model's column A, about −₪3.1M of cash against doing nothing in 2027, was close to what finance saw by the half-year.

**Future 2 — Curate, control, re-price, then choose (the path the expert recommended).** The rule and one enterprise assistant went live on 1 February; traffic to consumer AI sites fell by four-fifths by March; consent letters went to the four clients with AI terms. Carmel signed fixed prices for twelve contract types and a quarterly advice subscription instead of 15% off. Tamar's sprint finished the 300 most-used precedents in March. In the pilot no citation that failed the gate reached a partner, and Tech & IP's fixed-fee overrun fell from 38% to about 12%; Noa sold the freed hours as packages. The June RFP, with a proof of concept on the curated corpus, chose a platform for 30 seats with permissioned indexing. Year-1 cash was roughly break-even against doing nothing, and the firm had a price model that kept part of the gain.

**Future 3 — Ban it.** Shmuel's motion passed: no AI "until the market settles". Proxy traffic fell for two weeks, then moved to phones. Two more panel tenders were lost to fixed-price competitors; three associates left for a firm with tools. In May opposing counsel found a non-existent citation in one of the firm's court filings, drafted on a phone at night.

**What decided which future happened:** whether the near miss reached the managing partner and the ethics partner before the vote, and whether pricing was on the table next to the tool.

</details>

## Watch out

- **A menu for a vote.** Three options with pros and cons hand the decision back to 26 people. Give one recommendation in parts they can vote on.
- **The recommend-against in an annex.** If the platform question is not on the front page, it will be decided by the discount date.
- **Ambushing the sceptic.** The near miss delivered first in the committee is a fight about the associate; delivered privately, starting from his own words, it becomes his control.
- **Keeping the client's request from the partners.** The managing partner's instinct was to avoid "a grenade". A vote on AI without the pricing pressure is a vote on the wrong question.
- **Leaving with the knowledge.** If the owners cannot re-run the gate and the ROI model without you, the handover failed.
- **Reading the hidden truth first.** The debrief is only worth something if your paper was written without it.

### Steps

1. Write the paper's front page: one recommendation in parts, the recommend-against with evidence, the decision asked part by part, what a "no" means, the 2027 cost including the customer's share.
2. Run the integration check; fix or flag at least three contradictions.
3. Rehearse the hardest finding with Shmuel (roleplay twice, coach after each) or annotate his transcript with what you would change; brief Yoav the same day.
4. Run the committee (group roleplay) or write the 90-minute plan with an answer to each of the five concerns.
5. Write the decision record and the handover outline.
6. Score yourself with the rubric; then open the hidden truth and the futures; then compare with the expert set.

### Time

180 minutes: 40 for the paper, 25 for the integration check, 30 for the rehearsal, 30 for the committee, 25 for the record and handover, 30 for the debrief.

### Done when

- [ ] The paper has one recommendation in votable parts and a recommend-against on its front page.
- [ ] The integration check found and resolved (or flagged) at least three contradictions.
- [ ] The near miss was delivered privately first, without names, starting from the partner's own words; the second rehearsal was better than the first by the coach's account.
- [ ] The decision record has the vote per part, owners, dates, stop rules, the next decision date and any dissent.
- [ ] The handover names owners who can run the gate, the curation cycle and the ROI model without you.
- [ ] You scored yourself before reading the expert set, and wrote one difference per deliverable.
