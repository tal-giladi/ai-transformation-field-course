# 07.4 · The steering meeting

The pack decides what the committee knows; the meeting decides what it does. In this lesson you run Harbor & Pine's steering meeting of 19 January 2027: plan the 45 minutes, prepare an answer for each person's concern, handle the moment your sponsor argues for a bigger number than your evidence, and leave with a decision that can be minuted. You practise it in the steering-committee simulation and in a group roleplay with the five members. At the end you have a meeting plan, the minutes and the follow-ups that close what the meeting left open.

## The situation

Tuesday 19 January 2027, 10:00, the board room, 45 minutes. The pack went out on Sunday at 15:00 with the measured case first and Michal's upside labelled for September ([07.1](lesson-01.md), [07.3](lesson-03.md)). On Sunday Yoav chose his board sentence ("we replaced a vendor's figure with our own"), but in a room he still wants the programme to sound big. Around the table: Yoav (chair), Ronit, Michal, Avi and Samir. The board pre-read goes out on Thursday 21 January, so the minutes of this meeting are what the board will read on the 28th.

IBM's governance course ends a review with "ship, ship with conditions, or wait"; its executive-communication course gives answer first, evidence vs inference and live options; its consulting capstone rewards a recommendation against where the evidence says so. None of them puts you in the room when the CFO asks how it was measured and the sponsor answers with a bigger number. This lesson does.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Plan the 45 minutes

A steering meeting that starts with background ends without a decision. Plan it to the minute and hold the last five for the decision:

| Minutes | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | The ask: what, how much, conditions, the board | You |
| 3–8 | Three points: what was measured, what it is worth (cash, then capacity), the weak line | You |
| 8–38 | Questions, in the order the chair takes them | Members; you answer |
| 38–43 | The decision, asked in words that can be minuted, read back | You ask; the chair decides |
| 43–45 | Actions and owners; what goes to the board | Chair, you |

Bring one page, not slides: the cover note and the gate table. Every number you might be asked for (the per-language figures, the cost lines, the stop rules) sits in the pack with a page number you can say aloud.

### Step 2 — Prepare an answer for each concern you know is coming

You know each member's concern from your one-to-ones. Write the answer before the meeting, in three parts: the evidence (measured), the inference (what you conclude, labelled), and, where the evidence is thin, the concession tied to a condition.

| Member | The question you will get | Prepared answer |
|---|---|---|
| Yoav | "What do I tell the board on the 28th?" | The sentence he chose on Sunday, said back to him in the room: the cause found and fixed without AI, the pilot next with our own numbers, the vendor's figure replaced |
| Ronit | "Whose budget does it land in, and how was it measured?" | ₪17,000 translation (invoices, Customer Service licences); ₪96,000 temps (Samir's temp line, directional, signed plan); minutes timed on the test set, not in the queue; "that's why the ₪72,000 waits for 1 April" |
| Michal | (not a question) "I'd expect closer to ₪500,000 at full scale" | Step 4 |
| Avi | "Who supports it at two in the morning, and where does customer data go?" | The data terms the tool was approved under; the switch; the gap said aloud ("today the answer is you, and that is not fair") and turned into a condition with a date |
| Samir | "Is this about cutting my team?" | "No cuts are in this case. The only cash from people's time is not hiring two temps next winter, and that was your decision." |

The concession is the strongest move you have. A committee trusts the parts of a case it has seen you give up; a gap you name first becomes a condition, and a gap the CFO finds becomes a reason to send the paper back.

### Step 3 — Answer with the right move, not the comfortable one

Every answer you give in the room is one of a few moves. The simulation names them, and so should you:

| Move | What it sounds like | When it is right |
|---|---|---|
| Answer first | "We recommend X. Today I'm asking for Y." | The opening, and every answer to "so what?" |
| Evidence, then inference | "Measured: 82% on 400 emails. Inferred: production will be close, which is why gate 1 measures it." | Any question about a number |
| Concede, with a condition | "The minutes come from a test set; that is why the second stage waits." | A weak point you know about |
| Reframe, with credit | "There is more upside, and phase 2 tests it; the paper holds us to what we measured." | The sponsor argues past the evidence |
| Overclaim | "On track for the 40%." | Never: it buys a smile now and a CFO's folder later |
| Defer | "Let's take that offline." | Only for detail that changes nothing today; never for a number that will be minuted |

### Step 4 — When the sponsor argues past the evidence

Michal backed you for three months, and now she says, in front of the CEO and the CFO, that she expects ₪500,000 at full scale. You have three bad options and one good one. Agreeing puts an unmeasured number in the minutes. Correcting her bluntly ("that number is not supported") is true and costs you the sponsor. Deferring ("let's take it offline") lets the number into the CEO's notes, which is how it reaches the board. The good one keeps the number and her face at the same time: "Michal is right that there is more upside, and phase 2 is designed to test it. In the paper ₪113,000 is the expected case; anything above it is an assumption we test at the September gate, so the board holds us to what we measured." Then give her the credit and the job: she owns the September test.

If you slipped and corrected her bluntly, repair it in the same meeting: name what she did for the evidence ("Michal pushed for the shadow-mode run that gives us these numbers") before you move on. If the number got in anyway, take the next opening (here, Ronit asking which number goes to the board) and put it back where it belongs.

### Step 5 — Ask for the decision in words that can be minuted

At minute 38, ask for exactly what the pack asked for, every part of it, and end with a question that has a yes:

> "Approve phase 1 with conditions: ₪48,000 now; ₪72,000 at the 1 April gate if the four conditions in the pack are met; Arabic drafts reviewed by Arabic speakers and re-measured before 1 March; out-of-hours support before go-live; roadmap v1.1 as on page 5; the case to the board on the 28th. Can we minute that as approved?"

Then read the minute back before anyone stands up. Two failures are common: "approved in principle" (nobody can release a PO on it), and a decision the pack asked for that never got asked for in the room, so it is not minuted and someone will later say it was never approved.

### Step 6 — Close the loop the same day

Minutes to the members by 17:00, in five lines: decision, money, conditions, actions with owners and dates, what goes to the board. If anything was not minuted that should have been, say so in the cover email and ask for a written yes ("The pack also asked for roadmap v1.1; we did not take it in the room. Unless anyone objects by Wednesday 12:00, I'll record it as approved."). Then update the board pre-read so it says exactly what the committee decided, and send Yoav his slide.

### Practise it

The simulation runs this meeting as a branching conversation with the five members, scores support and honesty, and shows "six months later" for each outcome. Play it at least twice: once as you would really run it, once taking the comfortable answers (agree with Michal, tell Yoav "on track") to see where they lead.

The simulation follows the course story: the date fix went live on 14 January, and gate 1 is the charter's four conditions. The lesson's point: say only what you have measured, and ask for a decision that can be minuted.

## Watch out

- **Background first.** Twelve minutes of discovery history and the chair asks "so what are you asking us for?". Ask in the first three minutes.
- **"Let's take it offline" for a number.** It stays in the room, in somebody's notes, and reaches the board. Label it now.
- **Correcting the sponsor bluntly and moving on.** True, and expensive: she stops copying you on board drafts. Reframe with credit, or repair in the same meeting.
- **Overclaiming to the chair.** "On track for 40%" makes the meeting easier and the September gate impossible.
- **A concession with no condition.** "You're right, it's not measured live" without "that's why the second stage waits" sounds like weakness. Tie every concession to a gate or a condition.
- **The forgotten second decision.** Read the minute back against the pack's list of asks before people leave.

### Steps

1. Write the 45-minute plan and the five prepared answers (Step 2 table), each with evidence, inference and concession.
2. Write your Step 4 sentence for the sponsor's ₪500,000, and your repair line if you slip.
3. Play the simulation twice, once honestly and once with the comfortable answers; note the outcome and the "six months later" of each.
4. Run the group roleplay (about 30 minutes) or read the transcript; then run the coach. Transcript path: list the two slips and what each cost.
5. Write the minutes in five lines and the same-day follow-up email, closing anything that was not minuted.

### Time

90 minutes: 20 for the plan and prepared answers, 25 for the simulation, 30 for the roleplay or transcript and coach, 15 for the minutes and follow-up.

### Done when

- [ ] The ask is in the first three minutes and five minutes are kept for the decision.
- [ ] Each member's concern has a prepared answer, and at least one weak point is conceded with a condition.
- [ ] The sponsor's number is labelled as an assumption to test, with her credit and her test, not agreed, ignored or deferred.
- [ ] The decision covers every ask in the pack, in words that can be minuted, and was read back.
- [ ] The minutes and the follow-up went out the same day; nothing the pack asked for is left unrecorded.
