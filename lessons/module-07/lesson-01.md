# 07.1 · The steering pack

A steering committee decides from what it reads in the first two minutes and from whether the papers agree with each other. In this lesson you turn the decision-ready case you wrote in module 3 into Harbor & Pine's steering pack for 19 January 2027: the same ask, now carrying the pilot's test-set evidence, every number relabelled, every document stating the decision it supports, who can stop the work and what a "no" would mean, and an integration check that catches the contradictions between your own documents before the CFO does. At the end you have a pack your sponsor can review and the committee can decide from.

## The situation

Thursday 14 January 2027. The date fix went live this week after a clean dry run. Ronit signed ROI model v3 this morning ([03.7](../module-03/lesson-07.md)). The eval report of 7 January ([05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md)) says "proceed to shadow mode on 1 February" with one condition on Arabic. The steering committee (Yoav in the chair, Ronit, Michal, Avi, Samir) meets on Tuesday 19 January at 10:00 for 45 minutes; its papers go out on Sunday morning, two working days before. The board meets on 28 January and its pre-read ([03.9](../module-03/lesson-09.md)) is due on 21 January, so whatever the committee minutes on Tuesday is what the board will read. Michal asked to see the pack today, before it goes to anyone else.

IBM's business-case course calls the front page a decision-ready case; its executive-communication course ends with integration, finding the contradiction that lives only at the boundary between two sound documents. Here you do both for a pack that is going to be voted on.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Write the decision line before anything else

Every document in the pack opens with three lines, and so does the pack:

> **Decision this supports:** [what the committee is asked to decide, by when]
> **Who can stop it:** [the named people who can pause or end the work, and on what trigger]
> **What a "no" means:** [what happens on Monday if the answer is no]

For Harbor & Pine's pack:

> **Decision:** approve phase 1 of the reply-drafts pilot on 19 January: ₪48,000 now, ₪72,000 at gate 1 on 1 April 2027 if (a)–(d) are met, and send the case to the board on 28 January.
> **Who can stop it:** Samir (owner) or the pilot lead, the same day, on any stop rule in the charter; the committee chair at gate 1; the board on 28 January.
> **What a "no" means:** no drafts on 1 February; WhatsApp still moves into the helpdesk (it is plain integration, already approved); Russian and Arabic agents stay without a drafting tool through spring, at about 13 minutes per careful Russian reply; the two temps are hired again next November (₪96,000); the test set and harness are kept for a later decision.

Two lines in that block do work that a summary never does. "Who can stop it" tells a nervous committee member that stopping is designed in, which makes a yes cheaper. "What a no means" stops the meeting from treating "no" as free: a no is also a decision, with costs, and the committee should take it knowingly.

Write the same three lines at the top of the charter, the eval report and the roadmap extract in the annex, each in its own words. The eval report's decision line, for example, says it supports "proceed to shadow mode on 1 February" and that a "no" means another month on the test set.

### Step 2 — Update the decision-ready case with the pilot evidence

The front page of 14 January (03.9) was written before the eval was rated. Go through it claim by claim and relabel. The rule from module 5 holds: an offline test is **measured** about the test set and **directional** about production.

| Claim on the front page | Label before | Evidence now | Label now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80% of drafts accepted | assumed (alignment session) | 82% of 261 rated drafts; he 86, en 85, ru 79, **ar 72 (interval about 58–83%)** | measured on the test set; directional for production |
| 2 review minutes per draft | assumed | 1.9 minutes median *rating* time at a desk | directional (rating is not reviewing in a queue) |
| About 109 net hours a month at peak | measured on a sample (time study) | 117 hours at 2.0 review minutes; 92.8 at the gate's 2.5 | directional until the February timer readings |
| Wrong replies no higher than today | assumed | 4 safety issues in 261 drafts; agents today give a past date in 63.6% of WISMO replies | directional; "today" must be re-measured after the date fix |
| Peak hours cover both temps (320 h) | directional | pilot about 117 + date fix about 120, not yet measured = about 237 | **assumed; covers one temp on today's evidence** |
| ₪17,000 a year from translation | measured (invoices) | unchanged | measured |

Two consequences go on the front page, not in an annex. First, the Arabic figure: 72% passes the 70% bar, but its interval runs down to 58%, so the eval's condition (Arabic drafts reviewed by Arabic speakers only, and re-measured on 100 production drafts before 1 March) becomes a condition of the approval. Second, gate 1 (d): today's evidence covers one temp, not two. The ROI model already says what happens then ("re-presented with one temp before stage 2"); the pack says it again in one sentence, so nobody hears it first on 1 April.

### Step 3 — Assemble the pack in reader order

A committee reads in this order, so build it in this order, and keep the body to six pages:

1. **Cover note** (half a page): the decision line; what is in the pack; the two things you most need the committee to look at; when the minutes go to the board.
2. **Front page**: the updated decision-ready case, conservative scenario first ([decision-ready case](../../templates/decision-ready-case.md)).
3. **Gate table**: gate 1 (a)–(d), what the offline test shows, its label, what production must show, who measures it ([05.4](../module-05/lesson-04.md) reference answer).
4. **Conditions, stop rules and owners**: the six stop rules from the charter ([05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md)) with the person who presses each switch.
5. **Open items with owners and dates**: counsel's answer on using past messages (R-11, due 15 January), the privacy notice update, out-of-hours support, the load rule in the integration.
6. **Changes you are asking for** beyond the money, each a separate line the committee can approve or refuse (here: roadmap v1.1, Step 4).
7. **Annexes**: ROI model v3, eval report v1.0, pilot charter v1.0, roadmap extract, DPIA summary, the integration check.

The order is not cosmetic. Assembly runs from evidence upward; delivery runs from the answer downward. A pack that starts with "Background: eight weeks of discovery" asks the chair to find the decision on page five.

### Step 4 — Run the integration check across your own documents

Each document was right when it was written. Read them side by side, question first: for every number or date the committee will rely on, does every document say the same thing? Log each contradiction with the documents involved, which one is right now, and who fixes it ([integration check](../../templates/integration-check.md)). Harbor & Pine's check on 14 January found five:

| # | Contradiction | Documents | Resolution before the meeting |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Roadmap v1.0 starts Russian and Arabic drafts four weeks after Hebrew and English; the charter, the eval and the training plan assume all four languages from 1 February | Roadmap X1 vs charter scope vs eval verdict | Put to the committee as a separate decision: **roadmap v1.1**, all four languages from 1 February with a native-speaker spot check twice a week per language and a stop rule per language (the Russian and Arabic agents are the ones most likely to drift back to free chatbots) |
| 2 | The readiness assessment speaks of a 200-message test set; the eval used 400 plus 200 for development | Readiness (02.6) vs test-set spec (05.2) | Update the readiness entry; the pack cites 400 |
| 3 | Charter: 109 net hours a month at peak; eval: 117 at 2.0 minutes, 92.8 at 2.5 | Charter vs eval | The pack keeps 109 as the plan and shows the range with its label; no third number |
| 4 | Front page: gate (d) "covers the temps"; ROI model: "one temp covered, the second not yet" | Front page vs ROI v3 | Front page corrected to the ROI model's sentence |
| 5 | The DPIA lists the processing as "email and web form"; from 1 February WhatsApp contacts become helpdesk tickets and run through the pilot in shadow mode | DPIA vs release plan | DPIA addendum to counsel through Ronit before 29 January; listed as an open item |

Two rules make the check worth doing. Trace every human control to a cost line and a person: the Arabic spot check in contradiction 1 costs Lina about 45 minutes a day, which the ROI model counts under "control"; if it did not, the pack would promise a check nobody pays for. And when a contradiction changes what the committee decides, disclose it to the sponsor before the meeting; a quiet fix takes the decision away from the people who own it.

### Step 5 — Write what each answer would mean

Committees often end with "approved in principle", which nobody can execute. Give the chair the answers that can be minuted, each with its Monday:

| Answer | What happens next |
|---|---|
| Approve with conditions | ₪48,000 released; PO to the integrator on 20 January; the case goes to the board on 28 January; stage 1 on 1 February |
| Approve without conditions (all ₪120,000) | Not recommended: nothing can stop the second stage if review time creeps up; the ROI model's gate logic is lost |
| Send back | Name what would change the answer and the date; anything after 28 January moves shadow mode into the summer and gate 2 past the next peak |
| No | As in the decision line: no drafts, temps hired again, test set kept |

### Step 6 — Pre-wire, then send

Send the draft to the sponsor first, with the two things that changed since 03.9 marked: the Arabic condition and the one-temp sentence. Then talk to each member who has a stake in one line before the papers go out: Ronit (the relabelled numbers), Samir (the stop rules he owns and the no-cuts line), Avi (out-of-hours support). None of them should meet a surprise on Tuesday. Yoav gets his own conversation, because the pack contains news about his number (lesson [07.3](lesson-03.md)).

## Watch out

- **The old front page with a new date.** If the numbers are the ones from before the eval, the CFO will notice that "80%" is now 82% with a 72% inside it. Relabel claim by claim.
- **The pack as an archive.** Forty pages with the decision on page five is a pack nobody decides from. Body six pages; everything else is an annex with a page reference.
- **No "what a no means".** A committee that thinks "no" is free will say it, or will say "in principle", which is worse. Price the no.
- **Fixing a contradiction quietly.** Changing the roadmap's language order in the pack without saying so takes a decision away from the committee. Put it on the agenda as its own line.
- **Two numbers for one thing.** 109, 117 and 92.8 hours in three documents read as three claims. Keep one as the plan and show the others as its range, labelled.
- **A condition with nobody paying for it.** A spot check, a review step or an out-of-hours line that has no hours and no budget behind it is a promise. Trace it to a cost line before you write it as a condition.

### Steps

1. Write the decision line (decision, who can stop it, what a no means) for the pack and for each of the charter, the eval report and the roadmap extract.
2. Relabel every claim on your front page with the eval evidence (Step 2 table); add the Arabic condition and the one-temp sentence.
3. Run the integration check across at least five documents and log every contradiction with its resolution and owner.
4. Assemble the pack in reader order, body six pages at most, with the cover note, gate table, stop rules, open items and the separate line for roadmap v1.1.
5. Write the "what each answer means" table.
6. Read it as Ronit for two minutes (or ask an LLM chat primed as "a CFO who checks every number against its source"): can she find the ask, the money, the weak line and what a no costs?

### Time

120 minutes: 15 for the decision lines, 25 for the relabelling, 35 for the integration check, 30 for assembly, 15 for the answers table and the read-through.

### Done when

- [ ] The pack and each main document open with the decision, who can stop it and what a "no" means.
- [ ] Every number on the front page carries its new label; offline results are never called production results.
- [ ] The Arabic condition and the one-temp sentence are on the front page, not only in an annex.
- [ ] The integration check lists at least five contradictions, each resolved, and the ones that change a decision are separate agenda lines.
- [ ] Every condition traces to a person and a cost line.
- [ ] The body is six pages or fewer and the ask is in the first five lines.
