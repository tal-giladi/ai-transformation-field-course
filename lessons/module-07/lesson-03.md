# 07.3 · Delivering bad news upward

Bad news rarely gets blocked; it gets softened, one reasonable edit at a time, until the people who decide hear something that is no longer true. In this lesson you get your steering paper back from your sponsor with the bad news edited out, find what each edit did, rewrite the paper so the finding survives without humiliating anyone, talk to the sponsor before the paper goes out, and then tell the CEO, who promised the board 40%, that the measured case is about 3%. At the end you have a rewritten paper, two conversations behind you and a board sentence the CEO can own.

## The situation

This lesson steps back two and a half months from gate 1. It is Friday 15 January 2027, 22:40. You sent Michal the steering pack yesterday ([07.1](lesson-01.md)); it goes to the committee on Sunday and the committee meets on Tuesday 19 January. Michal's reply: "Good work, really. I've made it read better for Yoav. He needs something with energy for the 28th." Her version is in the module download, `labs/module-07/steering-paper-softened.md`. The title now reads "AI customer service pilot validates the path to our 40% target".

Yoav told the board on 24 September that support cost would fall by 40% (₪1.36 million a year). On 2 December he agreed, one to one, that the chatbot would wait and that the story for the board would be "fix the dates first, then a measured AI pilot" ([03.9](../module-03/lesson-09.md)). What he has not yet heard plainly is what the measured case is worth: about ₪113,000 a year in cash, about 3% of support cost, with one weak line inside it. Michal mentioned "82%, above target" to him in a corridor on Thursday.

IBM's executive-communication course names the pattern (organisational silence: every relay has more to lose by passing bad news up than by softening it) and the answer to it: lead with the decision and the date, land the hard finding once without hedging, label evidence and inference, leave two or three live options. It does not practise the part that is hard: the edit comes from your own sponsor, and the person with the number is the CEO.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Read the softened version edit by edit

Do not argue with the whole paper. Put each edit in a row and write what a committee member or director will now believe, and what is actually true:

| Edit | What the reader now believes | What is true | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title: "validates the path to our 40% target" | The pilot is evidence for 40% | The measured case is about 3% of support cost | The board hears the September number confirmed |
| "82% accepted, exceeding the 80% target" | Production performance is above target | 82% on a test set of last summer's emails; directional for production | Gate 1 looks already passed |
| Arabic 72% → "minor tuning ongoing for Arabic" | Arabic is fine | 72% with an interval down to 58%; a condition of approval | The condition disappears from the decision |
| "Agents review a draft in under two minutes" | Review time is measured | 1.9 minutes of *rating* time at a desk; no queue, no load | Gate (b) looks met before it is measured |
| "About ₪113,000 in year one, growing to around ₪500,000 a year at full scale" | ₪500,000 is a forecast | No model, no source; never measured | The bigger number is the one the board will remember |
| "Covers our seasonal staffing needs, so no temporary staff next winter" | Both temps are covered | Evidence covers one (about 237 of 320 hours) | The weak line of the case is now its strongest claim |
| "The support team is enthusiastic", with Olga's quote | Adoption is a given | One enthusiast's quote; training is in three weeks | Adoption risk vanishes; Olga is named in a board paper without being asked |
| "No material risks. Privacy has been reviewed and approved" | Privacy is closed | Counsel answered on Friday; privacy notice update and the DPIA addendum for WhatsApp are open | Open items with owners and dates become "none" |
| "WhatsApp and further channels to follow" | WhatsApp is a later extension | About 87% of written contact; untested | The biggest unknown is a footnote |
| Ask moved to the last line: "We propose to continue" | No decision is needed | A decision on ₪48,000, ₪72,000 at a gate, and the board | The committee may not minute anything that can be executed |

None of these edits is a lie on its own. Together, they turn "a measured pilot with one weak line" into "the 40% is on track". That is how softening works, and why you read it line by line.

### Step 2 — Separate what you can give from what you cannot

The sponsor's concern is legitimate: Yoav reads one page, the board meeting is in thirteen days, and a paper that reads like an apology helps nobody. Give her what serves that concern, and keep what the decision needs.

- **You can give:** a shorter cover note; a headline with energy that is true ("We fixed the cause first; the AI pilot starts with numbers we measured ourselves"); her upside in the paper, labelled as an assumption to test at the September gate; the appendix for everything that is not decision-relevant.
- **You cannot give:** the measured number first; the weak line on page one; the labels (measured, directional, assumed); the Arabic condition; the open privacy items with owners; the ask in words that can be minuted.

### Step 3 — Rewrite it answer first

Four moves, in this order, each once:

1. **Lead with the decision and the date.** "The committee is asked on 19 January to approve phase 1: ₪48,000 now, ₪72,000 at gate 1 on 1 April if four conditions are met, and to send the case to the board on 28 January."
2. **Land the hard finding once, unhedged.** "The measured case is about ₪113,000 a year in cash, about 3% of support cost. It does not deliver the 40% presented in September, and no evidence we have says it will."
3. **Keep evidence and inference apart.** What was measured (82% on 400 real emails; Arabic 72%), what is inferred (production will be similar; review in a queue will be close to rating time), what is assumed (the second temp; anything above ₪113,000).
4. **Leave live options with their costs.** For the board story: the measured case and the date fix; or the measured case with a labelled long-term ambition; or the date fix now and the AI case in April. Each with what it costs the CEO.

Write the hard finding once. Repeating it three times reads as an attack; wrapping it in three paragraphs of praise first reads as permission to ignore it.

### Step 4 — Talk to the person who softened it, first

Never send your version over the sponsor's head, and never let the CEO hear about the disagreement before she does. Book her before the paper goes out (here: Sunday, 08:30, coffee, her office):

- Start with her goal, in her words: "You want Yoav to walk into the board with something strong. So do I."
- Name the risk to *her*, not the rule you would break: "If the board hears 40% on track and Ronit's model says 3%, the person who signed the paper is you."
- Show the two or three edits that change the decision (the title, the temps line, the ₪500,000), not all ten.
- Offer what you can give (Step 2), and a role: her upside goes in, labelled, and she owns the September test of it.
- Agree who tells Yoav what. Here: you tell him the number at 13:00; she is welcome to join; the paper goes at 15:00 as rewritten.

If she refuses, say what you will do and when ("I will send my version at 15:00 and say in the cover note that you asked for changes I could not make; I would much rather not"). You will rarely need to; a sponsor shown the risk to her own name usually moves.

### Step 5 — Tell the person with the number, privately, before the room

In a group, a leader defends the number he has committed. Alone, with options, he can change it. Yoav gets twenty minutes on Sunday (persona prompt and scene below), and the five moves from 03.9 apply with two additions for a number already committed:

- **Find the chain.** Ask, without accusation, who has already heard a number from him since September, and what exactly. A board member, a parent who chairs the board, an investor, a vendor: each needs the same correction, and the correction is easier the earlier it happens.
- **Answer the question he fears.** Here it is Orna Sela's "a target or a forecast?". Give him the true answer in a sentence he can say: "It was a target from a vendor's figure; we have replaced it with a forecast from our own data."

End with something he owns (the sentence, the options he chose) and something you owe him (the slide by Wednesday). Do not ask him to admit in front of the board that he was wrong; the board needs evidence and a plan, not a correction of the CEO.

### Step 6 — Write down what was agreed, the same day

Two short notes after the conversations: to Michal ("as agreed this morning: paper at 15:00 with the ₪113,000 first, your upside labelled for the September gate"), and to Yoav ("as agreed: board sentence; slide by Wednesday 20 January; I'll draft a two-line note for your conversation with the chair"). A softened paper usually comes back once more, the evening before; the note is what you point to.

## Watch out

- **Fighting the whole document.** Ten objections read as a grudge. Show the two or three edits that change the decision.
- **Going over the sponsor's head.** Sending your version to the committee or the CEO before talking to her costs you the sponsor for every later decision.
- **The finding said three times, or wrapped in praise.** Once, plainly, early. Then options.
- **Correcting the CEO in writing.** The paper reports the measured case; it does not say that September was wrong. The method answers Orna; nobody needs to be blamed.
- **Accepting "the numbers are in the appendix".** The appendix is where a committee does not read. The decision-relevant numbers belong on page one.
- **Forgetting the relays.** If the CEO told the chair or an investor, the board slide alone does not correct it.
- **Naming a frontline employee in a board paper as proof of adoption.** Ask first, and use data instead.
