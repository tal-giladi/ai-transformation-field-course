# 16.6 · Readout and debrief

Four weeks are over. You have closed the exposures, the funder has agreed a shorter report, two workflows have been tried by their users and one page of rules is ready. On Wednesday evening the board gives you 20 minutes on Zoom, and on Thursday you leave for good. In this lesson you write the one-page pre-read, check that everything you wrote agrees, rehearse the hardest finding with the executive director, run the board item, record the decision and hand over what Yossi must do each month. Then you see what Open Hands was really dealing with, three ways the next six months went, and the expert's deliverables. At the end you have the pre-read, a decision record, a handover and your rubric score.

## The situation

Week 4. Sunday 6 December: the pre-read goes to the board. Monday 7 December: the Hanukkah party, the staff training, and afterwards 30 minutes alone with Ronit. Wednesday 9 December, 19:30: the board on Zoom, item 2, 20 minutes; the chair (Avi Shalev) presides, the treasurer (Vova) has read the money list. Thursday 10 December: two hours of handover with Yossi and Ronit, and then you are gone. You have eight hours this week.

This is module 7's readout compressed into one evening: [the steering pack](../module-07/lesson-01.md), [scale, fix or stop](../module-07/lesson-02.md), [bad news upward](../module-07/lesson-03.md), the [operating rhythm](../module-07/lesson-06.md) and [handover](../module-07/lesson-09.md). IBM's consulting certificate rewards recommending against when the evidence says so; at Open Hands the recommend-against is aimed at the board chair's expectation that the answer is "buy AI".

## What to do

### Step 1 — One page, one recommendation, one decision

Use the [steering pack](../../templates/steering-pack.md) front page, shrunk to what a volunteer board reads on a phone before a Zoom call:

- **The decision asked**, first, in minutable words: "Adopt the one-page rules (v1) for staff and volunteers, and approve no new spending: cancel ₪4,497 a year of unused or risky subscriptions; any AI purchase comes back in March with evidence."
- **What we found**, one sentence: people already use AI, often on personal accounts and sometimes with family and donor details; the shared drive was open far wider than the policy says; most of the writing burden came from one funder's report format.
- **What was done in four weeks**, four lines, with numbers: exposures closed (`Families` 46 → 9 accounts; the care-sheet link off; donor sheet 14 → 4 editors); the funder's shorter format agreed (≈ 6 hours per coordinator in the last report week; ≈ 2 expected); two tools on accounts Open Hands already has, at ₪0, with their test results; one page of rules.
- **The recommend-againsts, on the face of the page**: no paid AI tool now; GrantGenie; any AI with donor data; a chatbot for families.
- **What a "no" means**: rules stay unwritten; personal accounts "carefully"; subscriptions renew.
- **Still open**, with an owner and a date: the lawyer's answers (January).

### Step 2 — Check that it all agrees

Run the [integration check](../../templates/integration-check.md) across the charter, findings memo, diagnosis, boundary paper and build report. At Open Hands the contradictions are small and embarrassing, which is exactly why they matter in front of a treasurer:

- Does every document give the same account counts (46 before, 9 after) and the same money (₪4,497; ₪2,072 for Microsoft)?
- Does the pre-read say "test results" without saying they came from 10 and 12 items rated by the users, with one fail each fixed?
- Does any page still say the case database "is now at the basic security level"? (It is a question for counsel.)
- Does the monthly check in the handover have a name, a day and 15 minutes — or is it "IT will monitor"?
- Is the normal-week time log (7–11 December) owned by someone after you leave?

### Step 3 — Rehearse the hardest finding with the person it concerns

The hardest finding is Ronit's own: the September newsletter story was written by her, with her personal ChatGPT, from a coordinator's notes, about a family on the old consent form, and the mother found out from a neighbour. It is in the findings, without names. It cannot be left out of the board item, and it must not be delivered to her for the first time in front of her board.

Deliver it to her privately on Monday ([07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)):

- **Start with what the mother asked for**, not with consent statistics: an apology, in Arabic, from someone senior; to read any story first; to know who can see her son's file.
- **State the facts once**, as facts: the newsletter text, the consent version, how she found out.
- **Offer her the sentence for the board**, in her own voice: "In September I published a family's story without asking them. I have apologised to the family. The new rules mean it cannot happen again."
- **Do not soften the finding to protect the relationship.** Change the delivery as many times as needed.

Roleplay it: paste the [Ronit persona](../../labs/module-16/personas/ronit-executive-director.md) into a new chat, say you are meeting her after the Hanukkah party on 7 December, and deliver the finding. Run the transcript through the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) with this lesson's "Done when" list. Do it twice.

### Step 4 — Run the 20 minutes

Plan it: 2 minutes for the decision asked; 5 for what was found and done (Ronit presents; she opens with her own sentence); 8 for questions; 5 to get the decision minuted. Prepare one answer each:

- **Avi (chair):** "So are we doing AI or not?" — "Yes: on our own accounts, for two jobs, with rules; and we stopped paying for what we don't use."
- **Vova (treasurer):** "Every subscription, with an owner and a renewal date." — the list, attached.
- **A board member who is a parent:** "Will coordinators lose their jobs?" — "No. The hours go back to families; nobody's position depends on this."
- **Avi again:** "Isn't this too cautious?" — "The March decision is the ambitious one; it will rest on three months of use instead of a conference."

For a group roleplay, paste [Ronit](../../labs/module-16/personas/ronit-executive-director.md) and [Vova](../../labs/module-16/personas/vova-treasurer.md) into one chat with the instruction: "play both, plus the board chair Avi Shalev, who came back from an AI conference wanting Open Hands to be ambitious; each speaks only from their own prompt." End by asking for the decision in words that can be minuted.

### Step 5 — Record the decision and hand over 15 minutes a month

The [decision record](../../templates/decision-record.md): what was decided, the owners and dates, the next decision (March), and any dissent in the dissenter's words. Then the [handover pack](../../templates/handover-pack.md), sized to Yossi's Tuesday — the [operating rhythm](../../templates/operating-rhythm.md) of a 30-person organization is one checklist:

1. Sharing report: anything "anyone with the link"? Any personal account on `Families` or the donor sheet?
2. Leavers this month (from Liat and Galina): suspended?
3. Card statement: any new subscription without Vova's line?
4. The two Gems: still on the version in the change log?
5. Three questions to two coordinators and Noa: did you use it, for what, did anything go wrong?

Add where everything is (the page, the two instruction texts, the test sets to re-run after any change, the numbers sheet, the story bank, the subscription list, the lawyer's questions) and one call in March. A volunteer leaves on a date; the checklist is how the work survives the date.

### Step 6 — The debrief

Score your deliverables with the rubric in the [module project](../../projects/m16-open-hands.md) **before** you open anything below. Then open the hidden truth, the futures, and the expert set.

<details><summary>What was really going on at Open Hands</summary>

The stated ask was "help us use AI, we have no budget". Open Hands did not have an AI *adoption* problem. It had an AI *already-in-use* problem with the most sensitive information it holds, and a writing workload created mostly by one funder's template.

- **AI was already in use with family and donor data, on personal accounts.** Two coordinators pasted case notes, with children's first names, ages and diagnoses, into personal Gemini or ChatGPT to turn Hebrew notes into English narratives and Arabic letters. The head of development paid ChatGPT Plus on the organization's card (coded "office supplies"), uploaded the donor sheet — with ID numbers and notes about donors' own disabled children — to segment the Giving Tuesday campaign, and signed up for a "free for nonprofits" grant tool with her personal email, uploading past proposals and the funder reports with family narratives. The executive director wrote the September newsletter story herself, with her own ChatGPT, from a coordinator's notes. On personal accounts, the vendor's own page says chats may be used to improve its models and some are reviewed by people and kept for up to three years.
- **Access control predated AI and was the real exposure.** The `Families` drive had 46 accounts where 9 roles needed it: 31 personal accounts (23 of them volunteers who had stopped volunteering) and 2 former staff. Children's care sheets had been open to anyone with the link since a respite weekend in August 2024, the link pinned in every WhatsApp group. The donor sheet had 14 editors, including a former employee's private Gmail. One person administered everything, with 2FA on his personal phone.
- **One funder's template drove the writing.** The Adler-Haddad Foundation's quarterly report asked for 120 family narratives in English; the programme officer read the first ten. Open Hands' own outcomes framework, adopted in 2024, measured the same things and had never been used. Nobody had asked the funder.
- **Consent did not cover what was done.** Two thirds of families were on a 2019 consent form with no story clause. The newsletter told Adam's story — first name, age, town, school and syndrome — and his mother learned of it from a neighbour.
- **There was money.** About ₪4,500 a year was being spent, or about to be, on Microsoft seats nobody used after the grant ended, ChatGPT Plus on the card, a Dropbox nobody opened and a grant tool about to convert to paid. "No budget" was true about new money and false about money.

The fixes that mattered most were not AI: an afternoon in the admin console, a phone call to a funder, an apology in Arabic.

</details>

<details><summary>Six months later: three futures</summary>

**Future 1 — Tools first.** The board liked a demo of the grant tool Noa already had ("free for a year"); it became the grant tool. Coordinators were told "use Gemini, but anonymise", with no access fix and no sanctioned account. In January the grant tool converted to paid; nobody noticed until March. In April a former volunteer's Gmail, still on the family drive, was phished; the care sheets had been readable by link anyway. A parent complained to the Privacy Protection Authority, and Open Hands could not say who had had access. The funder renewed, with a condition: a data-protection review at Open Hands' cost (₪18,000). Coordinators stopped using anything; the writing load did not change.

**Future 2 — Boundary first, then two workflows (the path the expert recommended).** Access was fixed in week 2 and staff were given the work-account alternative the same afternoon. The funder accepted the short format for Q4 and wrote it into the 2027 renewal. In the January report crunch coordinators spent about 2 hours each on the funder report instead of about 6.5. By March four of six coordinators used the case-note helper for letters in Arabic and Russian; Amharic letters stayed with Tigist; two coordinators kept writing notes as before. GrantGenie was cancelled before it converted; Microsoft went to 3 seats. In March, after three months of Noa's use data, the treasurer approved one line: two nonprofit seats for development, ₪602 a year, paid from the savings. The lawyer's answers came in January; consent v3 went out in February, and 71% of families had re-consented at their next contact by June. Lina approved a story about Adam, in Arabic and English, with no town and no school. Yossi ran the 15-minute check every first Tuesday; in May it caught a new volunteer added to `Families` by mistake.

**Future 3 — Policy only.** The board adopted a five-page AI policy copied from a template; nothing changed in Drive; people kept using personal accounts "carefully"; nobody called the funder. Every staff member signed the policy; none could say what was in it. Nothing bad happened by June, which the board took as proof that it worked. The exposure was exactly what it had been in November.

**What decided which future happened:** whether the access fix and the sanctioned alternative came on the same afternoon, and whether someone with a name had 15 minutes a month to keep it true. *For your debrief:* what would you measure in June to tell future 3 from future 2, given that in both "nothing bad happened"?

</details>

## Watch out

- **Giving the board a demo instead of a decision.** A demo answers "can it?"; the board needs to decide "may we, how, and who keeps it true?".
- **The recommend-against in the small print.** If "no paid tool now" is not on the page, the March decision happens in January by enthusiasm.
- **Delivering the sponsor's own mistake in the room.** The same finding, given to her first in private with the mother's words, becomes her sentence, not your accusation.
- **"IT will monitor."** At Open Hands, IT is a person on Tuesdays. Name him, the day and the 15 minutes.
- **Leaving with the knowledge.** If Yossi cannot re-run the test sets after a change to the instructions, the handover failed.
- **Reading the hidden truth first.** The debrief is only worth something if your readout was written without it.

### Steps

1. Write the one-page pre-read: decision asked first, one sentence found, four lines done, the recommend-againsts, what "no" means, what is open.
2. Run the integration check; fix or flag at least three contradictions.
3. Rehearse the hardest finding with Ronit twice (roleplay and coach), or annotate her transcript with how you would deliver it.
4. Run the 20 minutes (group roleplay) or write the plan with an answer for each of the four questions.
5. Write the decision record and the handover, including the five-item monthly check.
6. Score yourself with the rubric; then open the hidden truth and the futures; then compare with the expert set.

### Time

150 minutes: 30 for the pre-read, 20 for the integration check, 30 for the rehearsal, 20 for the board, 20 for the record and handover, 30 for the debrief.

### Done when

- [ ] The pre-read asks for one decision first, in minutable words, and the recommend-againsts are on its face.
- [ ] The integration check found and resolved (or flagged) at least three contradictions.
- [ ] The hardest finding reached Ronit privately first, starting from the mother's words, and the board heard it from her.
- [ ] The decision record has owners, dates, the March decision and any dissent.
- [ ] The monthly check has a name, a day, 15 minutes and five questions Yossi can answer alone.
- [ ] You scored yourself before reading the expert set, and wrote one difference per deliverable.
