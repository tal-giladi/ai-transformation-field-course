# 11.1 · The call

The mayor's chief of staff wants "a citizen chatbot like other cities have" for a conference in ten weeks, and has decided it is yours because your job title has "digital" in it. You have no budget, no authority over the departments that write the content, and every purchase you might make is public and regulated. In this lesson you work out what being the accidental owner changes, get a real mandate from the director-general, read the data room on the first day, and plan the first week. At the end you have a signed-ready mandate memo, a first-read sheet, an access request and a five-day plan.

## The situation

Sunday 29 November 2026, 07:30, your desk in the Information Systems Division of the Givat Oren municipality. Read the [engagement brief](../../labs/module-11/brief.md) first (in the module download). In short: Givat Oren is a mixed city of about 221,000 residents who speak Hebrew, Arabic, Russian, Amharic and English at home. You have run Digital Services (website, forms, app content; four people) for four years. On Thursday night Eyal Ben-Haim, the mayor's chief of staff, emailed: "The mayor wants a citizen chatbot like other cities have … launch it at the residents' conference on 4 February … You're our digital person, so it's yours … Can you send me a plan by Sunday?"

There is a ₪600,000 "smart municipal chatbot" line in the draft 2027 budget, added at his request and not yet voted. A company called CivicTalk has offered "a free pilot". The director-general's office has given you thirty minutes on Monday.

You have met the three ways in at [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md): the internal lead, the consultant, the accidental owner. At Northwind ([08.1](../module-08/lesson-01.md)) you were an outsider with a contract; at Meridian ([09.1](../module-09/lesson-01.md)) a hired internal lead with a role. Here you are the third: the subject landed on you. IBM's consulting certificate would call the line between what you answer and what you route your *advisory boundary*; in a municipality that line runs through law at every step.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Write down what being the accidental owner changes

Use the entry-role table from [01.1](../module-01/lesson-01.md) and fill it for Givat Oren before you write a plan:

| | What it means here | What you write into the mandate |
|---|---|---|
| **Who gave the ask** | The mayor's office. It sets priorities; the director-general runs the administration and the divisions | The mandate comes from the director-general; the mayor's office is informed on a fixed rhythm |
| **Budget** | None of your own. The ₪600,000 line is a draft, and not yours | "No commitments by the project lead"; small purchases through the treasurer |
| **Authority over the content** | None. Every division publishes its own pages | Content owners named by the director-general, one per division |
| **Every purchase is regulated** | A municipality contracts by public tender unless the regulations give an exemption; a financial commitment binds the city only when the mayor and the treasurer sign it | "No supplier receives data or a promise"; all vendor contact minuted |
| **Everything you write is public** | The municipality is a public authority under the Freedom of Information Law | Write factually; no adjectives about colleagues; no junior staff named |
| **You stay** | Whatever you launch, you will maintain or explain for years | Every part has an owner who is not you |

The legal anchors, so you can say them correctly: the Municipalities Ordinance (פקודת העיריות) section 197 requires a public tender for goods, services and works, and section 203 says a commitment with a financial obligation binds the municipality only when the mayor and the treasurer sign it; the Municipalities (Tenders) Regulations, 5748-1987 (תקנות העיריות (מכרזים)) set the exemptions and the small-purchase amounts, which are linked to the consumer price index and updated every month. The Freedom of Information Law, 5758-1998 (חוק חופש המידע) lists local authorities among the public authorities it covers (section 2). You are not giving legal advice: you are making sure your plan does not assume a power you do not have. Every reading of these laws goes to the legal adviser.

The last row is the one accidental owners forget. A consultant leaves on a date; you will be the person residents and journalists ask about the chatbot in 2028.

### Step 2 — Answer the person with the ask, and go to the person with the authority

Eyal asked for "a plan by Sunday". Do not send one. A plan for a chatbot accepts the conclusion before anyone has looked. Send a short, warm reply that gives him something true to tell the mayor tonight and moves the decision to where it belongs:

- you are taking it seriously and will bring options for what the mayor can announce on 4 February, by a date;
- you are asking the director-general for a mandate so the administration owns it;
- nothing goes live without the legal adviser seeing it;
- one question: was anything agreed with or sent to CivicTalk?

Then prepare for Ronit Shemesh, the director-general. The method is [01.2](../module-01/lesson-02.md): the objective in the sponsor's words, read back, then "done". Two objectives exist, and the mandate holds both without pretending they are the same:

- **The stated ask** (chief of staff, for the mayor): "A citizen chatbot like other cities have", live on 4 February. Record it as *the claim this work tests*.
- **The objective** you propose for the director-general to correct: find out why residents don't get correct answers about permits and fees, and test whether a change, including an AI assistant, can answer them correctly in the city's languages; bring a recommendation and something true the mayor can announce.

"Done" must be checkable: a minuted decision at the management forum on 2 February; every number labelled measured, directional or assumed; a named owner for each part of the decision; any assistant tested on the city's own questions against gates fixed before the run; nothing in front of residents without the legal adviser.

### Step 3 — Scope that keeps the cause in view

Use the [scope boundary](../../templates/scope-boundary.md) from [01.3](../module-01/lesson-03.md). Three hard calls in Givat Oren:

- **Which topics.** "Citizen questions" is everything a city does. Take **permits and fees** (parking, signs, cafés, events, skips, building archive, bulky waste, business-licence first steps, small works, reasons and objections): they have rules that can be written down, they change, and they are a large share of 106's work. Arnona and enforcement are out, with a reason (different owners and law) and a date to revisit.
- **Decision letters.** They belong to the Licensing and Permits Division, not to you. Put them in "as far as they drive contact", or you will discover in week 2 that the cause is outside your mandate.
- **Purchasing.** Out. You prepare; the treasurer and the legal adviser decide the route.

### Step 4 — Read the data room on the first day

Follow [01.5](../module-01/lesson-05.md): inventory every file, note where it came from, list what is missing, and sort the statements. Open the data room's [INDEX](../../labs/module-11/data-room/INDEX.md). Two facts change how you read the rest:

- Moked 106's export is **every contact of a random 4% of caller numbers** (June–October), so a resident's repeated contacts stay together. Multiply by 25 for the city.
- The permits export is **complete** for 2026, and the website's permits pages are in `content/site/` exactly as published, one file per page and language.

Then sort. A worked example from Givat Oren's files:

| Statement | Where | Sort | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| "First-contact resolution 83%, above target" | `service/moked-kpi-report-2026-q3.md` | **Claim to test** | Footnote 1: transfers, tickets and scheduled callbacks count as resolved |
| "Callbacks within SLA 96%" | Same report | **Contradicted as a service measure** | Footnote 3: *scheduled* within two days; completion is elsewhere (`callbacks.csv`) |
| "Arabic-language service maintained" | Same report | **Claim to test** | The export has the language chosen and the language served |
| 2026 fees; leased cars eligible from 1 July | `city/council-decision-fees-2025-12.md`, `permits/parking-permit-procedure-v4.md` | **Verified** (as decisions) | Now compare them with what residents read |
| Second-car permit ₪90; leased cars "not eligible" | `content/site/PRK-ar.md` (Arabic page) | **Contradicted** | The council decision says ₪120 and eligible with a letter |
| "The municipality does not use AI systems" | `governance/foi-response-2026-09-ai.md` | **Contradicted** | `content/cms-pages.csv`: a "botnest-widget" script on three pages |
| "94% accuracy", "30 authorities", "no tender: framework" | `procurement/civictalk-proposal.md` | **Claim to test** | Conditions unknown; `framework-catalogue-extract.md` lists CivicTalk under forms and payments |
| Accessibility "level AA, all pages and documents" | `governance/accessibility-statement.md` (2021) | **Contradicted** | 2023 audit: 22 open issues, 23 scanned PDF forms |
| Repeat contacts | — | **Unverifiable** (never measured) | Possible from the hashed caller ids |

What is missing goes into the [access request pack](../../templates/access-request-pack.md): the quality unit's tests of 106, the desk's actual checklist, the translation vendor's order history, any correspondence with CivicTalk (through the legal adviser), and whether the director-general has anything in writing about 106 that you should know.

### Step 5 — Plan the first week around three places

The stated ask can be tested in three places, so the first week has to reach all three before anyone sees a plan:

- **The authority:** the director-general on Monday, mandate signed or corrected.
- **The front line:** Moked 106 and the parking-permits desk, where residents' questions are actually answered. Watch the work, don't ask about chatbots.
- **The data and its definitions:** the CIO for exports and for what is embedded in the website; the quality unit for any test of answers.

And the people who could stop it later: the legal adviser (before any vendor meeting), the works committee (before any proposal), the mayor's office (every two weeks, in writing, copy to the director-general). Use the [30-60-90 plan](../../templates/30-60-90-plan.md) shortened to ten weeks.

### Step 6 — Get the mandate

Meet Ronit (roleplay: [Dr. Ronit Shemesh](../../labs/module-11/personas/ronit-director-general.md); no-roleplay path: [the transcript](../../labs/module-11/personas/transcripts/ronit-director-general.md)). What you want: the objective read back and corrected in her words; done and scope agreed; owners in the divisions; data access; a slot at the forum; how the mayor's office is kept informed. Ask what she herself is accountable for this year. Then meet Eyal (roleplay: [Eyal Ben-Haim](../../labs/module-11/personas/eyal-chief-of-staff.md); [transcript](../../labs/module-11/personas/transcripts/eyal-chief-of-staff.md)): find out what triggered the ask, what the mayor needs to be able to say on the night, and exactly what was agreed with CivicTalk. Afterwards run each transcript through the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) with the persona's checklist.

> [!TIP]
> The government's *Guide to the responsible use of AI in the public sector* (National Digital Agency with the Ministry of Justice, version 1.0, May 2026) recommends that senior management name a Chief AI Officer and decide each AI project's risk track. It was written for public-sector entities and, as far as this course found, does not bind a municipality; it is still the best shared language you have with a director-general. Mention it once; don't lecture.

## Watch out

- **Sending the chief of staff a chatbot plan by Sunday.** You will have accepted the conclusion and made the mayor's office your sponsor. Reply warmly, give him a date and a sentence for the mayor, and go to the director-general.
- **Treating "free" as outside procurement.** A free pilot is still an engagement: data, access, a head start. Nothing is agreed until the people who can commit the city say so.
- **Scope as "all citizen questions".** You cannot fix arnona, welfare and permits in ten weeks. Pick a family of questions with written rules, and say why.
- **A mandate from the wrong person.** The mayor's office can make something urgent; it cannot assign division heads' time. Without the director-general, every content owner can say no.
- **Writing as if no one else will read it.** Your first-read sheet will one day be an FOI request. Facts, sources, no judgements of colleagues.
- **Ignoring the people who can stop it later.** The legal adviser, the works committee and the accessibility coordinator are cheaper to meet in week 1 than in week 9.

## Your turn

### Inputs

### Steps

1. Fill the entry-role table for Givat Oren (six rows).
2. Write the reply to Eyal (at most eight lines).
3. Interview Ronit (or read the transcript and mark what the interviewer found and missed). Read the objective back; agree done, scope, owners, access and the forum slot.
4. Write the mandate memo: objective, the stated ask as a claim, done, entry-role table, scope with reasons, advisory boundary with named people, reporting rhythm.
5. Do the first read: every file with its provenance, at least eight statements sorted with evidence, and what is missing.
6. Write the access request and the first-week plan, with the risks to the work.

### Time

90 minutes: 15 for the table and the reply, 20 for the interview, 25 for the mandate, 20 for the first read, 10 for the access request and plan.

### Done when

- [ ] The objective is the director-general's, read back; the chatbot appears as the claim tested.
- [ ] Done is a minuted forum decision, labelled evidence, named owners, gates set before any test, and the legal adviser before anything public.
- [ ] The memo says you commit no money and promise no supplier anything, and names who decides the procurement route.
- [ ] The advisory boundary names the legal adviser, the treasurer, the CISO, HR and the works committee for the questions you will not answer.
- [ ] At least eight statements are sorted with evidence, including FCR, the callback SLA and the FOI answer.
- [ ] The first week reaches the director-general, the front line and the data before Eyal gets anything but a reply.
