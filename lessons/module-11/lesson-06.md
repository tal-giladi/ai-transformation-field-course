# 11.6 · Readout and debrief

Two days before the mayor stands in front of 600 residents, the management forum decides what Givat Oren will actually do. You have the findings, the case, the constraints paper, an evaluated assistant and a register entry. In this lesson you write the readout, check that it holds together, rehearse the hardest finding with the chief of staff in private, run the forum, and record the decision and the handover. Then you see what was really going on in Givat Oren, what happened six months later on three different paths, and how an expert ran the same engagement. At the end you have the readout, a decision record, a handover and your rubric score.

## The situation

Thursday 28 January 2027: the pre-read goes out. Sunday 31 January: thirty minutes with Eyal Ben-Haim, alone. Tuesday 2 February, 08:30–09:30: the management forum (Ronit Shemesh in the chair; Eyal, Samer Haddad, Orna Ziv, Larisa Gurevich, Eli Saban, Dmitri Kogan, the treasurer; Moshe Azulay invited for the item on 106). Thursday 4 February, 19:30: the mayor speaks at the residents' conference.

Unlike a consultant, you do not leave on Thursday. You go back to running the website, and whatever the forum decides becomes part of your job or someone else's. This is module 7 compressed: the [steering pack](../module-07/lesson-01.md), [scale, fix or stop](../module-07/lesson-02.md), [bad news upward](../module-07/lesson-03.md), [the meeting](../module-07/lesson-04.md) and [handover](../module-07/lesson-09.md). IBM's consulting certificate rewards recommending against when the evidence says so; here the recommend-against is aimed at the mayor's own idea, two days before his speech.

## What to do

### Step 1 — One recommendation, a recommend-against, and a sentence for the mayor

Use the [steering pack](../../templates/steering-pack.md). One recommendation, in parts, each with an owner and a date:

1. The knowledge pack published as the website's permits-and-fees section in four languages by 1 March, each page with its owner and effective date; any council or division change updates it within five working days.
2. New decision letters from 1 March (the condition, the document, the objection route, in the applicant's language); the 33 leased-car refusals since 1 July re-examined under the current procedure.
3. Callbacks reported on completion and permits-family repeat contacts reported monthly, from February.
4. The assistant to 106 agents in shadow mode on 15 February, live for agents on 15 March if it holds the gates; the register entry published on 1 March.
5. The re-labelled ₪600,000 line used for two Arabic-speaking agents and the content and translation work; no chatbot licence in the first half of 2027.

The recommend-against, on the front page, with its evidence: **against a public chatbot on 4 February, and against the "free pilot" in any form.** An assistant reading today's website carries an outdated fee or rule in 17 of 93 answers on our own questions (illustrative) because 9 of 24 permits pages are wrong, mostly in Arabic, Russian and English (measured); the free pilot is a ₪522,000 commitment nobody authorised and a head start before any tender (legal adviser).

And the sentence, agreed with the chief of staff before the forum: *"From 1 March, every answer from the city in your language, every refusal with its reason, and every callback counted until it is made; we publish the numbers every month. Our 106 team is testing an assistant in Hebrew, Arabic, Russian and English. It goes to residents when it passes."* Every clause is something the decision record makes true.

### Step 2 — Check that it holds together

Run the [integration check](../../templates/integration-check.md) across everything since November. The questions that catch real contradictions in Givat Oren:

- Does every document give the same publication date for the content (the case said "by 15 February"; translation takes six weeks)?
- Do the assistant's 58 hours appear anywhere as money?
- The register entry says "updated when a rule changes": who is told when the council changes a fee, and who updates the pack?
- Does the mayor's sentence promise anything the decision record does not give an owner and a date?
- Is every "measured" number the same in the memo, the case and the readout?

### Step 3 — Rehearse the hardest finding with the person who made the ask

The hardest finding is for Eyal: *no chatbot for residents on 4 February, and his October email to CivicTalk is the procurement problem the legal adviser had to close.* Deliver it to him privately on Sunday, before the room ([07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)):

- **Start with his question, not your finding.** In November he asked what the mayor can say on the night. Give him the sentence first.
- **Show, don't argue.** The Arabic parking page with ₪90 and "not eligible"; three answers the crawl design gave from it.
- **State the procurement point once, as done.** "Samer has written to CivicTalk; nothing more is needed from you."
- **Give him something to own.** A monthly slide for the mayor with the four numbers.

Roleplay it: paste the [Eyal Ben-Haim persona](../../labs/module-11/personas/eyal-chief-of-staff.md) into a new chat, say you are meeting him on Sunday 31 January, and deliver the finding. Run the transcript through the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) with this lesson's "Done when" list. Run it twice; change the delivery, never the finding.

### Step 4 — Run the forum

Plan the 60 minutes ([07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)): 5 for the decision asked, 15 for evidence, 25 for questions, 15 to minute the decision. Prepare one answer for each concern you know is coming: Eyal ("what does the mayor say?"), Samer ("the register entry is still a draft"), Larisa ("my agents can't absorb shadow mode and the new measures in the same month"), Eli ("who re-examines the 33 refusals, and on what authority?"), Dmitri ("who owns the pack after you?"), the treasurer ("what is the ₪600,000 actually buying?"), Moshe ("put the training and no individual monitoring in the minutes").

For a group roleplay, paste the files of [Ronit](../../labs/module-11/personas/ronit-director-general.md), [Eyal](../../labs/module-11/personas/eyal-chief-of-staff.md), [Samer](../../labs/module-11/personas/samer-legal-adviser.md), [Larisa](../../labs/module-11/personas/larisa-moked-head.md), [Dmitri](../../labs/module-11/personas/dmitri-cio.md) and [Moshe](../../labs/module-11/personas/moshe-works-committee.md) into one chat with the instruction "play all six in a management forum chaired by Ronit; each speaks only from their own prompt". End by asking for the decision in words that can be minuted.

### Step 5 — Record the decision and hand over to people who will still be here

The [decision record](../../templates/decision-record.md): what was decided, owners, dates, stop rules, the next decision date (1 June: public pilot or not), and any dissent in the dissenter's own words. The [handover pack](../../templates/handover-pack.md) goes to the Resident Service Division (business owner) and the CIO (technical): the [deliverable map](../../templates/deliverable-map.md), the knowledge pack and its owners, the test set, gates and scripts with "re-run after every rule change", the monthly measures, the register entry and its review date, the works committee letter and training plan, and the legal adviser's open question on reliance. Write down when you stop being the assistant's product owner, and who takes over.

### Step 6 — The debrief

Score your deliverables with the rubric in the [module project](../../projects/m11-givat-oren.md) **before** you open the expert's set, then compare. Open the hidden truth only after your readout is written.

<details><summary>What was really going on in Givat Oren</summary>

The stated ask was a citizen chatbot "like other cities have". The real problem was that **residents could not get one correct answer, in their language, and a reason they could use**, and nobody in the city owned the answers.

- The council changed fees and the parking rule for 2026, and the Hebrew pages were mostly updated. The Arabic, Russian and English pages were maintained by a translation vendor on request, and nobody had ordered an update since 2023: 9 of 24 permits pages contradicted the council, mostly in those languages, and six pages still linked the 2025 fee table. 40.7% of the site's pages had no named owner.
- Moked 106 answered from the website and from an unofficial fee sheet with 2025 figures. Agents were right on three in four questions whose rule had not changed and on four in ten where it had. Arabic callers reached an Arabic speaker four times in ten; after 90 seconds the queue moved them to a Hebrew speaker.
- The "83% first-contact resolution" counted scheduled callbacks as resolved; a third of callbacks were never made. A third of permits-family contacts were the same resident coming back within two weeks.
- The permits desk kept applying a rule the council had abolished: leased cars were refused under the 2024 checklist, with a code that said "does not meet the criteria (section 3 of the procedure)", in Hebrew only. Amal Nassar was one of 33.
- AI was already talking to residents: a clerk's free chatbot, answering from 2025 documents, always in Hebrew, collecting ID numbers on a platform with no contract; and a clerk's phone app translating letters. The city had just told a journalist it used no AI.
- The chief of staff had told a vendor in October to "go ahead with the free pilot"; the vendor had crawled the website, old pages and all. A chatbot launched on 4 February would have answered Arabic speakers from the 2023 Arabic pages: New York's MyCity, in a mixed Israeli city, two years before municipal elections.
- What the mayor actually needed was something true to announce. A chatbot was the only thing anyone had offered him.

</details>

<details><summary>Six months later: three futures</summary>

**Future 1 — Launch for the conference.** The mayor's office went ahead with CivicTalk's "free pilot" over the crawled website, and it went live on 4 February. Within three weeks an Arabic news site posted screenshots: the bot quoted the 2025 parking fee in Arabic and told a resident a leased car was not eligible; another screenshot gave a café owner the old fee. A competing vendor wrote to the tender committee about a free pilot that had become a ₪14,500-a-month commitment. 106 calls rose, because residents called to check what the bot had said. The works committee stopped voluntary overtime in March. On 12 April the legal adviser froze the bot; the comptroller's annual report in the summer had a chapter on it. The mayor's line about "Givat Oren answers you" was quoted back at him in the council.

**Future 2 — Content and reasons first, the assistant behind the agents (the path the expert recommended).** The mayor announced the service promise on 4 February. The knowledge pack became the site's permits section in four languages on 1 March, with owners and dates; new letters carried the reason, the document and the objection route, in Arabic and Russian too; the 33 leased-car refusals were re-examined and 29 approved; callbacks were measured on completion. The assistant went to agents in shadow mode in February and live in March; the register entry was published on 1 March; the FOI answer had been corrected in December. A public "permits FAQ" pilot opened on 15 June after a second eval on residents' real questions, as a blue-track experiment, with the source under every answer. The RFI ran in March and the tender in May, with the eval as the specification. By August: permits-family repeat contacts down 27%, callbacks completed on time 84%, Arabic callers answered in Arabic 76% (two Arabic-speaking agents hired from the re-labelled line), mystery-shopper fee answers right 92%. No agent's job changed without the committee's agreement.

**Future 3 — Half-way.** The forum approved the assistant, and it passed the eval, but the division heads never named content owners: the content was "IT's job". On 1 July the council's new parking fee took effect; nobody updated the pack; the assistant quoted the old fee with a confident citation and its effective date. Agents stopped using it in August; the public pilot was postponed "until after the holidays"; the register entry was never published. The readout had named the dependency; nobody owned it.

**What decided which future happened:** whether the content had owners and an update rule in the decision record, and whether the mayor had something true to announce on 4 February.

</details>

## Watch out

- **A recommend-against with nothing to say instead.** If the chief of staff leaves the room without a sentence for the mayor, he will find a vendor who gives him one.
- **Correcting the mayor's office in the forum.** The same finding, delivered privately first and starting with his own question, is a different meeting.
- **A decision record without content owners.** Future 3 is a record that said "approved" and "content to follow".
- **Handing over to yourself.** You stay in the building; that is not a handover. Name the business owner and the date you stop.
- **Treating the public pilot as the finish line.** The public pilot is a separate decision with its own test and register entry, not the next step that happens by default.
- **Reading the hidden truth first.** The debrief is only worth something if your readout was written without it.

### Steps

1. Write the readout's front page: one recommendation in parts with owners and dates, the recommend-against with evidence, what a "no" means, and the mayor's sentence.
2. Run the integration check; fix or flag at least three contradictions.
3. Rehearse the hardest finding with Eyal (roleplay twice, coach after each), or annotate his transcript with what you would change.
4. Run the forum (group roleplay) or write the 60-minute plan with an answer to each of the seven concerns.
5. Write the decision record and the handover outline.
6. Score yourself with the rubric; then open the hidden truth and the futures; then compare with the expert set.

### Time

180 minutes: 40 for the readout, 25 for the integration check, 30 for the rehearsal, 30 for the forum, 25 for the record and handover, 30 for the debrief.

### Done when

- [ ] The front page has one recommendation, a recommend-against with evidence, and a sentence for the mayor whose every clause has an owner and a date.
- [ ] The integration check found and resolved (or flagged) at least three contradictions.
- [ ] The hardest finding was delivered privately first, starting from the chief of staff's own question, and the second rehearsal was better than the first by the coach's account.
- [ ] The decision record names content owners and an update rule, stop rules, the next decision date and any dissent.
- [ ] The handover names a business owner and a technical owner who can re-run the eval after a rule change without you, and the date you stop.
- [ ] You scored yourself before reading the expert set, and wrote one difference per deliverable.

The full set is `labs/module-11/expert/06-forum-readout.md`. The parts that carry the weight:

**Front page, one sentence:** Residents are not missing a chatbot: they are missing one correct answer in their language and a reason they can use; we have fixed the content, the letters and the callback measure, and an assistant for 106 agents passed our test in four languages, so it goes to agents first and to residents only when it passes again on their real questions.

**Rehearsal with Eyal (31 January):** the first attempt opened with the legal adviser's note; he heard "you broke the rules" and said he would ask CivicTalk directly.
