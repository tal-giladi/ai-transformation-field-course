# 06.5 · The comms pack: what you say, who says it, and what you can promise

The team has heard "40%" since September. Whatever you announce, they will read it against that number. In this lesson you write Harbor & Pine's comms pack for the drafts pilot: the sequence of who hears what first, the announcement, Samir's talking points, the FAQ with an honest answer to "will this replace me?", and the notice about what the tool logs about each person. Every promise in it is checked against the business case and approved, word for word, by the person who can keep it. You also work out what would change if Harbor & Pine had a works committee, because the organizations later in the course do. At the end you have comms pack v1.0, approved by Michal and Yoav, ready for the team meeting on Sunday 24 January.

## The situation

Wednesday 20 January 2027. Yesterday the steering group approved phase 1: ₪60,000 now, ₪60,000 at the May gate. The board meets on 28 January. The drafts go live on 1 February, with WhatsApp moving into the helpdesk the same day ([06.1](lesson-01.md)); training is on 7–11 February ([06.2](lesson-02.md)). Dana has already been asked by agents "are they replacing us with a robot?", and she sent back the first FAQ draft from Michal's office covered in comments (`labs/module-06/faq-first-draft-with-dana-comments.md`).

Three facts make honesty hard. Yoav told the board in September that AI would cut support costs by 40%. The business case's only cash line is not hiring the two seasonal temps next winter, about ₪96,000 ([03.7](../module-03/lesson-07.md)). And Harbor & Pine has no works committee, so nobody is formally entitled to be consulted, which makes it tempting to skip the hard conversation.

IBM's change course gives the frame: a communication and consultation plan per group (message, messenger, channel, timing, feedback route), any group materially affected gets real engagement whatever its power, and "check local legal and union consultation duties". It writes none of the documents. Here you do.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Find out what you are allowed to promise, before you write a word

List every promise people will want, then find who can make it and whether the business case allows it:

| What the team wants to hear | True? | Who can promise it | What the case says |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Nobody will lose their job because of AI" | Not as an open-ended promise | Only Yoav, in writing, with an end date | The case cuts no permanent role; headcount savings count only with a named plan (Ronit) |
| "The temps will come back next winter" | No | Michal, at gate 2 | The cash comes from not hiring them |
| "Nobody will watch how much I use it" | Partly | Michal, as owner of the AUP | Usage is logged per person; reported by team; never in reviews (04.1, 05.1) |
| "I can throw away a bad draft" | Yes | Already in the rule of use | 05.1 |
| "Someone who leaves will be replaced" | Unknown | Michal, case by case | Not decided |

Then get the approvals. The answer to "will this replace me?" is the one sentence that needs the CEO: take Yoav the draft wording in a ten-minute meeting with Michal, say why an open-ended promise would be broken by the first resignation that is not backfilled, and ask him to approve or change it, word for word. Write down who approved what and when.

### Step 2 — Sequence who hears what, from whom

People judge the message by who tells them and whether they heard it first. For Harbor & Pine:

| When | Who hears | From whom | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thu 21 Jan | Samir: the whole pack, the approved wording, his talking points | You and Michal | Face to face, 45 minutes |
| Thu 21 Jan | Olga, Lina, Shani: the pack and their champion role | Samir and you | Face to face |
| Thu 21 Jan | Tamar (logistics) and Avi (IT): their new duties | You | Face to face |
| Sun 24 Jan 08:15 | The support team (14 people) | Samir opens, Michal explains, you show the draft on one real message | Team meeting, 30 minutes, before the queue opens; FAQ handed out in four languages |
| Sun 24 Jan, 11:00 | The rest of the company | Michal | Two-paragraph email |
| Sun 24 – Thu 28 Jan | Each agent | Samir | Five-minute one-to-one: "what worries you?" |
| Sun 31 Jan | The team: what the board decided on 28 January | Michal | Short message |
| 1 April, gate 1 | The team: the results, team-level | Samir and you | Team meeting |

The temps' last working day is Thursday 28 January (their contracts end on 31 January). Samir tells them himself, honestly, that whether temps are hired next winter depends on the pilot, and thanks them. They will hear it anyway; better from him.

### Step 3 — Write the announcement: what, why, what does not change, what happens next

Short, in the team's four languages, signed by Michal, with Samir's name beside hers:

> From 1 February, every written message — email, the web form and, from the same day, WhatsApp inside Deskly — will come with a reply draft written by an AI tool, in the customer's language, with the order and the latest delivery date beside it. **You decide**: send it as it is, change it or throw it away. Every reply is still sent by one of you.
>
> Why: written contact has doubled since May, Russian and Arabic customers wait longest, and the old delay macro sent dates that had already passed. The date problem is being fixed separately, without AI.
>
> What does not change: you send every reply; claims are decided by Rina and Samir; nobody is scored on how many drafts they use.
>
> What happens next: training in your language on 7–11 February, on our own messages. Olga, Lina and Shani are your champions. Questions — the FAQ attached, Samir, Dana, or the anonymous form.

### Step 4 — Write the FAQ, starting with the hardest question

Answer the questions people are actually asking (Dana's list), in plain words, with no promise that has not been approved. The heart of it:

> **Will this replace me?**
> No one in the Customer Service team will be dismissed or have their role reduced because of this pilot while it runs — until the decision on 30 September 2027. Yoav and Michal have approved this answer. What happens after that depends on the results, and you will hear them first, from Michal and Samir, before anything is decided. Any change to anyone's role always follows the legal process, which includes a hearing.
>
> **Is the company cutting the support team by 40%?**
> In September Yoav told the board that the AI program would cut support costs by 40%. Since then we measured the work. The plan the steering group approved on 19 January does not cut the permanent team. It is meant to handle the volume, which has doubled since May, and, if the drafts work, to get through next winter without hiring the two seasonal temps.

Then the rest, each answer one to four sentences: Do I have to use it? (Open the draft, check it, mark it; you may discard any draft.) What if I send a wrong draft? (Four checks; discarding is the job; mistakes are looked at as design problems first — the stop rules in [05.1](../module-05/lesson-01.md).) What happens to the WhatsApp phones? Can I still use my own ChatGPT or the free translation extension? (No, as since November; the drafts and LinguaFlow Business cover it.) If someone leaves, will they be replaced? ("Decided by Michal each time; not automatically 'no'; you will be told.") Will the temps come back? What if I'm not good with computers? When will we hear the results?

### Step 5 — Write the monitoring notice: the one paragraph that gets checked

This is the notice you give before collecting, so it must be complete. The Privacy Protection Law's section 11 notice duty asks you to tell people the purpose and who receives the data (Chambers' 2026 Israel guide). The Privacy Protection Authority's position paper on monitoring employees (17 October 2023) adds that for monitoring activity on employees' computers, a general notice is not enough: say how it is done, what is collected, how often, what it is used for, where it is kept and for how long, in plain language that takes the employees' languages into account (§35). Harbor & Pine's text:

> **What the tool records about you.** The AI panel records, for each person and day, which features were used and how many times (opening the panel, asking for a draft, inserting a draft, translating). It does not put your replies or the customers' messages into any report. The helpdesk also records, for each drafted ticket, whether the draft was used, edited or discarded, and why. **Why:** to see whether the drafts help, where they go wrong, and who might want help. **Who sees it:** Michal, Samir and the steering group see team totals by week only. The pilot lead sees the per-person weekly figures, only to offer help through a champion. **Never:** in performance reviews, rankings or disciplinary steps. **How long:** per-person figures are deleted after the decision on 30 September 2027; the vendor keeps its own logs under its contract (we are checking whether that can be shortened). **Questions or objections:** Dana, or the anonymous form.

Check it against the AUP's section 6 ([04.1](../module-04/lesson-01.md)), the rule of use, and the dashboard design ([06.4](lesson-04.md)): one set of promises, everywhere. Send it to counsel or the DPO through Ronit with one question: "is this notice sufficient for the per-person view described?"

### Step 6 — Write Samir's talking points, including what not to say

Five points he says in his own words, and four things he does not say:

- Say: you send every reply; you may discard any draft; the approved answer on jobs, word for word; WhatsApp moves into Deskly and the phones go to Avi; your worries go to me, Dana, the champions or the form, and you will get an answer within a week.
- Do not say: "it's mandatory"; "don't worry, nobody will ever be fired"; "management will see who uses it"; "the AI is 80% accurate, so it's basically right" (it means one draft in five needs fixing).

### Step 7 — If there were a works committee: what changes

Harbor & Pine has none. Engagements later in the course (the municipality, the factory, the bank) do, and the sequence above would be wrong there.

- **Who represents the employees.** In Israel, collective agreements are made under the Collective Agreements Law, 5717-1957 (חוק הסכמים קיבוציים, התשי"ז-1957), by a representative union; the workplace committee (ועד עובדים) acts for the union at the workplace (Chambers, Employment 2025: Israel). Where a union is the representative organization, the employer bargains with it in good faith.
- **Consultation before change.** In an organized workplace, the employer must consult the employees' representatives before dismissals for downsizing, as a matter of good faith, even when the collective agreement does not say so; many collective agreements also require consultation before structural changes or set up joint committees (Kol Zchut, protections from dismissal in collective agreements). Read the actual agreement: the duty often lives there.
- **Monitoring policy.** The Privacy Protection Authority's 2023 paper notes the courts' view that the workplace policy on monitoring should be anchored in the employment contract and the workplace rules, set in consultation with the employees' representatives (§36).
- **What happens if you skip it.** In August 2026 the workers' committee at AIG Israel, with the Histadrut, declared a labour dispute over the renewal of its collective agreement after management moved unilaterally on AI pilots for call-centre and sales work; the committee demanded that any AI and automation be regulated in the collective agreement (Histadrut, 13 August 2026).

So with a committee, the sequence changes: the committee chair and the union hear **before** the team, with written information (what changes, what data is collected, the job-security position); the monitoring notice and the job-security wording are **negotiated**, not just approved by the CEO; the timeline allows weeks for it; labour counsel is in from the start; and a pilot that starts before the consultation risks an injunction or a dispute. Even without a committee, each employee keeps individual protections: any dismissal needs a proper hearing with the reasons in writing in advance (Kol Zchut, hearing before dismissal). This course is not legal advice: the duties depend on the agreement and the facts, and counsel decides.

## Watch out

- **The promise nobody can keep.** "No one will lose their job because of AI" without an end date and a signature is broken by the first unfilled vacancy. Write the narrower true promise and get it signed.
- **Hiding the number everyone knows.** If the team heard "40%", the FAQ must say what happened to it. "Not relevant" reads as "still true".
- **"We do not monitor you."** The tool logs per person. A notice that denies it is worse than no notice, and it is the notice counsel will read.
- **Email first, manager second.** If Samir hears the announcement with his team, he cannot answer a single question, and they will notice.
- **English only.** The notice has to be understood by the people it is about: four languages, checked by the champions.
- **Talking points that contradict the FAQ.** One set of approved wording, used by everyone, including the trainer in 06.2.

### Steps

1. Fill the promise table (Step 1) and write who must approve which sentence.
2. Write the sequence table, including Samir, the champions, the receiving teams and the temps.
3. Write the announcement (under 200 words).
4. Rewrite the FAQ from the first draft, answering every comment of Dana's and every question on her list.
5. Write the monitoring notice and check it against the AUP, the rule of use and the dashboard design.
6. Write Samir's talking points: five to say, four not to say.
7. Write half a page: how this pack would change at an organization with a works committee and a collective agreement.

### Time

90 minutes: 15 for promises and approvals, 15 for the sequence, 35 for the announcement and FAQ, 15 for the monitoring notice, 10 for the works-committee page.

### Done when

- [ ] Every promise in the pack is in the business case and has a named approver.
- [ ] The FAQ answers "will this replace me?" and "the 40%" without an unapproved promise and without dodging.
- [ ] The monitoring notice says what, why, who sees, never, how long, and where to object, in the team's languages.
- [ ] Samir, the champions and the receiving teams hear before the team; the team before the company.
- [ ] The works-committee page names who is consulted, before what, about what, and that counsel decides.
