# 03.6 · The leadership alignment session

"Use AI" is not a strategy, and a board slide that says "AI first" is not one either. The alignment session is the 60–90 minutes in which the company's leaders turn it into three bets they can each name, measure and defend: which goal, which number moves, which number must not get worse, who owns it, and what has to be true before money is spent. In this lesson you prepare the session, run it with Harbor & Pine's four leaders, and leave with one page that becomes the spine of the roadmap and the business case.

## The situation

Thursday 10 December 2026, 14:00, the board room. The scoring workshop ([03.5](lesson-05.md)) sent three candidates here: the promised-date fix, reply drafts and B2B quote drafts. On 2 December you met Yoav one to one ([03.9](lesson-09.md) teaches that conversation): he heard the evidence, admitted privately that the 40% came from a vendor slide, and chose "fix the dates first, and pilot AI drafting with the team" as his story for the board. He has not said so in front of the others. The board meets in seven weeks.

IBM's leadership course describes a 60–90 minute session that ends on one page of objectives, each with a KPI, a guardrail and a sponsor, with two or three bets kept and the rest parked. It does not show how to get leaders into the room, how to hold it, or what to do when the CEO wants his idea back on the page. That is this lesson.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Do the hard conversations before the room

Nobody should hear bad news about their own idea for the first time in front of colleagues. Before the session:

- **The CEO, one to one** (2 December): the evidence once, his options, his choice. Without this he defends the chatbot in the room and the others follow him.
- **The CFO** (Ronit): which indicators she already reviews, how she wants money staged, and that she will not see an estimated baseline in the paper.
- **The sponsor** (Michal): her objective in her own words, read back in November, still holds; she chairs, you facilitate.
- **The person whose team changes most** (Samir): what he can absorb and when; that headcount is not on the agenda.

### Step 2 — Send a one-page pre-read two days ahead

One page, not a deck. Four blocks:

1. The company's goals, in the leaders' words (Michal: "fewer angry customers, and a support cost that doesn't keep rising"; the board: a strategy and a business case in the standard format).
2. What discovery found, three lines with numbers (WISMO about 38% of contacts, most on WhatsApp; promised dates late by a median 11 days for Vietnam and China since June; agents drafted Russian and Arabic replies in free chatbots with customer data until the 12 November interim rule).
3. The workshop result: three candidates, each with its condition.
4. The parked list, each with its reason.

Ask one thing of the readers: "Come with the one number you would look at to know each goal moved."

### Step 3 — Run the agenda from goals to bets, never the other way

| Minutes | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | The output | "One page leaves this room: up to three bets, each with goal, number, guardrail, owner, gate; and what we are not doing now." |
| 5–20 | Goals | Each leader names the outcomes that matter; you read them back as two to four goals in their words |
| 20–35 | Numbers | For each goal: the KPI that moves, the guardrail that must not get worse, the baseline and its source; "which of these do you already review?" |
| 35–65 | Bets | Bring the candidates in one at a time against the goals: "Which number does this move, by how much, and how do we know?" Owner, sponsor, gate |
| 65–80 | The parked list | Each parked idea with its reason and a dated reopening condition; the favourite idea handled here |
| 80–90 | Read-back and money | The one page read back; where each bet's money lands; next steps |

Only after the goal and the number are on the wall do you name the solution type (rules, generation, a person). In the pre-run transcript the facilitator brings in no candidate until the goals and two of the numbers are agreed, and nobody misses the AI talk.

### Step 4 — Make every number honest: measured, or "to be confirmed"

A baseline is either measured, with its source and date, or written as "to be confirmed", with how and when it will be measured. Never an estimate dressed as a baseline. In the transcript the facilitator offers "about five contacts an hour" for support productivity; Ronit asks what it is based on (one week's tally and a time sheet that logs WhatsApp as email) and the facilitator rightly withdraws it: "baseline to be confirmed, measured in January once WhatsApp is inside the helpdesk". A guessed number in a board paper becomes the target the pilot is judged against.

Pair every efficiency number with a guardrail that would move the wrong way if the gain were fake: contacts per agent-hour with the repeat-contact rate and wrong replies reaching customers; quote turnaround with wrong prices in sent quotes.

### Step 5 — Write each bet as one sentence the owner can repeat

Use one sentence structure for every bet:

> To **[goal]**, improve **[KPI]** from **[baseline or "to be confirmed by …"]** by changing **[the workflow step]**
> through **[the bet]**, without **[guardrail]** getting worse; owner **[name]**, sponsor **[name]**;
> it starts when **[gate, yes/no]**.

Harbor & Pine's first bet:

> To have fewer angry customers, cut "where is my order?" contacts from about 1,650 a month (October,
> all channels) by telling customers the real delivery date before the promised one passes, through a
> daily date check and delay emails, without repeat contacts (about 23%) or CSAT (63%) getting worse;
> owner Michal with Tamar, Yoav to agree the lead-time change with Noa; it goes live when two weeks of
> dry run show no wrong date.

### Step 6 — Park, don't delete; give the favourite a date, not a quarter

When the CEO says "put the chatbot in the plan for Q2", do not argue about the chatbot again. Put it on the parked list with the reason in one line and a reopening condition with a date and evidence: "re-screen in October 2027, when the date fix has run for six months and WhatsApp is in the helpdesk, with a test on our own data". Let the sponsor carry the point if she will (in the transcript Michal says "October with evidence is better than Q2 without it"), and agree the wording he can live with ("re-screen", not "rejected"). A parked list with reasons is also what you show the board when someone asks "why not a chatbot?".

### Step 7 — Close with the story and the money

Two last questions decide whether the page survives the week. **Where does each bet's money land?** (date fix: about ₪35,000, inside Michal and Ronit's band; drafts: a staged case in Ronit's template by 14 January, first stage under ₪50,000; quotes: later, in sales.) **What does the CEO tell the board?** Give him a sentence that is true and his: "We found what drives the complaints in thirty days, fixed it without waiting for AI, and started AI where it helps the team, with a business case Ronit has signed." Read back the page, send it that evening, and ask each owner to reply "confirmed".

## Watch out

- **Starting from the candidates.** If the session opens with "here are our three AI projects", leaders debate projects. Open with their goals and the projects become answers.
- **A goal with no number, or a number with no guardrail.** "Better customer experience" cannot be checked; contacts per hour alone can be gamed.
- **Estimated baselines.** Write "to be confirmed" with the method and date. The CFO will find the guess, in the room or later in front of the board.
- **Surprising the CEO in public.** If the one-to-one did not happen, postpone the bets block rather than defeat his idea in front of his team.
- **A parked list without dates.** "Later" means never to the proposer and "soon" to the CEO. A dated, evidence-based reopening condition means the same thing to both.
- **Missing the line that turns hours into money.** Listen for budgets that already exist for the work (temps, overtime, outsourcing). They are the only route from freed capacity to cash, and they are mentioned once, in passing.
