# 03.9 · The decision-ready case

A board does not read a business case; it reads its first page, and decides whether to read the rest. The decision-ready case puts the ask, the return, the conditions and the cost of waiting on that page, in the order a decision-maker needs them, with every number labelled by how sure you are. In this lesson you write Harbor & Pine's front page for the board of 28 January, check it through the eyes of the CFO and the sponsor, and practise the conversation that has to happen before any of it: telling the CEO, privately, that his chatbot lost.

## The situation

Two moments, one skill. On 2 December 2026, before the alignment session, you met Yoav one to one to tell him what discovery found and that the chatbot would not be the first step; the pre-run transcript of that meeting is in the persona pack. Now it is 14 January 2027: Ronit has reviewed the ROI model ([03.7](lesson-07.md)), the roadmap is at v1.0 ([03.8](lesson-08.md)), the date fix went live this week after a clean dry run, and the board pre-read is due on Thursday 21 January. The minority investor's representative asked in September for "a business case in the company's standard format"; the independent director Orna Sela asked whether the 40% was "a target or a forecast".

IBM's business-case course calls this reader-order assembly: one-page front, one-sentence ask, measured / directional / assumed labels, source tags, cost of inaction as a two-column comparison, the two-reader check. Its executive-communication course adds answer first and the seam between evidence and inference. Here you write the page and hold the conversation.

## What to do

### Step 1 — Tell the sponsor first, one to one, before anything is written for others

Bad news about someone's own idea must never reach them first in a meeting or a paper. Book a private meeting with the person whose favourite lost, and run it in five moves:

1. **Answer first.** "A chatbot is not the fastest way to cut support load here. The biggest driver is something we can fix in weeks, without AI, and I'd like your decision on which path to take to the board."
2. **Evidence once, in their company's numbers.** About 38% of tickets are "where is my order?", closer to 45% since July; the median Vietnam or China order arrives 11 days after the promised date; the vendor's own deck says that without an order-tracking integration the bot answers from the order confirmation, the wrong date. Do not repeat it three times, and do not add a fourth fact.
3. **Ask, privately, what they have committed.** "Can I ask what exactly you told the board, and where the 40% came from? Just between us." In the transcript, this is where Yoav says the number came from the vendor's slide. Also ask what they have promised the vendor, which the transcript forgets.
4. **Options, not a verdict.** Two or three real options with what each would show the board: sign the bot now and promise 40%; fix the dates first and pilot AI drafting with the team; present a strategy only. Let them choose.
5. **A story they can tell, and a next step they own.** "We found what drives the complaints in thirty days and fixed it." Agree what you will and will not put in writing ("Don't put that in a slide." "I won't.").

What you avoid: softening the answer until he hears only the good part, criticising the decision he made in September, and any hint that you will tell the board he was wrong. The board hears evidence and a plan, not a correction of the CEO.

### Step 2 — Write the ask as one sentence

What is decided, by whom, by when, and with what money. If the sentence needs two "and"s, it is two decisions. For Harbor & Pine:

> **The board is asked on 28 January 2027 to approve up to ₪267,000 over three years for a
> reply-drafts pilot in customer service, released in two stages: ₪48,000 now, and ₪72,000 from
> 1 April only if gate 1 is met.**

Everything else on the page supports this sentence. The date fix (about ₪35,000, approved by Michal and Ronit within their band) is reported, not asked for; the chatbot is reported as parked with its re-screen date, not put to a vote.

### Step 3 — Return and timeline, conservative first

Give the scenarios in the order a sceptic wants them: low, expected, high. Low (measured cash only): −₪196,859 NPV; this is a capacity investment, not a saving. Expected (with the temps decision Samir and Michal have signed): ₪113,000 a year in cash, NPV ₪33,717 at the 12% hurdle rate, payback 22.5 months, plus about 109 freed agent-hours a month at peak that are capacity, not cash. High (with assumed upside): NPV ₪195,000, shown but not relied on. Say when the board will see evidence: gate 1 on 1 April, gate 2 on 30 September.

### Step 4 — Label every claim and tag its source

Each number on the front page carries one label and one source tag:

| Claim | Label | Source tag |
|---|---|---|
| 38% of tickets are WISMO; about 1,650 WISMO contacts in October across channels | measured | findings memo (1 Dec), finding 1; baseline 02.4 |
| Vietnam and China orders arrive a median 11 days after the promised date since June | measured | findings memo (1 Dec), finding 1 |
| Translation spend ₪42,000 a year, ₪17,000 after netting | measured | ROI model claim 4 |
| No temps next peak: ₪96,000 | directional (signed plan, depends on gate 2) | ROI model claim 7; roadmap gate 2 |
| About 109 net agent-hours a month at peak | measured on a sample of 400 real emails; capacity | ROI model §1 |
| WISMO contacts will fall about half with the date fix | directional (live since this week) | roadmap N1 |
| Fewer goodwill credits; repeat purchases | assumed; upside only | ROI model claims 5–6 |

Readiness sets what you may claim and the roadmap sets when. A claim with no source tag does not go on the page.

### Step 5 — The cost of waiting, in the same units

"Delay is risky" is a warning. A two-column comparison is a decision aid:

| | Decide on 28 January | Decide at the April board |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow mode | February–March, off-peak | May–June |
| Gate 1 | 1 April | July |
| Temps decision for the Nov 2027–Jan 2028 peak | Made at gate 2 on 30 September | Too late: no summer of evidence; temps hired again (₪96,000) |
| Russian and Arabic replies without an approved drafting tool | Approved tool from February | Three more months at about 13 minutes per careful Russian reply instead of 5, and pressure to return to free chatbots |
| Money committed | ₪48,000 now | ₪0 now |

### Step 6 — Keep the strategy and the numbers in separate sections

The front page has the ask, the return, the conditions and the cost of waiting. The strategic reason ("on time" is on every delivery van; the company's AI work starts where customers feel it) goes in a short separate section, so that a director who doubts the strategy can still check the numbers and one who doubts the numbers can still see the purpose. The appendix holds the ROI model, the roadmap, the fast screen and the parked list.

### Step 7 — Run the two-reader check

Give the page to two people for one minute each. **Finance (Ronit):** can she trace every number to its source and see what is cash and what is capacity? **The sponsor (Michal), reading as a director:** can she say, after one minute, what the board is asked, what it gets and what happens if it says no? Whatever either cannot find in a minute, move up or rewrite. Then send the pre-read on 21 January, and nothing new after that.

## Watch out

- **The case for the wrong reader.** Eleven pages about how the model works, ending with one ROI figure, is written for engineers. The board needs the movement of money and the decision.
- **Two decisions in one ask.** "Approve the pilot and the chatbot evaluation and the hiring freeze" cannot be answered with one yes. Split it.
- **Labels missing or inflated.** One "measured" that turns out to be a vendor figure discredits every other label on the page.
- **Optimistic case first.** Directors anchor on the first number. Lead with the conservative one; it is what makes the expected case believable.
- **Telling the sponsor in the room.** The CEO hearing that his idea lost in front of the board or his team makes him defend it, whatever the evidence.
- **Softening until the message disappears.** "There may be some opportunities to sequence the bot slightly later" is heard as "the bot is fine". Say the answer first, once, plainly.
- **Correcting the CEO in writing.** The board needs evidence and options, not a record that the September number was wrong. Orna's question is answered by the method, not by blame.

- [ ] The ask is one sentence with what, who, by when and how much, and no second decision hidden in it.

- [ ] The cost of waiting is a two-column comparison in the same units.
