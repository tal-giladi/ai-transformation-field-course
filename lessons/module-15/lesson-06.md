# 15.6 · Readout and debrief

The CEO told the board "forty pilots and nothing in production" and asked for a bigger centre. You are about to tell his management committee that it was fewer than forty, that it was not nothing, that the centre he asked for would make things slower, and that the bank's answer to its supervisor needs correcting. In this lesson you write the readout, check that it holds together, rehearse the hardest finding with the CEO alone, run the meeting, and record the decision and the handover. Then you see what was really going on at Crescent, what happened twelve months later on three paths, and how an expert ran the same engagement. At the end you have the readout, a decision record, a handover and your rubric score.

## The situation

Thursday 11 March 2027: the pre-read goes out; at 16:00, thirty minutes with Avner and Dalit. Monday 15 March, 14:00–15:00: the Management Committee (Avner in the chair; Dalit, Moshe Dahan, Irina Kogan, Nir Ashkenazi, Lina Nassar, Gideon Shalev; Hagit Mor and Karim Haddad for the portfolio item). The Katvan AI notices are due the same day. Tuesday 30 March: the board, with the budget revision and the CEO's first word on AI since November.

You stay after Monday: you run what the committee decides. This is module 7 compressed — the [steering pack](../module-07/lesson-01.md), [scale, fix or stop](../module-07/lesson-02.md), [bad news upward](../module-07/lesson-03.md), [the meeting](../module-07/lesson-04.md), [the operating rhythm](../module-07/lesson-06.md) and [handover](../module-07/lesson-09.md). IBM's consulting certificate rewards recommending against when the evidence says so; here you recommend against the board's own design and against the CFO's biggest cut, in the same meeting.

## What to do

### Step 1 — One recommendation, two recommend-againsts, and what the CEO can say

Use the [steering pack](../../templates/steering-pack.md). One recommendation, in parts, each with an owner and a date:

1. AI policy v2: decision rights and validation by risk tier, the tier rule MRM's (Board Risk Committee, 20 April).
2. A hub-and-spoke CoE of 10 instead of a central team of 16; the head of the CoE proposed to the board as AI officer (board, 30 March).
3. The catalogue of every AI use with one owner each by 31 May; the survey answer corrected through regulatory liaison in April.
4. The platform on IT's landing zone by September; kit and templates by June.
5. The portfolio: stop 10, merge 4, clean 3, route 26; 14 in production through the approved route by March 2028.
6. The money: about ₪3.57M saved against the CFO's ₪3.02M, the assistant kept with a usage condition.

The recommend-againsts, on the front page, with their evidence:

- **No central build team of 16.** It adds builders to a queue that validates 1.5 AI systems a quarter (measured); the bank already employs 46 people building AI in its units and IT (measured); in the simulation the central design ends year three with 23 shadow tools (educational model).
- **No cancellation of the assistant.** "16% usage" counts seats activated before training; 66% of onboarded users and the two pilot teams at 74% and 68% use it weekly (measured); two of the best-evidenced pilots run inside it (measured).

And what "no" to your recommendation means: the board's design, "ten by December" again on 30 March, and a first report to the board's risk committee that lists pilots, not what is used.

Write the sentence Avner can say to the board, and test it on Dalit: "We counted. Twenty-six real candidates, fourteen in production by March next year through a route the CRO owns, and every AI use in the bank listed by May."

### Step 2 — Check that it holds together

Run the [integration check](../../templates/integration-check.md) across everything since January. The questions that catch real contradictions at Crescent:

- Do the case (4 February), the plan and the readout give the same date for the first go-live?
- Is every build in the same tier in the operating-model paper, the triage and the plan (watch the call summaries)?
- Is any money counted in two places (IT's landing zone, the merge work, the double-booked lines)?
- Does every "measured" number match across the memo, the case, the triage and the readout?
- Does every kept build have one owner in the catalogue, and is that owner a person who still works at the bank?

### Step 3 — Rehearse the hardest finding with the CEO

The hardest finding is Avner's: **"nothing in production" was not true; what the bank told the supervisor about its inventory is not true; and "ten by December" measures the wrong thing.** Deliver it to him privately on Thursday, with Dalit, before the room ([07.3](../module-07/lesson-03.md)):

- **Start with his words, not your finding.** On 4 February he said success is "customers and employees using AI the bank stands behind". Start there.
- **Show, don't accuse.** The Branch FAQ quoting an outdated fee to a customer in November; the call summaries written into customer records unread. Nobody did anything wicked; the route had no room.
- **Give the survey to the person who owns it.** "Lina proposes to correct it through liaison in April, once the catalogue is complete" is a plan; "you misled the supervisor" is an attack, and untrue.
- **Give him a measure he can own.** Fourteen through a route the CRO owns, the inventory by May, three numbers every quarter.

Roleplay it: paste the [Avner Golan persona](../../labs/module-15/personas/avner-ceo.md) into a new chat, say it is Thursday 11 March and Dalit is with you, and deliver the finding. Run the transcript through the [coach prompt](../../labs/common/coach-prompt.md) with this lesson's "Done when" list. Run it twice; change the delivery, never the finding.

### Step 4 — Run the meeting

Plan the 60 minutes ([07.4](../module-07/lesson-04.md)): 5 for the decision asked, 15 for evidence, 25 for questions, 15 to minute. Prepare one answer for each concern you know is coming: Avner ("so do I get ten by December?"), Moshe ("where exactly is my ₪3.02M, and what if the assistant isn't used?"), Irina ("the tier rule is MRM's document, and nothing changes before the catalogue"), Nir ("who runs the platform at night, and with what money?"), Lina ("the survey correction goes through liaison, not through this committee"), Hagit ("Retail's themes report must not get worse"), Karim ("who decides Cards' low-risk go-lives from April?").

For a group roleplay, paste the files of [Avner](../../labs/module-15/personas/avner-ceo.md), [Dalit](../../labs/module-15/personas/dalit-coo.md), [Irina](../../labs/module-15/personas/irina-cro.md), [Nir](../../labs/module-15/personas/nir-cio.md) and [Lina](../../labs/module-15/personas/lina-compliance.md) into one chat, with the CFO's memo (`company/cfo-budget-review-2027-01-12.md`) for Moshe, and the instruction "play all six in a management committee chaired by Avner; each speaks only from their own prompt; Moshe speaks from his memo". End by asking for the decision in words that can be minuted.

### Step 5 — Record the decision and hand over

The [decision record](../../templates/decision-record.md): what was decided, owners and dates, the **stop rule** (fewer than 5 in production through the approved route by 30 September → the committee re-decides the design), the re-decision dates (the hub's shape at the 2028 budget; the remaining 12 builds at the Q1 2028 review), the assistant's condition, and dissent in the dissenter's own words.

The [handover pack](../../templates/handover-pack.md) and the [operating rhythm](../../templates/operating-rhythm.md): business owner of the operating model (Dalit); the tier rule and validation (Irina and the head of MRM, with a successor named if he leaves); the platform (Nir); the catalogue and the three numbers (the hub's catalogue lead, with `triage.py` re-run on the catalogue export every quarter with identical rules); each kept build's one model owner; the [deliverable map](../../templates/deliverable-map.md). Directive 369 puts responsibility for model risk with the board and senior management and gives internal audit the job of assessing it; your handover makes their job possible, it does not replace it. Write the date you stop owning each part.

### Step 6 — The debrief

Score your deliverables with the rubric in the [module project](../../projects/m15-crescent-bank.md) **before** you open the expert's set, then compare. Open the hidden truth only after your readout is written.

<details><summary>What was really going on at Crescent</summary>

The stated ask was "40 pilots and nothing in production", and the remedy already chosen was a bigger central team. The real problem was **who decides, not who builds**: one committee, full validation for every AI use whatever its risk, and two validators who also carried the Directive 369 phase-in for about 140 traditional models.

- The gate held 3 AI review slots a quarter, 1.5 full validations. Forty builds needed 80 slots: 27 quarters. A central build team of 16 would have added builders upstream of a gate that could not validate what they built.
- Because the gate never opened, "pilot" became a status that never ended. The Branch FAQ answered about 2,000 questions a week in 31 branches for 15 months on a sandbox "not for production use", and quoted an outdated fee in November. Call summaries went into customers' CRM notes through an unreviewed macro, under two owners. The chatbot ran on 2% of web traffic after the committee returned it.
- Work went around the gate. Cards' disputes team drafted about 1,400 letters a month through a public LLM API on a team lead's corporate card, with customer names and card digits in the prompts, because its approved pilot had been "on hold" since April. Digital paid for a writing tool on personal accounts. Branch staff drafted Arabic and Russian letters on their phones.
- The 40 was not 40. Three rows were the same build listed by two units and budgeted twice; four tasks had been built twice on different vendors; ten builds failed their own evidence — including a CV screen that ranked applicants by home town and an AML triage that had auto-closed real alerts. About 26 were worth taking forward.
- The CFO's cut would have cut the wrong thing: the assistant's "16% usage" divided December's users by seats activated before training, and two of the bank's best pilots ran inside it. The register's dependency column was empty.
- The bank had told its supervisor that a central inventory listed every AI application with its owner and that no AI was in production with customers. The list it meant was the register. Its CCO had begun to doubt it and wanted to correct it; she needed an inventory she could stand behind.
- The head of model validation had an offer from a fintech. In the simulation, that is the resignation in Q2 2028; at Crescent it was the people risk nobody had written down.

</details>

<details><summary>Twelve months later: three futures</summary>

**Future 1 — A bigger centre.** The committee kept the board's design: 16 central hires by September, the Model Committee doubled to 6 slots, full validation kept, and the CFO's cut of the assistant approved. By March 2028, 5 builds were in production through the approved route, 7 waited and the backlog was growing; the procedure and knowledge assistants were deleted with the tenant in January; Cards' letters had moved from the public API to staff phones after the host was blocked; two new tools surfaced in an audit. The central team built production versions of pilots that then waited for validation. In Q1 2028 the supervisor asked for the inventory of AI and models in use with validation evidence; the bank sent the procurement vendor list. Internal audit's Q2 2027 report had already listed 23 AI uses "in use without validation". (In the simulation, a central CoE with twice the slots: 5 in production after a year, 18 after three, 19 shadow tools.)

**Future 2 — Let the units run.** Avner, frustrated by June, gave unit heads go-live rights with a light check. By March 2028 nearly all 40 were live in some form, including the 10 weak ones; four tasks ran twice on six vendors; five incidents in the year — the chatbot quoting a cancelled fee to 300 customers, CV screening that HR had to suspend after a complaint from an applicant in a northern town, a valuation check that passed a stale valuation, and two smaller ones. The board's risk committee asked why the AML triage had closed real alerts. (In the simulation, federated: 40 live after a year, 10 weak, 4 duplicates, 5 incidents; 23 incidents after three years.)

**Future 3 — Hub-and-spoke, decided by risk (the path the expert recommended).** Policy v2 approved on 20 April with MRM's tier rule; a hub of 10; spokes in six units and IT's data team as the shared spoke; the catalogue live on 28 May with 58 entries, including the shadow tools, the perma-pilots and AI in vendor products; Lina corrected the survey answer through liaison in April. Ten builds stopped, four merged (Retail's themes report ran side by side for two months and improved), three double counts cleaned; the assistant kept and staged (weekly use 58% of assigned seats in September). The head of MRM left in May; the validation contractor and the templates held the queue. Platform live in September on the landing zone. By March 2028: **17 in production** through the approved route (the plan committed 14), 7 high-tier builds waiting with dates, median decision time 41 days, no known shadow tools, one minor incident (a mortgage FAQ answer citing a withdrawn form, caught by the owner's re-run of the kit). The central programme spent ₪10.2M against a plan of ₪10.0M: the contractor was extended by three months, with the CFO's approval. When the supervisor asked in Q1 2028, the answer came from the catalogue. (In the simulation, the expert's run: 26 in production after a year, 36 after three, 2 incidents, no shadow tools — faster than the bank, as the plan said.)

**What decided which future happened:** whether decision rights followed risk instead of the word "pilot", and whether the catalogue — not the build team — came first.

</details>

## Watch out

- **Recommending against the board without giving the CEO a sentence.** He has to stand in front of the board on 30 March; if you leave him nothing to say, he will say "ten by December" again.
- **Delivering the survey finding as blame.** It was written from the list the bank had; the CCO owns the correction. Say it as her plan.
- **Correcting the CEO in front of his committee.** The same finding, delivered privately first and starting from his own words, is a different meeting.
- **A decision record without a stop rule.** "Hub-and-spoke approved" with no condition for re-deciding becomes the next design nobody chose.
- **Handing over to yourself.** The tier rule is MRM's, the platform IT's, each build its owner's. Write the date you stop.
- **Reading the hidden truth first.** The debrief is only worth something if your readout was written without it.

### Steps

1. Write the readout's front page: one recommendation in parts with owners and dates, the two recommend-againsts with evidence, what a "no" means, and the CEO's sentence for the board.
2. Run the integration check; fix or flag at least three contradictions.
3. Rehearse the hardest finding with Avner (roleplay twice, coach after each), or annotate his transcript with what you would change.
4. Run the meeting (group roleplay) or write the 60-minute plan with an answer to each of the seven concerns.
5. Write the decision record (stop rule, re-decision dates, the assistant's condition, dissent) and the handover outline with the dates you stop.
6. Score yourself with the rubric; then open the hidden truth and the futures; then compare with the expert set.

180 minutes: 40 for the readout, 25 for the integration check, 30 for the rehearsal, 30 for the meeting, 25 for the record and handover, 30 for the debrief.
