# Laws, Guidance and Standards

The laws, regulator guidance and standards this course relies on, Israel first, with the date each entry was last checked. Rules change: before you rely on one in a real engagement, open the source, check its current status, and route legal questions to counsel or the organization's data protection officer (DPO). This page is not legal advice.

## Israel

| Instrument | What it is | Status (checked) |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy Protection Law, 5741-1981 (חוק הגנת הפרטיות, התשמ"א-1981) | Israel's main privacy law: lawful processing, notice, data subject rights, databases, enforcement. | In force (2026-11-02) |
| Amendment 13 to the Privacy Protection Law (תיקון 13) | Broad reform: updated definitions, expanded notice duty, DPO duty for certain organizations, board responsibilities, stronger Privacy Protection Authority (PPA) powers and administrative fines. | In force since August 2025 (2026-11-02) |
| Privacy Protection (Data Security) Regulations, 5777-2017 (תקנות הגנת הפרטיות (אבטחת מידע)) | Security levels for databases, procedures, access control, incident reporting. | In force (2026-11-02) |
| PPA draft guidance: application of the Privacy Protection Law to AI systems | How the law applies across the AI lifecycle: lawful basis and consent, transparency, limits on scraping public data for training, data subject rights in AI outputs, management responsibility. | Draft, published April 2025; no final version found (2026-11-02) |
| PPA guide on privacy-enhancing technologies in AI systems | Techniques to reduce personal data in AI development and use. | Published December 2025 (2026-11-02) |
| PPA draft guidance on consent | When consent is valid ("suspicious consent"). | Draft (2026-11-02) |
| PPA position statement on the DPO duty | Who must appoint a data protection officer under Amendment 13, and what the DPO does. | Final, July 2026 (2026-11-02) |
| PPA Directive 1/2024 on the board of directors | The board's responsibility for privacy where processing is central to the business. | Published September 2024 (2026-11-02) |
| Privacy Protection (Transfer of Data to Databases Abroad) Regulations, 5761-2001 | When personal data may be transferred out of Israel, e.g. to a cloud or AI vendor abroad. | In force (2026-11-02) |
| Privacy Protection Regulations on information transferred to Israel from the EEA, 2023 | Extra duties for personal data that came from the EU/EEA (deletion, accuracy, notice). | In force (2026-11-02) |
| Israel's Policy on Artificial Intelligence Regulation and Ethics (Ministry of Innovation, Science and Technology with the Ministry of Justice) | Sector-by-sector, risk-based regulation; six ethics principles based on the OECD AI Recommendation; prefers soft tools (principles, standards, self-regulation). Not legislation. | Final policy published December 2023 (2026-11-02) |
| Recommendations on AI in the financial sector (inter-regulator team) | Responsible AI risk management for financial institutions; supervision focused on high-risk uses. | Final report December 2025; check the issuing bodies and text before use (2026-11-02) |
| Sector regulators | Bank of Israel (Banking Supervision), Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority, Israel Securities Authority, Ministry of Health, public procurement and Freedom of Information rules, labour law and works committees. | Lessons cite the specific instrument they use |

## European Union

| Instrument | What it is | Status (checked) |
|---|---|---|
| GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Applies to Israeli organizations that offer goods or services to people in the EU or monitor them. Israel holds an EU adequacy decision. | In force (2026-11-02) |
| AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 | Risk-based AI law for providers and deployers placing AI on the EU market or whose output is used in the EU. | In force; obligations apply in stages (2026-11-02) |
| Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026 | Amends the AI Act: stand-alone (Annex III) high-risk obligations move to 2 December 2027, high-risk AI in regulated products (Annex I) to 2 August 2028; new prohibitions and a grace period to 2 December 2026 for Article 50(2) marking of AI-generated content by systems already on the market. Prohibitions, AI literacy, general-purpose AI rules and Article 50 keep their original dates. | Published OJ L 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026 (2026-11-02) |

## Standards and frameworks

| Instrument | What it is |
|---|---|
| NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) and the Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1) | Voluntary US framework: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage; the profile lists generative-AI risks and actions. AI RMF 1.0 is being revised (checked 2026-11-02). |
| ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Certifiable AI management system standard (policy, roles, risk, controls, improvement). |
| OECD AI Principles | The international principles Israel's AI policy builds on. |
| OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2025) | The common security risks of LLM applications: prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, excessive agency and others. |
| US Federal Reserve model risk guidance (SR 11-7, replaced by SR 26-2 in April 2026) | The reference for model risk management in banks; used in module 15 as an international comparison. |
